Chapter Four

Jessica’s Predicament, and Her Compromise

Half an hour earlier

A tea lounge, on the quiet side of a hotel

A private corner, a white cloth, and a folder that was never opened in front of a camera

“No. Absolutely not.”

“You actually want me to spend one night with a man I have never met?”

“What do you take me for?”

Jessica came up out of her chair with both hands on the table.

The coffee in her cup shook. A few drops threw themselves onto the white cloth and sat there like marks somebody would have to explain.

Her face, normally so fair and so perfectly kept, had gone thin-red with fury.

Those eyes that are always cool in front of a lens were close to catching fire.

If the last thread of her reason had not still been holding, she would have poured the coffee over the woman sitting across from her.

Half an hour earlier a woman — the kind of woman who reads as a senior assistant — had asked for ten minutes of her time.

Polite tone. There was a piece of business she would like to discuss.

Jessica had assumed a stage event. Or a brand deal.

What she could not have come up with, on her best day, was this: a transaction this filthy, put in words this plain, with the venue already booked.

The Shilla.

When the name arrived, something in her head went white.

It is not as though she has never seen the dark side of this industry.

She knew the circle was not clean from the trainee years onward, the way a person knows the weather.

Drinking dinners. Banquets. Investors. Producers.

Two phrases every idol learns to translate

“Special consideration” — somebody is deciding you should be grateful.

“Resource exchange” — somebody has already priced what you are being asked to give.

In this business the vocabulary is built so that no sentence ever has to be repeated in plain language. A woman who has worked ten years knows both meanings and never says them aloud.

She had watched plenty of pretty young girls grin brilliantly at a camera and then be walked by their own manager into a private room.

She had also watched some of them bite down and say no, and afterward vanish off every call sheet in the country.

Jessica had always believed that kind of thing lives far away from her.

Because she was big enough, and proud enough, and had never once been a woman who bends when it is convenient.

And now somebody had set it in front of her on a table with a white cloth.

Openly. With nothing over it.

“Do you take me to be merchandise?”

The woman opposite did not move. She did not even frown.

The woman across the table, as Jessica reads her

Suit black, professional, expensively modest

Makeup finished to a standard that takes time in the morning

Voicesoft, level — the kind of mild that has never once had to raise itself

Hands on the table, unhurried, nothing held

There is not one trace of a broker’s oiliness on her.

That is what makes it unbearable. She does not look like a woman doing something shameful in a corner. She looks like a woman processing an entirely normal commercial contract.

Plate set from her card portrait — the tea lounge, Chapter 4

The more correct she was, the sicker Jessica felt.

She set the cup down carefully and spoke lightly, as a person speaks when they have decided in advance that the room will agree with them.

“Ms. Jung, I understand how you feel. There is one thing I’d ask you not to misunderstand.”

“What’s that?”

“The fact that I came to sit down with you today means that you have already run out of choices.”

“There is one road in front of you, and it is cooperation.”

She let a beat pass, still mild.

“Unless you would prefer not to keep working in Korea.”

Jessica’s pupils narrowed. The anger got so large that it turned into a laugh.

“Is that a threat? Who do you think you are?”

The woman did not answer.

She took a card out of her bag and pushed it across the table, gently, so that it stopped in front of Jessica and nobody had to reach for it.

Jessica looked down.

The next second her face stopped working.

The card

Nothing on it that shows off. No titles. No ornament.

One name. One number.

And at the bottom, one word that very few people in this country do not know: samsung.

Her heart went down like a stone let go underwater.

In a single instant the fury that had been standing in her throat was pushed back down by a hand she could not see.

What does Samsung mean in Korea?

It is not a company.

It is not a brand.

It’s a net. It is in every corner of this country the way air is, and you only notice it when somebody else decides you should.

An ordinary person can hate it. Can curse it on a forum. Can complain about it after four shots and feel better in the morning.

But when the name appears in front of you in the flesh — on a card, across a table, in a sentence about your own life — most people go silent without deciding to.

Because you understand you are not looking at a person.

You are looking at a whole system, so large that the room feels smaller under it.

“You’re Samsung?”

The woman said, in a low voice: “To be exact — I do things for the President.”

Jessica’s face got worse.

The Shilla. The President. Samsung.

Put three words like that in one room and a woman does not need an explanation. She needs only arithmetic.

She bit the inside of her cheek.

“Lee Boo-jin?”

The woman did not confirm it.

She did not deny it either.

She smiled.

Jessica’s fingers closed slowly on the edge of the table.

She can slam a table at an ordinary producer. She can cold-shoulder the executives of her own agency. She can get up and walk out on an investor who thinks himself interesting.

Facing a person like Lee Boo-jin, for the first time she felt with total clarity that her pride does not have the hardness she had always assumed for it.

However big her name is, she is an artist.

And what is an artist in a chaebol’s eyes?

The exchange rate she is being quoted

Not her opinion of herself. The other side’s.

Merchandiseshrink-wrapped, seasonal, returnable

Traffica number in a report, useful while it climbs

Promotional toolgood for three photographs and one appearance

Replaceable resourcethere is always another girl with a better contract

She has stood in the middle of a stadium for ten thousand people. She has fans, she has a name, she has commercial value. In front of this kind of woman all of it makes her a chip that cost slightly more than the others.

“And if I refuse?”

The woman had plainly been waiting for exactly that question.

She drew several sheets out of her folder and laid them face up on the table, not pushing them over, just putting them where they could be seen.

“Your label is in conversation about overseas channels.”

“Your contract dispute with your former company has not been closed out.”

“Of the two endorsements you have been pursuing, one is about to go into final review.”

“And you’ve been preparing to restart your activities in Korea. Isn’t that right?”

With every sentence, another degree went out of Jessica’s face.

What was said, and what it means that it was said

Overseas channelsin negotiation. Signed under an NDA nobody in this room broke.

The disputeopen, unresolved, priced in lawyers.

The endorsementstwo; one in final review; both discussed in a room with five people in it.

The Korea restarta decision she has not told her own stylist.

Most of this is not public. Some of it she has never said outside her own team.

There is no point asking how. The useful question, the only one worth carrying out of this room, is what it costs to be on the receiving end of that kind of sight.

The woman went on.

“If you refuse, of course nobody is going to do anything to you. We are not a gang.”

What happens instead, and none of it illegal

01Some partnerships may pause.

02Some reviews may run late.

03Some programme lineups may be adjusted at short notice.

04Some brands may reassess the risk of your name.

05And some media may find a sudden, renewed interest in things from your past.

Not one of those sentences contains a verb she could report to anybody. That is the design.

Jessica stared at her.

“You are despicable.”

“This isn’t despicable. This is reality.”

“So reality is what you use to push a woman into a man’s bed?”

“We’re not pushing you.”

The woman looked at her, and her voice did not change by one degree.

“We are making a trade with you.”

Jessica stared at her for three seconds and then laughed.

“Heh.”

She had been made to laugh by genuine anger.

She had seen shameless.

What she had not seen is shameless stated so comfortably, with so little need to defend itself.

Threaten her career. Use her future as the chip. Put a strange man in a hotel room with her. Then sit in a tea lounge in the daylight and tell her mildly that nobody has coerced anybody.

So the stories about Korean chaebol really are that bad. Nobody started them.

She took a long breath.

She wanted, more than anything, to stand up and walk out. She wanted to tear that card into pieces and throw the pieces at the polite woman’s face.

In the end she did not move.

Because she is no longer the trainee who could charge at the world on one breath of stubbornness.

A negative report filed at a quiet hour.

A contract that simply stops moving.

A programme whose lineup changes on the morning of the taping.

A brand that says, with great regret, that it is reassessing.

To an ordinary person none of that is a scandal. None of it is even news.

For an artist it is a corridor: dim, walked one step at a time, from the middle of a stage to somewhere the cameras no longer point.

And the person sitting across from her is not an ordinary person.

Behind her stands Samsung.

Jessica sat down again, slowly.

The anger had not left her face. Her eyes, though, were much cooler than they had been a minute ago.

“All right. You said this is a trade. Then what am I getting?”

The woman had obviously been waiting for this question too. She smiled a little and let her body go back against the chair.

“Ms. Jung is clearly a clever person.”

“Skip the courtesies.”

She did not mind the tone.

She opened the folder beside her, drew out several pages, and did not hand them over — she only put her head down and read them once, in front of her, the way a person reads a document they do not need to check.

Position · as the other side files it

Where Jessica stands, according to a folder

Read aloud, without pleasure, in the voice of somebody reciting a clause.

ClassificationSM has not said it in public. In this industry you have already been flagged a controversial artist.

Public sentimentSince you left the group it has not recovered. Brands have become careful with you.

BroadcastersNetworks do not want to use you. Advertisers think you carry risk.

EndorsementsYou have almost no new commercial endorsement left — is that not so?

Your labelStrong at launch. Controversy ever since. Channels, cash, stock, promotion, and the confidence of your partners: not one leg of it is stable.

The readOutsiders think it’s an idol turning fashion entrepreneur. Everybody inside knows how hard that road is.

Every line of it is correct, and that is the reason she cannot answer.

Jessica said nothing.

Her face was cold. The diagnosis was accurate. It is exactly where she stands, and whoever wrote this knows it down to the day.

The woman looked up and kept her voice mild.

“Oh — and there is your sister.”

Jessica’s eyes changed all at once.

“What did you say?”

Her sister is the one place on her that no one is allowed to touch.

The woman behaved as though she had not seen the warning in her face.

“Krystal.”

“Her career has taken damage because of you, hasn’t it?”

“After you left the group, SM has not openly gone after her. But the resource allocation changed, the scheduling changed, how often she appears changed.”

“She could have had a smoother road. And where is she now?”

She closed the folder with both hands, lightly, and said it as a list.

“Half-shelved. Marginalized. She still has popularity, she still has a face, she can still hold a stage — and she has been pressed into a position where she can neither rise nor walk away.”

Jessica was silent.

The rebuttal that had been sitting on her tongue a minute ago, ready to be swallowed, was gone.

Because the woman is right.

Every sentence cuts, and every one of them is true.

If this were only about herself, she might not be hurting like this.

She was made by crawling out of the trainee years one inch at a time; she knows what an apprenticeship costs and she has never expected the world to refund her.

Her sister is different.

Krystal loves the stage just as much.

She had been in training since she was small and had taken the same long, gray apprenticeship on the chin.

She could still be shining. There was still so much open in front of her.

And because of her sister’s business, the company took it out on her, the team pushed her to the edge, and the resources were drawn away slowly, like water.

Jessica knows. She has simply never let herself look at it.

Because the moment I look at it, I feel guilty — and it hurts too much to feel guilty.

The woman across from her watched the change in her face, knew she had found the soft place, and so she let her tone go gentle and pushed one tempting contract across.

What is on offer · four items

Item one

Your work in Korea runs smoothly again. At the very least: nobody blocks it.

Item two

Your label gets new channel support. Department stores. Duty-free. Overseas boutiques. Online promotion. All of that is hard for you.

For us it is one sentence said to one person.

Item three

Your sister’s situation improves. A new script. New pictorials. New brand partnerships. SM stops keeping her in a corner.

Item four

One chance to walk back into the mainstream commercial eye. We build an event — the Shilla, or a fashion dinner, either. Your label is brought in as a partner. The media photograph you again. Brands reassess you, this time upward, and the wind of opinion turns back by degrees.

Signed nowhere. Dated not at all. It is not a contract. It is a menu with her name spelled correctly.

Jessica lifted her eyes and looked at her coldly.

“You can move SM?”

She had hesitated, and the hesitation cost her something.

The woman smiled.

“Ms. Jung — that is a question you have already answered.”

In Korea, however large an entertainment company is, it remains an entertainment company.

However famous an idol is, she remains an idol.

The people who decide which way resources flow are capital and the chaebol houses, and they have never once needed to say so at a press conference.

The woman pushed the folder an inch closer.

Jessica listened to all of it, and her face got worse and worse.

Not because the terms are bad.

Because the terms are too good — good to the point that every line hits something she is currently bleeding from. Every item they have laid out is precisely the thing she needs most.

And the better they fit, the sicker she feels.

Everything she has clawed at for years and cannot reach, these people set down on a table lightly, in daylight, as the price of making her bend.

She laughed suddenly.

“So. My dignity is worth this much?”

The woman answered without any discomfort.

“Dignity matters a great deal, Ms. Jung. But if you cannot keep your stage, your brand, and your sister’s future — how long do you think dignity will protect you?”

Jessica’s head came up sharply.

“Shut up.”

The woman did not shut up.

“I know it isn’t pleasant to hear. You know it’s true.”

She did not slam the table again. She did not curse.

She only sat there, looking coldly at the folder on the tablecloth.

In that moment she felt as though somebody had taken hold of her and pressed her against a mirror.

What the mirror showed was not the woman on a stage.

Not the queen her fans name.

Not the proud, icy Jessica on the cover of a fashion monthly.

It showed a woman being pushed into a corner, one inch at a time, by contracts, by opinion, by capital, by the people she loves, and by reality.

I don’t want to bend. The trouble is that there’s nothing behind me to step back onto.

The woman let the silence work, and then went on.

“Of course you can refuse. We do not force anyone.”

“It’s only that after you refuse, the problems you have today will probably stay. Perhaps they will get worse.”

“Your brand’s channels stay jammed. Invitations keep shrinking. Your sister’s resources will not come back inside a year. And standing firm in Korea again —”

She let the sentence wait.

“— will be harder than it already is.”

“We could also approach your sister. If she were told that she could help her older sister, she might agree to our little trade before you do.”

“Don’t you dare. Don’t touch her.”

Jessica closed her eyes.

Her sister: the one place on her that no one is allowed to touch.

A dressing room, some years back

Krystal sitting on a road case with her stage shoes still on, hair half taken down, saying quietly — in the tone of a person trying not to be a burden — “Unnie, I’m fine.”

How could she be fine?

Nobody who really loves a stage can be standing in the wings being pushed further back and feel nothing at all. She is only considerate. She has simply never said the hurt out loud.

Jessica opened her eyes.

Her gaze had gone cold, and there was nothing left in it to negotiate with.

She had compromised.

“I want to know who the man is.”

The woman laughed lightly.

“Put your mind at rest — it isn’t some ancient in a bathhouse. Quite the opposite. He is finer than every male artist your company has on its roster. You are not getting the short end.”

Jessica did not take much from that sentence. She went on.

“And I want to know why it’s me. Why me, and not somebody else?”

The woman looked her over from head to toe, mildly, the way one looks at a thing that has asked a fair question at the wrong counter.

“Because — you must be the lucky one.”

On the last line of this chapter

That answer is not a joke by a secretary. In this book, luck is a currency the powerful spend on other people, and the exchange rate is set in a room exactly like this one. Nobody in this chapter is wrong; that is why nobody in this chapter wins.
