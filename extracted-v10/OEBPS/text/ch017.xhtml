Chapter Seventeen

Park Ji-yeon

Late that night · darker than usual

The apartment building · elevator hall and entrance

Two women coming home from the cold, and one recognition that took half a second

When Bae Do-yoon got off work, the sky had gone completely black.

Today had indeed been busy past reasonable measure.

After returning from JYP he had organized the materials, reported at the Department Chief’s office, and then confirmed with Kim Mi-ju the list to be pulled tomorrow. By the time everything was handled, the hour stood well past his usual one.

Back at the apartment building, he had barely reached the elevator hall when a familiar voice came from beside him.

“Ah, it’s really cold.” The voice was soft, carrying a little complaint in it.

Bae Do-yoon looked up.

At the apartment entrance, two women were coming in from outside.

One of them was small in frame, wearing a hat and a mask, an insulated bag hanging from her hand.

Even covered as tightly as she was, Bae Do-yoon recognized her at a glance.

Jeon Bo-ram.

And the woman standing beside her was plainly taller, in a loose coat, the cap brim pressed very low, a mask hiding most of her face.

But those eyes were highly identifiable.

Young. Pretty. Quick and bright.

However well she was disguised, Bae Do-yoon recognized her too.

Another member of T-ara.

Park Ji-yeon.

Park Ji-yeon

T-ara’s maknae-line visual; the neighbor’s neighbor, as of one cold night

Tall where Boram is small, bright where Boram is careful; the unnie-call that proves nothing except arithmetic

Recognized behind cap and mask by her eyes alone

Curious by constitution and unapologetic about it. Counts small kindnesses other people perform without noticing — doors waited at, seats not taken, questions not asked — and files them under a verdict she announces at full volume in whispers.

The curious one

Coming-home inventory: the taller woman at the entrance

Coat loose, oversized, swallowing a tall figure on purpose

Cap dark, brim pressed very low over the brow

Mask high over the nose; most of the face gone

Eyes young, pretty, quick and bright — the one part doing all the introducing

The whole assembly reads as one sentence: a person who very much wishes not to be recognized, and would be recognized anyway by anyone who has seen those eyes once.

Identity-locked to her card portrait (id-20): same eyes, same hairline under the cap, same half-turned way of holding a doorway. Plate set from the elevator-hall scene, Chapter 17.

Bae Do-yoon’s step checked, faintly.

Jeon Bo-ram saw him at the same moment.

Her eyes lit all at once, as if she had not imagined meeting him at this hour; a happy smile came onto her face quickly.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi — what a coincidence.”

Bae Do-yoon smiled too.

“What a coincidence.”

Jeon Bo-ram came a little closer and looked up at him.

“Are you only getting off work now?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Mm. Work ran a little long.”

“This late, even.”

Jeon Bo-ram looked at him once, her tone carrying a natural concern.

“Have you eaten, then?”

Bae Do-yoon was about to answer when Park Ji-yeon beside her had already looked over, curious.

Her line of sight traveled once between Bae Do-yoon and Jeon Bo-ram, and plainly brightened by a few degrees.

“Unnie — who is this?”

Her voice carried the particular liveliness of a young girl.

Hearing that unnie, Bae Do-yoon could not help a light laugh.

Jeon Bo-ram turned to look at him at once.

She guessed, almost instantly, what he was laughing at.

It was not the first time she had been teased about this, after all.

Going by looks alone: she was small in frame, her face read very young, and even under hat and mask it would be easy to take her for somebody’s junior. Park Ji-yeon, on the contrary, was tall and vivid in bearing; standing beside Boram, she looked for all the world like the younger sister who had grown up more mature than the elder.

And yet Jeon Bo-ram was the unnie.

The unnie in that address was not wrong. Only the contrast was too strong.

Jeon Bo-ram puffed her cheeks.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

Bae Do-yoon worked to gather his smile back in.

Jeon Bo-ram glared at him.

“No laughing.”

Bae Do-yoon raised both hands, tone fully cooperative.

“All right. Not laughing.”

“You are plainly laughing.”

“Not now.”

“You were a second ago.”

“That was a second ago.”

Jeon Bo-ram wanted to glare at him harder.

Park Ji-yeon stood beside them looking from Jeon Bo-ram to Bae Do-yoon and back, her eyes brighter and brighter.

It was the first time she had ever heard Jeon Bo-ram use that tone with a man.

This was not the small talk of ordinary neighbors.

She could not help asking, in a small voice: “Unnie — are you two close?”

Only then did Jeon Bo-ram realize they had not been introduced.

She gave a light cough and said: “Ji-yeon — this is Bae Do-yoon. My neighbor.”

Then, to Bae Do-yoon: “This is Park Ji-yeon. You know of her, I take it?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded politely.

“Hello, Park Ji-yeon-ssi.”

Park Ji-yeon took off her mask, revealing a pretty, young face.

When she smiled, her eyes bent a little, carrying a few parts mischief.

“Hello. I’m Park Ji-yeon of T-ara.”

“Bae Do-yoon.”

Bae Do-yoon introduced himself simply as well.

Park Ji-yeon looked him over.

Tall. Good-looking. A clean, decisive bearing.

When she had first glimpsed him from a distance she had assumed some actor or model. And then Jeon Bo-ram said he was merely a neighbor.

A neighbor?

Park Ji-yeon blinked.

Since when did Boram-unnie acquire a neighbor this handsome? And judging by how the two of them just went at it — plainly more than a nodding acquaintance.

Her curiosity hooked in all at once.

Jeon Bo-ram did not notice the change in Park Ji-yeon’s eyes.

She suddenly lifted her hand and swung the insulated bag a little.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi — you’re back at the right time. Shall we eat fried chicken together?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at the bag in her hand.

Jeon Bo-ram added, eyes crinkling: “There’s pork cutlet too.”

Park Ji-yeon’s eyes went wide at once.

She looked first at Jeon Bo-ram, then at Bae Do-yoon, her gaze carrying a little of the excitement of somebody watching a good scene.

“Oh?”

Jeon Bo-ram turned to her.

“What is that face for?”

Park Ji-yeon shook her head at once, smiling in perfect innocence.

“Nothing. I only think unnie did buy rather a lot.”

Jeon Bo-ram: …

Bae Do-yoon looked at the insulated bag, and was in fact somewhat surprised.

He truly had not eaten dinner. After a full day’s work, at this hour, to claim he was not hungry would be a lie.

But the two women in front of him were, after all, entertainers — and T-ara members at that.

Bae Do-yoon thought it over, and still said: “Better not, I think.”

Jeon Bo-ram blinked, taken aback.

“Why?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “You are entertainers. For a man to go into your home for a meal is not quite appropriate. If someone sees it, the effect on you two would not be good.”

Hearing that sentence, Park Ji-yeon’s gaze moved, faintly.

She did not take it as an insult; if anything she was surprised.

Because Bae Do-yoon’s sentence was not shielding himself from suspicion. It was considering the two of them.

Jeon Bo-ram understood his meaning too; the smile on her face gathered in a little.

“But the security here is good. And it is only a meal.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “Precisely because it is only a meal, there is all the less reason to make trouble for you.”

Hearing that, Park Ji-yeon could not help laughing.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi — you really are very careful, aren’t you.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

Park Ji-yeon said, in a light tone: “But I think it’s fine. Unnie invited you, and I am here too — it isn’t two people alone.”

Saying it, she looked at Jeon Bo-ram and added, crinkling her eyes: “Right, unnie?”

Jeon Bo-ram nodded at once.

“Right. Ji-yeon is here.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at the two of them.

Jeon Bo-ram’s face was full of expectation. Park Ji-yeon’s was full of curiosity, as if she could hardly wait for him to agree.

Bae Do-yoon was silent a moment, and in the end smiled, a little helplessly.

“If you two don’t mind — then I’ll intrude.”

The smile came back onto Jeon Bo-ram’s face at once.

“No intrusion at all.”

Park Ji-yeon said, smiling too: “Welcome, Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

· · ·

The three of them entered the elevator together.

The car rose slowly.

Jeon Bo-ram stood in the middle, hugging the insulated bag, in what seemed a very good mood.

Park Ji-yeon stood beside her, her peripheral light falling onto Bae Do-yoon now and then.

She was, truly, a little curious.

This man did not look like an ordinary neighbor. His manner was mild, but there was a very restrained quality about him.

Park Ji-yeon had met many people.

But Bae Do-yoon was not much like any of them.

He showed no excitement at meeting entertainers, and made no particular effort to get close; from beginning to end he was simply natural.

The more so, the more one could not help wanting to look at him a few times more.

When the elevator reached the floor, Jeon Bo-ram led the two of them to her own door.

She keyed in the code and opened it.

“Come in.”

Bae Do-yoon did not enter at once.

He stood at the doorway and waited until Jeon Bo-ram and Park Ji-yeon had gone in first; only then did he change into slippers and step inside.

That small motion did not escape Park Ji-yeon.

The curiosity in her eyes deepened a degree.

Jeon Bo-ram set the insulated bag on the low table.

“You two sit first. I’ll get plates.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “No trouble needed — we can eat straight from the boxes.”

Jeon Bo-ram looked back at him.

“How would that do? A guest comes, plates come.”

Park Ji-yeon raised her hand at once.

“Unnie, I’ll help.”

The two women went into the kitchen.

Bae Do-yoon stayed in the living room: not looking around freely, not walking about, simply waiting quietly beside the sofa.

Kitchen · two voices lowered over plates and chopsticks

Are you two close? Not that close. Not that close, and you invite him home for fried chicken? I bought too much. Unnie, that reason isn’t very convincing. I really did buy too much. Fine — let’s call it bought too much.

But Bae Do-yoon-ssi really doesn’t look like a bad person. He isn’t, originally. Unnie is that sure? He helped me. …Helped you? Mm. Then no wonder. No wonder what? No wonder unnie looks so happy when she sees him.

The two of them came back to the living room carrying plates and chopsticks.

Bae Do-yoon was still standing beside the sofa, not seated.

Seeing it, Jeon Bo-ram found it a little strange.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi — why aren’t you sitting?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “The host hasn’t returned. It wouldn’t do to sit first.”

Jeon Bo-ram blinked, and then could not help laughing.

“You really are too polite.”

Park Ji-yeon said, smiling beside them: “Bae Do-yoon-ssi has lovely manners.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “Only basic courtesy.”

The three of them sat around the low table.

Jeon Bo-ram took the fried chicken and the pork cutlet out, one item at a time.

Bae Do-yoon watched the low table fill, and was silent for a moment.

“Jeon Bo-ram-ssi.”

“Mm?”

“This is ‘bought too much’?”

Jeon Bo-ram’s hand paused.

Park Ji-yeon covered her mouth at once, laughing.

Jeon Bo-ram said, with great composure: “We were always big eaters to begin with.”

Park Ji-yeon dismantled it at once.

“Unnie — that’s you. Don’t drag me in; I don’t eat much.”

Jeon Bo-ram: …

Silently, she pushed one box of fried chicken toward Bae Do-yoon.

“Which is why we need Bae Do-yoon-ssi’s help.”

Bae Do-yoon gave a low laugh.

“So I was invited to clear inventory.”

Jeon Bo-ram corrected him, in earnest: “To share good food.”

Park Ji-yeon nodded, smiling.

“Yes. Sharing good food.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

“Park Ji-yeon-ssi seems strongly in favor of that version.”

Park Ji-yeon blinked.

“Because I also want to eat more, but I don’t want unnie telling me off.”

Jeon Bo-ram turned to her at once.

“Ya, Ji-yeon.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed, thoroughly happy.

The atmosphere, with her liveliness in it, went light all at once.

· · ·

After they had eaten a while, Jeon Bo-ram suddenly asked: “Bae Do-yoon-ssi — was work very busy today?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Mm. I went out, and organized some materials.”

Park Ji-yeon asked, offhand: “Bae Do-yoon-ssi — what work do you do?”

Bae Do-yoon did not go into detail; he answered simply: “A civil servant.”

“A civil servant?”

“Mm.”

Park Ji-yeon looked him up and down once, her tone curious.

“Really?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

“I don’t look like one?”

Park Ji-yeon nodded, very honestly.

“Not really.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Then what do I look like?”

Park Ji-yeon thought about it.

“Like an actor.”

Jeon Bo-ram nodded beside her.

“I thought the same when I first met him.”

Bae Do-yoon was a little helpless.

“Then perhaps you two have met too few civil servants.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed: “Or perhaps Bae Do-yoon-ssi simply looks too little like one.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “What should a civil servant look like?”

Park Ji-yeon considered it seriously, then shook her head.

“I don’t know. But anyway, not like Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

Jeon Bo-ram laughed.

Bae Do-yoon gave a low laugh too.

“Then I’ll try harder to look the part going forward.”

Park Ji-yeon was made to laugh by him.

“That is something one can try at?”

“Probably not.”

“So Bae Do-yoon-ssi was just brushing me off earlier?”

“It was a polite reply.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed harder.

She found this man genuinely interesting. He looked calm and restrained, yet his speech was not dull; he did not push forward particularly, but he caught whatever was thrown to him.

That feeling of being together was comfortable.

Park Ji-yeon asked again: “Are civil servants all busy?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “It depends on the department, and on the season.”

Jeon Bo-ram asked: “And you have been busy lately?”

“Mm.” Bae Do-yoon nodded. “Things have piled up recently.”

Park Ji-yeon rested her chin on her hand, curiosity in her eyes.

“You said earlier you went out — was that fieldwork?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

Park Ji-yeon blinked at once.

“Can’t you say?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “It’s not that I can’t say. It’s just not interesting.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed: “The more you say that, the more curious a person gets.”

Jeon Bo-ram tapped her lightly.

“Ji-yeon. Don’t press people about their work.”

Park Ji-yeon sat up properly at once.

“Understood.”

Bae Do-yoon did not mind, in fact.

He only said: “Mostly handling materials, confirming procedure, and talking with the people concerned.”

The answer was very general. Jeon Bo-ram thought nothing of it; Park Ji-yeon, however, narrowed her eyes a little.

She felt that Bae Do-yoon seemed not to be lying — but not to be telling everything either.

It sounded ordinary. Yet against his bearing, it somehow did not stop at sitting in an office.

What Jeon Bo-ram did not elaborate on, in a kitchen, to a junior

He helped me. Three words, and she would not spend a fourth on them. Not the supermarket line, not the bulb at nine at night, not the cap pulled low or the crabs owed — Ji-yeon was given the fact and denied the story, and was wise enough to take it.

Some debts are not embarrassing to owe. They are simply too warm to display under a kitchen lamp, in front of a junior whose eyes have already gone bright as coins.

He isn’t, originally.

On fried-chicken diplomacy

Two boxes of chicken, one cutlet, and a third place setting negotiated at an elevator hall: this is how neighborhoods actually sign treaties. The terms were simple — the guest considers the hosts’ names before his own hunger, the hosts consider the guest’s hunger before their own secrets, and the junior attaches herself as an observer with full eating rights. No signatures, no seals. Only radish cubes, passed without being asked for.
