Chapter Twenty-Three

Really Leaving Now

The same night · the corner, still

Behind the vending machines · a manager’s voice, suddenly nearer

Where one man steps half a pace forward and becomes, without ceremony, a wall

Just then.

Outside, the manager’s voice came suddenly nearer.

Irene stopped moving at once; Bae Do-yoon also recovered his composure. He turned sideways, placed himself a little in front of her, and let his gaze go out through the gap.

Irene’s heart had not yet fully settled from the string of embarrassments just now.

But when she saw Bae Do-yoon put his attention back on the outside, standing before her to block the line of sight, her mind grew inexplicably steady again.

This person was really strange.

At the moment he ought to make her heart move, he would say, perfectly straight-faced, that there was a spiderweb.

At the moment he ought to make her angry, he would be so reliable that she could not truly be angry.

The corner · reconnaissance, two voices wide

Is he coming over? — No. — Then what is he doing? — On the phone. — Will he find us? — If you stop pinching me, probably not.

That was an emergency. — Pinching me was an emergency? — It was emergency response. — Article three of the artist’s survival logic?

Correct, said the keeper of the articles, holding up three fingers, solemn and small-voiced, in a corner with no room for ceremony.

Outside: a manager, on his phone, unaware that his proximity was currently being legislated against.

Irene bit her lip and asked, in a low voice: “Is he coming over?”

Bae Do-yoon watched for a while.

“No.”

“Then what is he doing?”

“On the phone.”

“Will he find us?”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at her. “If you stop pinching me, probably not.”

Irene said at once, in a voice only the two of them could hear: “That was an emergency.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Pinching me was an emergency?”

Irene, with perfect legitimacy: “It was emergency response.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “Article three of the artist’s survival logic?”

Irene had meant to keep her expression stern; hearing this, she could not.

“Correct.”

She held up three fingers and summarized, solemnly and in a small voice:

“First: do not get found by the manager.”

“Second: keep quiet when necessary.”

“Third…”

She glanced at Bae Do-yoon, and said slowly:

“If someone deliberately plays innocent, appropriate punishment may be carried out.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

“Appropriate?”

Irene drew her fingers back and touched the tip of her nose, guiltily.

“A little on the heavy side.”

The artist’s survival logic · three articles, recited at whisper volume

01Do not get found by the manager. Status: holding, behind one row of vending machines and one manager’s phone call

02Keep quiet when necessary. Status: breached repeatedly by one prosecutor issuing risk advisories; breaches tolerated

03If someone deliberately plays innocent, appropriate punishment may be carried out. Status: enforced twice; appropriateness under review

The keeper of the articles conceded, fingertip to nose, that the enforcement had been a little on the heavy side. The victim’s thigh filed no objection; the victim’s face had.

Bae Do-yoon did not speak. Irene stole another look at his thigh; two seconds later she asked, in a small voice:

“Did it really hurt?”

“What do you think?”

Irene, mouth hard: “Serves you right for laughing at me.”

Having said it she paused, and then, as if against her will, added one more line:

“Then… next time I’ll be lighter.”

A little laughter surfaced in Bae Do-yoon’s eyes. “There is a next time?”

Irene realized at once what she had said and amended it immediately:

“I mean if! If next time you are being this annoying again!”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Mm. If.”

Irene glared at him.

“Don’t use that tone.”

“Which tone?”

“That tone as if you know everything.”

Bae Do-yoon did not speak; he only watched her.

Irene, looked at that way, grew uncomfortable again; she turned her face aside and muttered in a small voice:

“For a slow-burn type, you read people far too well.”

Bae Do-yoon heard it, and asked in a low voice: “Is that also a flaw?”

Irene said: “Depends on the situation.”

“And what is the situation now?”

Irene’s eyes turned; she said, in a small voice: “Now is a dangerous situation. Evaluation suspended.”

Bae Do-yoon was made to laugh by this solemn nonsense of hers.

“Good. We evaluate once we are safe.”

The manager stood at the alley mouth for perhaps two or three minutes.

Those few minutes were exceptionally long for Irene.

One moment she was tense about whether the manager would walk over; the next she could not help remembering how she herself had closed her eyes just now.

The more she thought, the more embarrassed she grew; the more embarrassed she grew, the more she wanted to steal looks at Bae Do-yoon.

She was even a little grateful for the mask — at least the blushing would not show too plainly.

But Bae Do-yoon, as if deliberately refusing to let her off, asked in a low voice: “Are you very hot?”

Irene went alert at once. “No!”

“But your ears are red.”

Irene put her hands over her ears.

“They are frozen red.”

Bae Do-yoon looked once at the motion of her covering her ears.

“But it is spring now.”

Irene was silent for one second; then she said, perfectly seriously: “My constitution is special.”

Bae Do-yoon: …

Seeing him throttled, Irene finally felt a small taste of victory.

Her eyes curved; she said, in a small voice: “Is that not allowed?”

Bae Do-yoon, looking at this rare bit of childishness she had let show, did not expose her further.

“Allowed.”

Only then was Irene satisfied.

She was rarely like this, ordinarily.

Before lenses she had to be beautiful, proper, steady.

Before the members she was the unnie, the leader, the one who had to look after others.

At the company she had to guard every sentence, every expression, every moment of being seen by anyone.

But before Bae Do-yoon she seemed always to loosen, a little, without noticing.

She would put out her tongue; she would keep a hard mouth; she would go mortified-angry over a little embarrassment; she would threaten him, childishly, not to laugh.

These faces she rarely showed to anyone.

Not that she withheld them on purpose — it was only that other people could not necessarily draw them out of her.

The selves she keeps, and the one door they walk out of

Before lenses: beautiful, proper, steady. Before the members: unnie, leader, the one who carries. At the company: every sentence weighed, every expression filed, every being-seen moment guarded.

Before him, without noticing: tongue out, mouth hard, mortified-angry at a little embarrassment, childish threats against laughing. Faces rarely shown to anyone — not withheld on purpose; only that no one else can quite draw them out.

They waited a while.

At last, outside, the car door closed again.

The manager’s business van started and drove slowly out of the alley mouth.

Irene waited until the van’s taillights had utterly disappeared before she let out a long breath.

She leaned back against the wall, her whole person like someone finally recovering from a sudden accident; even her shoulders loosened.

“Scared me to death.”

Bae Do-yoon watched her, a little laughter still at the bottom of his eyes.

“Now we can come out.”

Irene did not move at once.

She first straightened her cap brim, then touched her hair tips, as if checking whether that scramble of moments had left any trace behind.

Field disguise, re-inspected at the corner’s exit

Cap black, brim re-pressed low after the ambush; inspected for disorder

Mask white, high over the nose; blamed for nothing, credited with hiding the blush

Hair tips patted back into place; one strand formerly carrying spider silk, now carrying only a fingerprint of it

Ears the breach in the whole assembly: red in spring, unmasked by any cloth

Purpose of the inspection, on record: to see whether there is a second spiderweb. The inspector’s eyes said otherwise.

Bae Do-yoon looked down at her.

“What are you looking for?”

Irene gave him a wary look.

“Checking whether there is a spiderweb.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted a brow.

“Didn’t I just take it off for you?”

“Who knows whether there is a second one.”

Bae Do-yoon, amused by this solemn air of hers, gave one light laugh.

Irene glared at him at once.

“You’re laughing again.”

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

“I am only breathing normally.”

Irene, throttled again, pressed her lips and did not speak.

But in those eyes of hers the mortification of just now plainly lingered; even her glare carried an indefinable cuteness — like someone trying very hard to look fierce and nowhere near succeeding.

Bae Do-yoon studied her for two seconds, and suddenly said in a low voice: “At this moment you look nothing like a popular artist.”

Irene paused.

“Then what am I supposed to look like?”

Bae Do-yoon considered; his tone was very light, and yet carried a deliberate wickedness.

“Like the child who, just now, nearly handed herself over with her eyes closed.”

Irene: …

She went red all at once, whole temperature rising.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi!”

Seeing her truly flustered, Bae Do-yoon smiled more plainly instead, and even shifted back half a step, as if leaving her a little safe distance.

Irene looked at him — at that air of knowing perfectly well she was shy and saying it out loud anyway — and was furiously angry.

But she could not truly make a scene here; she could only press her voice low and say, fiercely:

“Say one more word and I’ll really be angry.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded at once.

“Good. Not saying.”

Irene hmph’d, and only then finally came out from behind the wall corner.

Minutes later · under the streetlight

The alley, quiet again · the scramble folded away as if it had never been

Where a sleeve is seized for safety, and safety is ruled cute

The two of them stood back under the streetlight. The alley had gone quiet; that scramble of moments just now seemed never to have happened.

Only Irene herself knew that her heartbeat had not yet fully returned to normal.

She walked on, stealing glances up at Bae Do-yoon as she went.

Bae Do-yoon noticed her line of sight and turned his head to look over.

“Why do you keep looking at me?”

Irene drew her gaze back at once.

“Who is looking at you.”

Bae Do-yoon gave a low laugh.

“Then I must have seen wrong.”

Irene had meant to retort, but that deliberately accommodating tone of his left her somehow helpless; she could only turn her face away and mutter in a small voice:

“You are truly annoying, you know.”

Bae Do-yoon, however, seemed to consider it very seriously.

“That one just now has been entered in the record.”

Irene stopped walking at once.

“Entered what in the record?”

Bae Do-yoon glanced at her and said, leisurely: “Mental damages.”

Irene: …

He had truly laughed her into exasperation.

“You really do hold grudges.”

Bae Do-yoon did not deny it in the least.

“This is called preserving evidence.”

Irene stared at him for two seconds; then, suddenly, she reached out and lightly seized his sleeve cuff.

Bae Do-yoon lowered his eyes to her hand.

Irene herself stilled a moment, as if not expecting herself to make such a motion.

But she quickly pressed that small discomfort back down, and explained, mouth hard:

“I’m afraid you’ll talk loosely again.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, his tone carrying a knowing ask-he-already-knows-the-answer quality.

“So you need to hold me?”

Irene loosened her grip a little at once — and yet did not wholly let go; she only pinched a small point of his cuff’s cloth.

“…This way is safer.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at that hand of hers; two seconds later he said, softly:

“This ‘safety’ of yours is rather cute.”

“You shut up!”

Bae Do-yoon, very obediently, said no more.

But the quieter he grew, the more Irene felt some indefinable thing slowly rising in the air between them.

That almost-touching distance in the wall corner still remained in her body’s memory. Now, outside, the space was plainly wider — and yet she still felt a little short of breath.

She raised her head to look at Bae Do-yoon.

He was dressed simply today, and stood easily; and yet it was precisely that ease that made him look more like a real young man — one who laughed, who made noise, who held grudges — and less like someone forever immaculate to the last thread.

Irene looked and looked, and suddenly asked, in a small voice: “Just now… were you doing it on purpose?”

Bae Do-yoon lifted a brow.

“On purpose what?”

“The spiderweb…”

Bae Do-yoon had not expected her to ask this suddenly; then he looked down at her, and a faint smile surfaced in his eyes.

“You closed your eyes just now.”

Irene paused.

Bae Do-yoon went on: “I thought you were waiting for me to take the spiderweb off — not waiting for anything else.”

Irene: …

She understood in an instant what he was saying, and her face grew strange at once.

“Bae Do-yoon!”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, tone very innocent.

“Mm?”

“You knew perfectly well!”

“Knew what?”

“You—”

Halfway, the words ran out on her; she could not go on.

She had indeed misunderstood just now — and misunderstood thoroughly, at that.

Thinking of herself with eyes closed, seriously waiting for something to happen, and in the end only having one strand of silk brushed from her hair — she wanted to find a seam in the ground and slip into it.

Bae Do-yoon watched her face redden further and further, and at last could not hold it in: one low laugh.

That one laugh set Irene’s mortification alight outright.

Almost by conditioned reflex she reached out and pinched his thigh again, hard.

Bae Do-yoon, caught off guard, stiffened all over; his brow knotted sharply, he forced one breath back down, and his expression went momentarily ferocious.

“Joo-hyun.” His voice was pressed low, altered a little. “Again? And isn’t your hand a bit too brutal?”

Irene watched him inhale through the pain — first a flash of guilt, and then, having at last won a round back, she could not help one small hmph.

“Serves you right for laughing at me.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at his leg, then up at her.

“I think I need an explanation.”

“What explanation?”

“Why is it always me who gets injured?”

Irene, with perfect legitimacy:

“Because you deserve it. Serves you right!”

Bae Do-yoon was left speechless by this; he was silent two seconds, and in the end only gave one helpless laugh.

“Fine. Whatever you say is right.”

Irene, looking at that conceding air of his, felt the mortification in her chest scatter a little at last.

She was still blushing, plainly, and yet she put on a very composed look and lifted her chin.

“Next time, don’t do this.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

“Do what?”

“Deliberately make me misunderstand.”

Bae Do-yoon paused; his eyes rested on her face, and he spoke slowly:

“Then — were you already prepared just now?”

Irene stiffened, whole body.

“Don’t talk nonsense.”

“Am I talking nonsense? You were, after all…”

Irene glared at him — but those eyes had no lethality left in them; if anything they looked angry and shy at once.

Bae Do-yoon did not go on teasing her; he only stood one step away, looking at her quietly.

Irene, looked at that way, grew even more uncomfortable; after a good while she said, in a small voice:

“Are you leaving now?”

Bae Do-yoon paused, faintly.

“Mm.”

Irene made an oh sound, lowered her eyes, and her fingers pinched at her cuff without her meaning them to.

She had been fierce only moments ago; quieted like this, she became instead inexplicably well-behaved.

Bae Do-yoon regarded her and suddenly asked: “What — sad to see me go?”

Irene lifted her head at once.

“Who is sad to see you go.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded, as if following her words onward.

“Then I’m leaving.”

Irene had meant to keep her mouth hard, but the words stopped at her lips all the same.

She looked at him; after a long moment she suddenly reached out and lightly tugged his sleeve.

The motion was very light — like a probe.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

Irene, two seconds later, said in a small voice: “Just now… you were really a bit too much.”

Bae Do-yoon only looked at her, as if waiting for her to finish the words left unfinished.

Irene, looked at that way, felt her chest jump; they were nearly at the parting already, and yet she did not want this sentence to end just so.

So she added one more:

“But… when you stood in front of me just now, you were quite reliable.”

The laughter in his eyes slowly thinned a little, replaced by a quieter kind of feeling.

“Mm,” he answered, in a low voice; and two seconds later he went on: “You were quite remarkable just now, too.”

Irene stilled.

“What?”

Bae Do-yoon’s gaze rested on her face. “In that kind of moment you could still grab me and get us hidden — that shows your reactions are fast.”

Irene had meant to look as if it were nothing; hearing this, the corners of her mouth rose all the same.

“Of course.”

Bae Do-yoon gave a laugh. “Right — popular artist, after all.”

This time Irene did not retort.

Under the streetlight the night wind came over, brushing lightly past her cap brim.

Irene suddenly had a strange feeling.

As if, unless something were said now, this night would truly end just like this.

And yet she did not want the ending to come so fast.

So she pressed her lips together and called him, softly: “Bae Do-yoon.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

“Mm?”

Irene paused, as if arranging her words; in the end she only said, low:

“On your way back… be careful.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her; something very shallow and soft appeared in his eyes.

“Good.”

Irene nodded.

And then, as if not quite satisfied with this ending, she added one more line, fast:

“And — don’t laugh at me again.”

Bae Do-yoon laughed at last.

This time it was not that held-in laugh of before, but a plain curving of the lips.

“Noted.”

He paused, and added one more:

“Though that look of yours just now was indeed rather cute.”

Irene stilled, whole person.

“Bae Do-yoon!”

Bae Do-yoon had already shifted back half a step, his tone carrying the ease of one who has got away with it.

“I’m leaving.”

Irene looked at him, bit her lip, and in the end did not chase after him to strike.

She only stood where she was, watching him turn.

A few steps out, Bae Do-yoon turned his head and looked at her once more.

Irene still stood under the streetlight, cap brim pressed low, mask hiding most of her face — but those eyes were very bright.

Seeing him turn, she shifted her line of sight aside a little at once, as if a bit embarrassed — and yet not truly hiding.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her; his lips moved.

Then he raised his hand and made a very light gesture toward her, as if saying: go back in.

Irene stood where she was, not moving, only watching him leave.

Only when that figure had utterly turned into the alley mouth did she slowly let out a breath.

But close behind it, that indefinable warmth in her chest rose up again, slowly.

She stood a while with her head lowered, then lifted a hand to touch her ears, still hot; and at last she could not help one light laugh.

That person just now — his mouth was annoying to the point of crime.

And yet, somehow, he made it impossible to be truly angry.

On annoying mouths and honest ears

He said spiderweb at the exact second the corner deserved a kiss, and logged mental damages at the exact second the streetlight deserved a confession; she legislated three articles of survival logic and broke the second one by laughing, and the third by enforcing it a little on the heavy side. The mask hid the blush and the ears betrayed it; spring was cited as alibi and refused to testify. Nothing was confessed under that streetlight. Everything was entered in the record, preserved as evidence, and carried home in a sleeve held for safety — which is, of course, exactly how confessions travel when neither party is ready to say the word.
