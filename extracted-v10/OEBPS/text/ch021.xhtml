Chapter Twenty-One

Irene: I Do

The same evening · the practice room, continued

Basement one · two cups, one mirror, no third person

Where an old friend turns out to be a simpler word than the thing itself

The two of them talked a while longer.

The topics ran from the old Seoul station, to her first trainee years at the company, to the changes in Bae Do-yoon across these years.

Irene found that he had indeed become different from before.

There had always been a sharpness about him earlier — like a boy forced to see too many things too early, so that even standing quietly he made people feel he was ready to charge forward at any moment.

The sharpness was still there now. It was only pressed down under a deeper steadiness.

Sitting there, posture relaxed, tone calm, an occasional joke on his lips, he was harder than before to look away from.

Irene bent her head over her cup and drank.

She did not know whether the rest room’s lighting was too warm, but her cheeks had been feeling hot the whole while.

She had only wanted to see an old friend.

But after meeting, she discovered that so-called old friend was perhaps not as simple as she had imagined.

At least the Bae Do-yoon of this moment was no longer the person in her memory who only calmly handled her troubles and spoke like a written evaluation report.

He seemed more mature. And more dangerous.

Not the danger that frightens people — the danger that makes people want, without noticing, to step closer.

Holding her cup, Irene suddenly seemed to think of something; she lifted her head and asked, smiling:

“By the way.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “Mm?”

She blinked, keeping her tone as natural as she could. “We haven’t seen each other in so long — have you found a girlfriend?”

When the words landed, Bae Do-yoon did not answer at once; he only looked at her.

Irene had been holding the smile on her face, but under that look her heart went inexplicably thin.

She glanced down at her cup by reflex, then lifted her head quickly, pretending nothing had happened.

“Why are you looking at me like that?”

The corner of Bae Do-yoon’s lips moved a little. “What — you care about my private life that much?”

Irene’s heartbeat slipped once, but she straightened her back a little, as if finally finding her legitimate ground.

“You said we are friends. So what’s wrong with a friend caring about a friend’s private life?”

Having said it, she even put on a deliberately justified air.

“Isn’t it normal for friends to ask this?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her and did not speak.

Irene, looked at that way, grew a little uncomfortable; the tips of her ears reddened by degrees. She gave up pretence and set the cup on the table, hurrying him:

“Ai — say it, say it quickly.”

“I’m very curious.”

Bae Do-yoon could not help a laugh.

“Very curious?”

“Mm.”

Irene nodded. “Extremely curious.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once, in the manner of a man with no choice left, and finally said:

“No.”

“Single.”

Hearing it, Irene’s eyes lit, almost in an instant.

It was a very fine reaction; on another person it might have passed unnoticed. But Bae Do-yoon sat not far from her, and he had been looking at her all along — so that small change was seen by him with perfect clarity.

Irene herself seemed to realize it quickly too.

She gave a light cough, lowered her head, and took up her water cup, pretending only to confirm offhand:

“Mm? Really?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, perfectly straight-faced. “Really.”

“When have I ever lied to you?”

Hearing that sentence, Irene could not help twisting her lips. “As if you haven’t lied to me plenty.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted a brow. “Have I?”

“Of course you have.”

Irene, as if finally catching his handle, found her tone lightening.

“I was strung along by you no few times back then.”

Bae Do-yoon thought about it. “Examples, please. Why can’t I remember any?”

Irene said at once:

“The first time we met, you said the company was close to the station.”

Bae Do-yoon: …

Irene looked at him and began settling old accounts. “As a result I sat in a taxi for ages.”

Bae Do-yoon said, evenly: “For Seoul, that is not far.”

“That is your standard,” Irene sniffed lightly. “For me, just arrived in Seoul then, it was as far as going to another city.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “I’ll count that one as imprecise expression on my part.”

Irene continued:

“Another time, I said the month-end evaluation pressure was enormous, and you told me I only had to perform normally.”

“What is wrong with that sentence?”

“Enormously wrong,” Irene said, serious. “At moments like that, performing normally is the hardest thing of all.”

Bae Do-yoon let a moment pass. “Then what should I have said?”

Irene looked at him, and suddenly smiled.

“You should have said: Bae Joo-hyun — you definitely can.”

Having said it, she herself stilled first.

That sentence had only been an offhand joke. But spoken aloud, it suddenly dragged the trainee-era self of many years ago back into the room.

Then, she had truly wanted to hear such words.

Only one simple sentence: you definitely can.

Bae Do-yoon seemed to hear what lay inside her words too.

He was quiet a few seconds; then he looked at her and said: “Bae Joo-hyun.”

Irene lifted her head. “Mm?”

Bae Do-yoon’s tone was calm, and very serious.

“You definitely can.”

Irene froze.

The sentence came too late — late by many years.

And yet she did not know why, when it truly came out of Bae Do-yoon’s mouth, something in her chest still moved, lightly.

She looked at him, and only after a good while said, in a small voice: “You’re saying it now?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “Making up for it.”

Irene lowered her head and smiled.

“Who makes up encouragement years and years later?”

“Late is better than never.”

Irene lifted her eyes to him; the smile in them had gone very soft. “Then I’ll accept it reluctantly.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “Thank you.”

“You really don’t hold back at all.”

Both of them laughed.

The delicate air the girlfriend question had made seemed to be carried past, lightly, by the old story.

Irene lowered her head and drank water.

But her mood was plainly brighter than before.

Bae Do-yoon had no girlfriend.

Single.

She should not be too visibly glad about that; they were only friends who had not met in a long while.

And yet the corners of her mouth simply would not stay pressed down.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her and suddenly spoke. “You look happy.”

Irene’s motion stopped; she lifted her head and worked to make her expression natural.

“Uh… do I?”

“You do.”

“You saw wrong,” Irene said, pressing her lips.

Bae Do-yoon said: “My eyesight is decent.”

Irene: …

She discovered that in front of Bae Do-yoon she could never pretend particularly well.

She had debuted so long ago; she was long used to managing her expression. Before lenses, reporters, fans, she could keep perfect propriety.

And yet sitting opposite Bae Do-yoon, every smallest thought of hers seemed easy for him to see.

Irene simply counterattacked. “Then what about you?”

Bae Do-yoon, face full of question marks: “Me what?”

Irene looked at him. “When I asked whether you had a girlfriend, you didn’t seem very surprised either.”

Bae Do-yoon said, lightly: “A friend caring about a friend’s private life is normal.”

Irene was blocked by her own words. “You learn fast.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “Learned and used on the spot.”

Irene hmph’d lightly; a moment later, as if offhand, she asked again:

“Then why haven’t you found anyone?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “You have a great many questions today.”

“Because we haven’t seen each other in so long,” Irene said, fully justified. “All those empty years in between — one has to catch up on the situation, doesn’t one?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “Reasonable.”

“So?”

Bae Do-yoon weighed it a moment, then said: “No time.”

Irene was plainly not satisfied with that answer. “Only no time?”

“And I haven’t met anyone suitable,” Bae Do-yoon said, after genuine consideration.

That sentence quieted Irene a little; her fingers rubbed the cup wall lightly, and she asked, in the manner of pure casualness: “Then what kind of person counts as suitable?”

Bae Do-yoon glanced at her.

She had only been following the conversation; but under that glance she suddenly felt the question had crossed a boundary.

She was about to say you don’t have to answer — and Bae Do-yoon had already opened his mouth.

“Someone who makes me want to see them again.”

Irene lowered her head and drank a little water. “The requirement sounds low.”

Bae Do-yoon shook his head. “Not low.”

“Why not?”

“I don’t like wasting time. If I am willing to see a person several times, that person is special.”

Irene’s ear tips reddened by degrees. Bae Do-yoon today was really good at making people misunderstand.

No.

Perhaps it was not misunderstanding. Perhaps he was doing it on purpose.

Irene asked, lightly: “Then is coming to see me tonight a waste of time?”

Bae Do-yoon studied her and asked, smiling: “What do you think?”

Irene did not answer; she only looked at him.

After a few seconds, Bae Do-yoon said: “All right — it isn’t.”

Irene’s chest skipped, lightly.

Bae Do-yoon added: “And it is worth more than I expected.”

This time Irene truly could not hold her expression together.

She looked down at her cup, and the corners of her mouth would keep curving upward.

“You really have changed a lot.”

“Where?”

“You didn’t speak like this before.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Then how did I speak before?”

Irene thought about it, and imitating his old tone, deliberately straightened her face:

“The meeting itself possesses a certain value of catching up on old times, and does not constitute a waste of time.”

Bae Do-yoon: …

Having delivered it, Irene laughed first herself.

When she laughed, her whole person grew much brighter than usual. Very natural, very at ease — as if for a while forgetting she was an entertainer at all.

Bae Do-yoon watched a moment, then said: “When you laugh like this now, you look better than on stage.”

Irene’s laugh slowed to a stop.

She lifted her head to look at him. “Here we go again.”

“What?”

“Making people misunderstand again.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “This is not misunderstanding.”

“Then what is it?”

Bae Do-yoon let the question sit; he only looked at her quietly for a few seconds.

Irene had meant to press further, but under his eyes she was the one who moved her line of sight away first.

“Forget it,” she said, in a small voice. “I won’t ask.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Afraid of the answer?”

Irene lifted her eyes to him.

“No.”

She paused a moment; her lips curved, lightly. “I think some answers can be left for next time.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, then nodded. “That works too.”

The smile in Irene’s eyes deepened.

“So there really is a next time?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “If you are willing…”

Irene, almost without hesitation: “I do.”

Only after the words were out did she realize how those three characters sounded in this context.

Her cheeks warmed; she added at once: “I mean — the next meeting.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, a little laughter in his eyes. “I know.”

Irene suspected he was entirely doing it on purpose, and glared at him lightly.

“You’d better really know.”

Bae Do-yoon smiled and did not expose her.

Then Irene suddenly remembered something and looked at him again.

“But I asked you — won’t you ask me?”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his eyes. “Ask what?”

Irene swung her cup, lightly. “For instance — whether I have a boyfriend.”

She had only meant to throw the question back. But when Bae Do-yoon truly looked at her, she suddenly grew nervous.

Bae Do-yoon did not rush the question; he said: “This question — may I ask it?”

Irene had not expected him to say that.

By his character, if he truly wanted to ask, he could simply ask it outright. And yet he stopped a moment and asked whether he may.

Irene said, in a low voice: “You may.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “Then — do you have a boyfriend?”

Irene shook her head. “No.”

Bae Do-yoon’s eyes were very calm. But Irene still sensed, keenly, that after hearing the answer he seemed to relax a little.

She could not help asking: “You just breathed a sigh of relief too, didn’t you?”

Bae Do-yoon did not deny it. “Mm.”

Irene froze; she had not expected him to admit it so directly.

“You…”

Bae Do-yoon said: “A friend caring about a friend’s private life is normal.”

Irene could not help laughing. “You really do hold grudges.”

“Learned and used on the spot, merely.”

Irene laughed and lowered her head.

They were only two ordinary questions, plainly.

But on this night of long separation, in this quiet private rest room, they were like two people quietly confirming a certain possibility.

Neither of them was anyone’s lover; neither had yet fully walked into another’s life. The empty years between had not, it seemed, blocked every road — they had left one very narrow, very hidden gap.

Whoever stepped forward once more might see another kind of answer.

Irene said, softly: “Looks like we are both quite busy.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “Mm. Busy to the point of having no time to date — or perhaps we haven’t met anyone we want to date.”

Irene looked down at her cup.

They had just mutually confirmed their singlehood.

Plainly only ordinary chat — and yet the air had grown unordinary.

And so, she did not know what possessed her; suddenly, against all sense, she said:

“How about I introduce you to someone?”

The words out, she herself stilled first.

Bae Do-yoon lifted his eyes to her. “You’ll introduce me?”

Irene was past the point of retraction; she could only harden her scalp and go on, smiling in a very natural way.

“Right. Don’t forget — I am an SM Entertainment artist.”

She lifted her chin a little, as if proving her resources. “Our company has ever so many great beauties, you know.”

Having said it she regretted it a little: she had only meant to deflect the thickening air. And yet somehow, once said, her heart felt worse — as if she had pushed Bae Do-yoon toward someone else with her own hand.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, the corner of his lips moving. “All right.”

Irene had expected him to refuse, or at least to say no need.

And he actually agreed…

Irene blinked. “You really want one?”

Bae Do-yoon, expression calm: “Didn’t you say you would introduce?”

“I meant…” Irene caught for a moment.

She wanted to say she had only mentioned it casually; but saying that now would be too obvious.

So she could only keep pretending at generosity.

“I only think — if you really have no time to meet women, then as a friend, introducing one isn’t impossible.”

Bae Do-yoon considered it a moment, then said, lightly: “Then Yoona would be good.”

Irene’s heart jumped hard; she lifted her head almost at once.

“What?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her and repeated: “Yoona. I said she would be quite good.”

Irene’s eyes went wide. “You mean Yoona-unnie?”

“Mm,” Bae Do-yoon said, tone very natural. “Isn’t she an SM Entertainment artist?”

Irene looked at him, her expression for one instant extremely rich.

She had only meant a joke. And this one sentence of his had pressed down all the small joy that had just sprouted in her chest.

Yoona-unnie was of course good.

Pretty, excellent, high national recognition. Years since debut, stable popularity, and a good nature too.

Precisely because she knew all that too well, hearing Bae Do-yoon say she was good stuffed something inexplicable into her heart.

Irene worked to keep her tone level.

“Could it be you like Yoona-unnie’s type?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once. “She is SM Entertainment’s face. Who doesn’t like her?”

Irene: …

The sentence was very reasonable; she could not refute it.

Yoona was indeed a type many people would like. Bae Do-yoon saying he liked her was not strange at all.

But Irene simply felt uncomfortable.

She pressed her lips and took up her cup for a sip of water.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her and asked: “Will you introduce her to me?”

Irene nearly choked on the water; she set the cup down and looked at Bae Do-yoon with a complex expression.

“Her I cannot do.”

“Why not?”

Irene said, perfectly straight-faced: “Yoona-unnie is too strong.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted a brow. “Too strong?”

“Mm,” Irene nodded. “I don’t have the ability to persuade her to come see a man.”

Having said it, she added one more line: “Especially one like you.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “One like me is what?”

Irene looked at him; she had meant to say a very dangerous man, but at her lips it felt too ambiguous.

So she changed it: “A person who makes people misunderstand very easily.”

Bae Do-yoon smiled. “That counts as a flaw?”

“Of course it counts.”

“And you’ll still introduce me?”

Irene was caught; she stared at Bae Do-yoon a few seconds, then simply turned her line of sight away.

“Anyway Yoona-unnie is no good.”

Bae Do-yoon shook his head. “Then it’s not interesting.”

Irene looked at him, incredulous.

“Not interesting?”

“Mm,” Bae Do-yoon said. “Didn’t you say SM has many beauties? I picked the most famous one, and you say no.”

Hearing his calm tone, that discomfort in Irene rose a little further.

She could not help saying: “You truly want to date Yoona-unnie? Let me tell you — don’t dream it.”

Her tone grew serious. “Even if unnie went blind and took a liking to you, the company would never agree.”

Bae Do-yoon: …

The information content of that sentence was somewhat large.

He looked at Irene. “Blind?”

Only then did Irene realize she had spoken too fast.

She gave a light cough and tried to repair it. “I don’t mean you’re bad.”

“Then what do you mean?”

“I mean…” Irene paused a moment. “An artist of Yoona-unnie’s level — dating is not her affair alone.”

That sentence, at least, was true.

She put away the joking tone and grew a little serious.

“Company, fans, advertisements, drama crews, brand sides — so many things get pulled in.”

“Especially for an artist with Yoona-unnie’s national recognition: every one of her moves gets magnified.”

“So she cannot casually meet a man — much less come out because of one introduction from me.”

Reaching there, she could not help glancing at Bae Do-yoon once.

“And it’s you, besides.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “What about me?”

Irene pressed her lips.

“You are a prosecutor now.”

“So?”

“So it’s more troublesome.”

Irene said, in a low voice: “An artist and a prosecutor meeting — if it’s photographed, what will the media write?”

“Ordinary friends?”

“Do you think they’d be that kind?”

Bae Do-yoon smiled. “Probably not. I’d guess another round of oiled-and-salted chaotic writing.”

Irene hmph’d lightly. “Good that you know.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his cup and drank a sip of water. “So your meaning is: Yoona and I have no possibility?”

“Of course not,” Irene answered quickly. “I am analyzing from objective conditions.”

“Mm.” Bae Do-yoon smiled and nodded. “I understand.”

Irene kept feeling that mm of his carried a suggestive weight.

She could not help asking: “What are you smiling at?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “I’m not smiling.”

“You plainly are.”

“I only feel your analysis is very thorough.”

Irene looked at him; a distinctly bad feeling rose in her chest.

Bae Do-yoon set the cup down, tone calm:

“I said Yoona’s name once, casually. And you have already analyzed for me all the way through company, fans, media and advertising contracts.”

Irene: …

She finally realized she had overreacted — and very obviously.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her and went on:

“Joo-hyun.”

“Mm?”

“You don’t really want to introduce me to anyone, do you?”

Irene’s heartbeat lurched a full beat; she denied it by reflex: “No.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

“Really?”

Irene forced composure.

“Really.”

“Then introduce me one more.”

… Irene went suddenly silent.

A little more laughter entered Bae Do-yoon’s eyes. “Why not speaking?”

Irene held her cup, voice somewhat low.

“Suddenly it feels not suitable.”

“Not suitable where?”

Irene pressed her lips.

“People at the company are all very busy.”

“And?”

“Artists dating is very risky.”

“And?”

Irene finally lifted her head and looked at him, half embarrassed, half annoyed.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

“Mm?”

“Must you ask it this clearly?”

Bae Do-yoon watched her, the corner of his lips curving a little.

“Professional habit.”

“This isn’t an interrogation.”

“Which is why my tone is already gentle.”

Irene was left exasperated and amused at once.

She discovered that tonight she seemed to have been led along by Bae Do-yoon the whole way. She had been the one to ask about the girlfriend first; she had been the one to offer introductions first; and yet the one left embarrassed was, in the end, herself.

Irene put the cup down with decision. “All right. I admit it.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “Admit what?”

Irene drew a deep breath and worked to look fully justified.

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “So — only politeness.”

Irene heard the laughter in his voice and could not help glaring.

Irene: …

Somehow it had even turned into her own fault.

Bae Do-yoon said, lightly: “And what I said is also fact.”

Irene’s just-settled mood was hooked up again by that sentence.

“What fact?”

Irene looked at him, her tone going a degree sour despite herself. “Yes — Yoona-unnie is of course good.”

She lowered her head and arranged the napkins beside her cup, pretending not to care.

Bae Do-yoon watched her small movements.

“You like her very much?”

Irene’s motion stopped; she lifted her head to look at him. “I am not unhappy.”

“Yes.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “Then it seems I misunderstood.”

Irene looked at him. His words were a retreat — and yet she felt he did not believe it at all.

She grew a little deflated.

After a while she said, in a low voice:

“Do you truly want to meet Yoona-unnie?”

Bae Do-yoon did not answer at once.

Irene looked at him and regretted asking again: if he said want, what would she do? Actually go and ask for him? She did not want to at all. But not helping would make her introducing-you offer look like mere talk. It had been mere talk — yet seen through by Bae Do-yoon, it became extremely awkward.

Bae Do-yoon watched her shifting expression, and at last stopped teasing her.

He gave a light laugh. “Don’t want to.”

Irene froze. “What?”

Bae Do-yoon said: “I don’t want to meet Yoona.”

Irene blinked.

“Then why did you say she was good just now?”

“Her being good, and whether I want to meet her, are two different matters.”

Irene was silent a moment; that little emotion of hers seemed suddenly to land on empty air.

Bae Do-yoon went on: “And I don’t much like being introduced by others.”

“Why not?”

“Troublesome.”

Irene looked at him. “That is troublesome too?”

“Mm,” Bae Do-yoon said. “Having to know someone anew, greet them anew, judge anew what kind of person they are.”

His tone was very light. “My work already holds too much judging. Privately, I don’t want to do it again.”

Irene quieted.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “So, compared with being introduced — I prefer someone I already know.”

Irene’s chest skipped, lightly.

She vaguely guessed what he would say next; and precisely because she guessed it, she did not dare catch the words.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, voice calm. “Especially someone I have known a long time…”

The rest room went suddenly quiet.

Irene’s fingers tightened a little on her cup; she lifted her eyes to him, and Bae Do-yoon did not move his gaze away.

Someone known a long time.

There was no third person here. And no third person was needed.

Irene, stubborn: “That was from the angle of a company senior’s professional safety.”

Irene could not help snatching the cushion beside her and hitting him with it, lightly.

Only after hitting him did Irene realize the motion was a little intimate.

They had not seen each other in so long; and yet in this moment she seemed suddenly returned to many years before. When she was not yet Irene. When he was not yet this prosecutor with the distance about him. When their being together could be more casual — not every sentence overthought, not every motion weighed for whether it crossed a line.

Bae Do-yoon put the cushion back on the sofa. “Angry?”

Irene turned her face away. “No.”

Irene whipped her head around to look at him. “Who is jealous?”

Bae Do-yoon said nothing; he only looked at her.

Irene, looked at for a few seconds, let her voice sink slowly. “I am not.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “I haven’t yet said whose vinegar you are eating.”

…

Irene was utterly speechless. That sentence of Bae Do-yoon’s was too bad.

She now finally confirmed it: a few years apart, Bae Do-yoon had truly changed. Before, he teased occasionally, but was mostly calm and restrained. Now he was still calm — but into the calm had been added a composure very hard to withstand.
