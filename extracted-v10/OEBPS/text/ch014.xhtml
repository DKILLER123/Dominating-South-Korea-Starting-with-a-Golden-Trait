Chapter Fourteen

Women Only Slow Down My Case-Solving Speed

The same afternoon · the originals on a screen

JYP Entertainment · an internal meeting room

Clearer pictures, and none of them useful

Bae Do-yoon had assumed that at JYP, at least, the original material would yield clues clearer than any screenshot.

The fact disappointed him a little.

The original CCTV was indeed much clearer than the captures JYP had previously submitted. But clear is not the same as useful.

The suspicious person on the footage wore a cap and mask throughout; ordinary clothing; no particularly distinctive build. He seemed to know the cameras’ positions well: at every appearance he never faced a lens frontally, and the places he stood precisely avoided the angles easiest to identify a face from. The parking-lot entrance time was likewise exact — he did not come too close, and left no direct contact trace of any kind. On camera he looked more like a passerby who happened to be passing.

Unless the several anomalies were laid on one timeline, it would be hard to prove from any single one of them that he was the harasser.

Bae Do-yoon checked the emails’ original information next.

The result was equally unideal. Temporary mailboxes; sending sources obviously processed; IP addresses hopping several times over; no tracing the true location in a short time. The online comment accounts were much the same: registration times different, nicknames different, avatars different, some accounts deleted soon after commenting. The comment content carried clear targeting, and yet its tone stayed on an ambiguous boundary — making a person uncomfortable, without every sentence constituting an explicit threat.

The gift parcels offered little breakthrough either. Sender information false. Telephone numbers not connecting. Sending locations scattered across convenience stores and courier pickup points in different districts; payment methods leaving no direct identity behind.

Bae Do-yoon finished reading all the material and leaned back in his chair.

Kim Mi-ju stood at his side; seeing his expression, she knew the situation was not optimistic.

Park Dae-jun noticed too.

He asked, low: “Prosecutor — is the material still not enough?”

Bae Do-yoon let a second pass before answering.

He looked once more at the CCTV frame paused on the screen: the capped figure standing near the parking-lot entrance, head slightly lowered, the whole face almost entirely hidden behind brim and mask.

“It is not ‘not enough.’”

Park Dae-jun paused.

Bae Do-yoon went on: “The other side is careful. He is not an ordinary extreme fan.”

Kim Mi-ju asked: “Why?”

Two kinds of obsession, and which one is worse

Ordinary extreme fans often hurry to prove their existence. They want to be seen; they want the idol to know who they are; so they leave behind masses of personally distinctive information.

But this person is different. He approaches Bae Suzy while deliberately hiding himself. He manufactures presence while avoiding leaving identity. Each time he comes not too close — and yet close enough for the victim to be aware that he is there.

An impulsive fan wants to be seen. This one wants to never be seen — and to always be felt.

This kind is more troublesome than the simply impulsive type. Impulsive extreme fans may be dangerous, but they are often easy to expose. The man on this footage is more restrained, more planned: a hunter hidden in darkness, ready to deliver his one blow at any time.

Park Dae-jun’s face sank as well.

“Then next…”

“Next, widen the information sources,” Bae Do-yoon said. “We cannot look only at material your company keeps in-house. Platforms, couriers, telecom, surrounding road CCTV, outsourced security personnel lists, schedule sheets touched by event organizers — all of it gets checked.”

Park Dae-jun nodded: “I will cooperate.”

Bae Do-yoon stood up.

“That is enough for today.”

Kim Mi-ju immediately gathered her notebook and the document bag. Park Dae-jun rose with them: “I’ll see you both out.”

Bae Do-yoon did not refuse.

But just as the few of them were preparing to leave the meeting room, footsteps came from outside.

The next second, the meeting-room door was pushed open from without.

A man and a woman walked in.

The man in front was simply dressed, and yet highly identifiable. Even without any formal introduction, Bae Do-yoon recognized him at the first glance.

Park Jin-young.

The founder of JYP.

Park Jin-young

Founder of JYP Entertainment; the representative-nim of every greeting in the building

Once wanted to be an entertainer himself — singing, dancing, writing all strong; only his look missed the idol mainstream of his era, and the early interviews went badly

Famous for the story of being turned away at every big company’s door, SM’s included; he still tells it on variety shows, and Lee Soo-man still teases him for remembering it after all these years

He walks into a tense meeting room and lowers its temperature one degree without apologizing for anything. He offers two prosecution visitors a tour of his company the way another man would offer an alibi — neatly, and with a reason attached.

The founder

In the Korean entertainment world, Park Jin-young counts as a very special existence. His youngest dream had in fact been to become an entertainer: voice, dance and compositional ability all good — only his outward conditions did not fit the entertainment companies’ mainstream idol aesthetic of those years, so the early interviews went badly. The stories of him hitting walls at the big companies’ audition doors have been told on many variety shows; his having gone to SM Entertainment’s interview especially is dragged out constantly for teasing. Park Jin-young himself has recalled Lee Soo-man’s reaction of the time on programs more than once, and Lee Soo-man has laughed and teased back that so many years have gone by and the man still remembers it.

Of these public anecdotes Bae Do-yoon had heard some. He had not expected to meet Park Jin-young himself in a setting like this one.

And the woman standing behind Park Jin-young made the meeting room’s atmosphere more subtle still.

Bae Suzy.

The protagonist of this incident.

Meeting-room inventory: the protagonist of the file, in person

Hat low-brimmed, shading without hiding

Coat light knit, pale, soft at the shoulders

Hair long, falling naturally at her sides, no stage styling in it

Face almost no heavy makeup; faint shadows under the eyes; fingers lightly gripping her sleeve, as if holding her own calm in place

No lights, no lenses, no fans’ cheers. Standing in a meeting room she is not the top entertainer countless people love; she is an ordinary young woman who has been losing her sense of safety for a long while.

Her beauty is not the sharp, pressing kind: clear, clean, soft of brow and eye, the features sitting well together — and the eyes that are bright and smiling on screen carry very little smile in them today.

Plate set from the meeting-room scene, Chapter 14 — hat, knit coat, no stage anywhere

Park Jin-young walked in, and his gaze landed first on Bae Do-yoon.

“Prosecutor Bae?”

Bae Do-yoon stood and inclined his head slightly.

“I am Bae Do-yoon.”

Park Jin-young offered his hand of his own accord.

“Thank you for coming in person. Originally this kind of matter shouldn’t have been made a prosecutor’s special trip.”

Bae Do-yoon shook it, tone level.

“The material needed confirming on site.”

Park Jin-young nodded; his expression was not light.

“I heard from Lawyer Park. The material we provided the prosecution earlier — indeed some places in it were processed too much.”

“Not some places,” Bae Do-yoon said. “Most of the key positions lost their investigative value.”

Park Dae-jun stood beside them, his expression somewhat embarrassed.

Park Jin-young, to his credit, did not immediately argue. He was silent a moment before speaking.

“The company has the company’s concerns. Suzy’s attention level right now is too high; once this matter gets out, it is very easily magnified by the media.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “I understand.”

“We are not refusing to cooperate.” Park Jin-young glanced at Bae Suzy behind him. “We simply none of us want the matter to grow too large.”

Bae Do-yoon did not take the sentence up; he only signaled him to go on.

Park Jin-young said: “This visit, too, was decided after Suzy and I talked it over. Since the prosecution has come to the company in person, we thought: rather than continuing to have legal relay everything, better that we explain the situation directly.”

His voice went a shade lower.

“If it helps the investigation, we will cooperate as much as we can.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at Bae Suzy.

“Is this Bae Suzy-ssi’s meaning as well?”

Bae Suzy had not spoken until then. Hearing herself asked directly, she lifted her eyes.

“Yes.”

“I also want to know who is doing these things.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once — without the curiosity people show a star, and without any extra warmth on account of her identity.

From the moment she entered the meeting room, Bae Suzy had been observing the situation in front of her, unhurried and unshowing. Her first glance had been at Kim Mi-ju: standing by the meeting table with a notebook in hand, capable in bearing, in every way matching Bae Suzy’s image of prosecution personnel. So at first she had assumed, by instinct, that Kim Mi-ju was the one in charge of this matter. Only after Park Dae-jun’s introduction did she discover that the young man seated beside the table’s head was the prosecutor who had come.

Bae Suzy had indeed been surprised, then.

She had assumed the prosecutor handling this kind of matter would be an older man, or an experienced woman with a serious face. In no version had she imagined someone like Bae Do-yoon.

Too young. And too unlike any prosecutor I imagined.

In outward appearance at least, Bae Do-yoon outshone even many of the male actors entertainment companies were currently packaging. His features were clear, his brows and eyes calm, his nose high, his face’s lines clean and decisive. Even in ordinary plain clothes, that upright, composed air was hard to conceal.

JYP did not lack people of superior appearance. Entertainers, trainees, actors, models — Bae Suzy had seen too many. But the feeling Bae Do-yoon gave was different from all of them. He had no camera-trained display-sense of entertainers about him, no trace of performed charm. He simply sat there, expression quiet, tone restrained, all of his attention on the case itself.

And the more so, the more particular he seemed.

Bae Suzy realized suddenly that her line of sight had stayed on him somewhat long. Startled a little in her heart, she hurriedly dropped her eyes.

The feeling was unfamiliar to her. It was not as simple as a good-looking face: the entertainment world holds too many good-looking people, and she no longer sways easily over a face. What truly surprised her was how comfortable his way of speaking was. He did not speak down to her; he did not treat her as a product the company must protect; he showed no extra excitement at her being a top entertainer. When pressing JYP for complete material he had been firm — and had kept the measure in his firmness the whole time.

That gave Bae Suzy a touch of something she could not quite name.

The box she has been kept in

All this while, everyone around her has been saying: we will protect you; don’t let the matter grow; the company will handle it. Goodwill, all of it. And yet sometimes she feels as if she has been put into a sealed box.

Bae Do-yoon is different. In his look at her there is no fan’s heat and no media’s probing. There is only a person being treated as a party who may express her own will.

That feeling loosened, by a little, some place in Bae Suzy’s chest that had been taut for a very long time.

She is even somewhat surprised at herself: toward a man met for the first time today, this strange stirring — not liking. More like meeting, in the middle of unease, someone she feels she can trust.

Bae Do-yoon knew nothing of these changes inside Bae Suzy.

Even if he had noticed her observing him, he would not have taken it to heart. He is a prosecutor now.

Women only slow down my case-solving speed.

He sat down again and pushed the materials on the table a little aside.

“Since Bae Suzy-ssi is willing to explain in person, I’ll ask several questions.”

Bae Suzy nodded.

“Good.”

Kim Mi-ju opened her notebook at once, ready to record. Park Jin-young and Park Dae-jun also took seats nearby.

Over the time that followed, Bae Do-yoon asked the contents he judged important. Bae Suzy recalled as best she could, and answered as best she could.

Only, many of the things themselves were blurred.

An entertainer’s schedule is too dense: public activities, advertisement shoots, drama promotion, magazine interviews, company practice — moving to a different place almost every day. Many anomalies, at the moment they occur, are not immediately defined as danger. A person standing far off. An email in a strange tone. A gift with no signature. A comment that makes one uncomfortable. Taken one by one, none of them seems to reach the level of must report to the police. But when they appear in succession — and even begin approaching her non-public schedules — only then does the wrongness arrive, after the fact.

Bae Do-yoon listened seriously. Now and then he interrupted, asking Bae Suzy to confirm a time, a place, a person. But he did not hurry her when she hesitated, and showed no impatience when she could not recall some detail.

Bae Suzy had been somewhat tense at first; as the questions and answers went on, her state steadied by degrees instead. She found his questions very clear. Not one of them was asked idly. He seemed all along to be trying to string those blurred fragments into a single line.

Only, that line was not easy to string.

Her information matters and stays at the level of feeling and memory: usable as investigation direction, not convertible directly into evidence. Directions are not proof; proof is what he goes back to hunting.

A dozen minutes and more later, Bae Do-yoon stopped his pen.

Kim Mi-ju looked at him. Bae Suzy also lifted her eyes, as if waiting on his judgment.

Bae Do-yoon looked down at the few records he had just set in order.

After a moment, he still frowned, very lightly.

The situation had not become clear at once because of Bae Suzy’s personal explanation. What she gave was important, and most of it still stayed at the level of feeling and recollection. Such things can serve as investigation directions; they cannot become evidence directly. Bae Do-yoon could not infer so-called key intelligence from a few sentences. Still less could he, because Bae Suzy said that person seemed to be waiting for me, conclude outright that someone inside JYP must be leaking schedules.

Case-handling is not story-writing. There are not so many moments in it where one sentence breaks the whole case open. What they held now was still only a few incomplete fragments.

Bae Do-yoon set the pen down, and said in a level tone: “What you just said is useful. But it is not enough.”

Bae Suzy’s eyes darkened, a fraction.

Park Jin-young frowned as well.

Bae Do-yoon saw their reactions and did not revise the sentence.

“I cannot make a judgment on your feeling alone.”

Bae Suzy said, low: “I know.”

Bae Do-yoon went on: “But your feeling can become an investigation direction.”

She lifted her head.

Bae Do-yoon looked at the materials on the table.

“The problem now is that we lack the thing which connects these matters.”

Kim Mi-ju asked: “For example?”

“The person behind one same account. One same dispatch source. Or one same schedule-information leak chain,” Bae Do-yoon said. “If a single point connects, the matter becomes much clearer.”

Park Jin-young asked, in a heavy voice: “And if it never connects?”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his eyes.

“Then we investigate from another direction.”

“What direction?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at Park Dae-jun.

“Do not stare only at what the harasser left behind.”

He paused.

“Investigate why he could know those places.”

Bae Suzy looked at Bae Do-yoon, and that strange feeling floated up in her lightly again.

She could not say why. It was precisely this calm amounting almost to restraint that made her feel steadied instead. Because she could hear it: Bae Do-yoon was not brushing her off. He was truly investigating — even now, with no clue yet in his hands.

On women and case-solving speed

The chapter’s title is his joke at himself, filed under defense mechanisms. The record shows the opposite and the same at once: attention paid to him costs him nothing, and attention paid by him costs nobody anything — every look in this room is logged, weighed, and returned to its owner unused. A prosecutor who notices everything and acts on nothing is not cold. He is merely keeping the evidence chain clean, and the chain includes his own eyes.
