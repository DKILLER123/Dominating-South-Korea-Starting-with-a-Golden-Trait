Chapter Twenty-Five

Unnie, Are We Sick?

The same night · next door’s living room

One door quietly closed, one corridor light gone dark, one water glass going cold in a hand

Where a smell becomes a secret, and a secret becomes a symptom

Boram had meant to laugh at her; but seeing how serious her expression was, the laughter slowly withdrew again.

She put the cushion back on the sofa and seated herself along the motion of it.

“All right. I’ll hear you out.”

Park Ji-yeon went to the sofa’s edge and sat, hugged a cushion to herself, leaned her whole person a little closer to Boram, and then said, gravely:

“There is a smell on Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

Boram looked at her once.

“If you do not explain that sentence clearly, it is very easy to misunderstand. Middle of the night, the neighbor just left, and you sit here saying, perfectly seriously, that there is a smell on him. I have already begun to wonder whether I should keep listening.”

Park Ji-yeon frowned at once.

“Unnie, don’t misunderstand. Not that kind of smell. A good smell. Not sweat, not cigarette smoke, and certainly not alcohol. It’s perfume.”

Boram had still been somewhat casual; hearing this, she leaned her body back a little against the sofa.

“Perfume is hardly strange. He is a person who meets people too; it isn’t as if he goes about utterly unkempt. Besides, a man wearing perfume is perfectly normal. It hardly warrants that face of yours — the face of someone who has discovered major evidence.”

“If it were ordinary perfume, of course I wouldn’t be like this.”

Park Ji-yeon held the cushion a little tighter; her voice went lower still.

“The problem is — it isn’t a man’s perfume. It is very clearly a woman’s perfume.”

Boram’s expression stopped. After a few seconds she looked at Park Ji-yeon again.

“Are you certain? You were just eating jokbal and jumping about; this room currently has sauce, garlic and sesame-oil smells all mixed together — and from that one moment at the doorway you could pick out a perfume? I am a little impressed, and a little doubtful…”

Park Ji-yeon shook her head at once.

“I couldn’t be wrong. That smell was very clear. It came out when he approached the doorway. And it wasn’t sprayed — it was transferred. As if it had settled onto his coat.”

Boram did not rush to tease her this time.

She looked at the door, thoughtful; after a moment she spoke.

“Do you know which one?”

Park Ji-yeon looked at her, paused a little.

“CCET. Pink Encounter. The luxury among perfumes. Many artists use it.”

Boram’s expression changed, subtly.

Park Ji-yeon caught it instantly.

“Unnie, you know it, right? That face you just made is the face of knowing.”

Boram cleared her throat lightly and set her water glass back on the low table.

“I know it. Someone in our waiting room used to wear it, and I often smelled it around the stylists’ side too. That scent indeed isn’t niche, and it leaves an impression easily. At the front, a little fruit; behind it, a softer floral and musk. Not sweet-cloying. Very clean.”

Park Ji-yeon leaned closer at once, her eyes brightening more plainly.

“See — you also think I didn’t mis-smell it, right? This scent really is distinctive. I didn’t just invent it to scare myself.”

Boram did not rebut at once.

She leaned against the sofa back, fingers touching the rim of her glass lightly, her line of sight drifting once more toward the door.

“If it truly is Pink Encounter — then it gets a little interesting.”

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi does not look like a person who would spray that scent himself. Of course, one cannot say it is wholly impossible. But his state just now — casual clothes, deep night, no alcohol, and he didn’t look like someone just back from overtime either — and on top of that, this smell left on his coat… It is truly hard not to think too much.”

Park Ji-yeon, hearing her say this, visibly perked up.

“That is exactly what I mean. Taken one by one, every single thing can be explained.”

“Coming home late can be said to be business. Dressing casually can be said to be a rest day. The perfume smell can be said to be passing somewhere by chance. But put together — it isn’t that simple.”

The threads of evidence · each explainable alone, insoluble together

01Return past midnight — explainable: business

02Casual dress, no alcohol, no overtime air — explainable: rest day, an evening out of uniform

03Woman’s perfume on the coat, transferred not sprayed — explainable: passing somewhere by chance, a borrowed car, a favor repaid at close range

04The set of three together — verdict of the cushion conference: not that simple

Corollary on distance, jointly ratified: for a scent to stay on cloth, either the distance was close or the time was not short. At the very least, not a dozen seconds standing in an elevator.

The scent dossier · CCET, Pink Encounter · opened at whisper volume, closed by pact

IdentificationCCET, Pink Encounter — the luxury among perfumes, many artists use it; named from one moment at a doorway, through jokbal, garlic and sesame oil

The nose’s accountNot sprayed — transferred; settled onto the coat as if it had lived there; fruit at the front, soft floral and musk behind; not sweet-cloying; very clean

CorroborationSecond witness (reluctant, formerly calm): knows the scent from waiting rooms and stylists’ corridors; concedes it is distinctive and impression-leaving; concedes the whole evening is, therefore, a little interesting

Alternative explanations, filed and left openAn older sister; a friend; a senior; a colleague; a borrowed car; a favor repaid at close range; a few words leaned close and said

Joint finding on transfer: proximity or duration — close quarters, or no brief meeting. At the very least, not a dozen seconds in an elevator.

Exhibit withheld by pact: no whiteboard, no photograph, no red thread, no caption. The dossier remains open in two heads that have agreed, seriously, to close it tonight.

Boram looked at her once and could not help a laugh.

“That sentence of yours sounds like suspect analysis out of a crime drama. Shall I fetch a whiteboard, stick Bae Do-yoon-ssi’s photograph on it, draw a red thread across the middle, and write ‘mystery woman’s perfume’ beside it?”

Park Ji-yeon, made a little sheepish, kept her mouth hard all the same.

“I’m not that exaggerated. I am only reasoning reasonably. And you said just now that it was interesting — you can’t be curious yourself and then push the whole gossip responsibility onto me.”

Boram did not deny it this time.

She thought about it. “All right, I admit it. I am a little curious too. After all, the impression he usually gives is far too regular — the kind of person who gets up on time, goes out on time, handles his documents on time. Suddenly coming back in the middle of the night carrying such a gentle woman’s perfume on him — that gap does make one want to look twice.”

Park Ji-yeon hugged her cushion and nodded slowly.

“Yes — the gap. He doesn’t usually look like a person with any complicated private life.”

Boram looked at her.

“That sentence is a little excessive. He is merely quiet of speech; it isn’t as if he has handed his whole life to the office for safekeeping. And the more regular a person looks, the less certain it is that they are truly simple. Look at the dramas — the kind of person who wears a suit by day with a perfectly cool face, at night…”

She stopped halfway.

Park Ji-yeon lifted her head at once.

“At night what?”

Boram paused, and drew the words back in.

“At night they also go home to sleep.”

Park Ji-yeon stared at her for several seconds.

“Unnie, you absolutely wanted to say something else just now.”

Boram lifted her water glass and pretended not to hear.

“Never mind what I wanted to say just now. The point is: we have no evidence now. We smelled a little perfume. You may be curious — but you may not directly assign the man an entire storyline.”

Park Ji-yeon said, in a small voice:

“I didn’t assign an entire storyline.”

Boram looked at her.

“You didn’t? Don’t deny it. Your face cannot hide things.”

Park Ji-yeon, struck home, fell silent a moment.

She lowered her head and picked at the cushion’s edge.

“Then — what if it’s a girlfriend?”

The words out, the living room went suddenly a little quieter.

The easy gossip-feel of just now seemed lightly pressed down by those three words.

Boram looked at her and did not speak at once.

Park Ji-yeon herself seemed to realize this sentence was a different kind; her voice went lower than before.

“Actually it’s normal, isn’t it? Bae Do-yoon-ssi isn’t bad-looking, his work is good. His speech is a little cold sometimes, but he isn’t unpleasant at all. If he has a girlfriend, it isn’t strange in the least.”

Boram leaned on the sofa and listened quietly to the end.

After a while she spoke, slowly:

“It is normal. Only — you had just made it a secret. The moment girlfriend is said aloud, the secret stops being so fun.”

Park Ji-yeon lifted her head to look at her.

Boram did not laugh at her; her tone was, instead, very gentle.

“At first, when you smelled the perfume, it was like catching hold of a very interesting little thread. But if the far end of the thread is truly a girlfriend, then it will remind you: Bae Do-yoon-ssi has a life you know nothing at all about.”

Park Ji-yeon stilled; she did not take up the words at once.

Boram, watching her reaction, did not press further; she only drew the topic a little toward the lighter side.

“Of course, it may not be that complicated. An older sister, a friend, a senior, a colleague. Or merely having ridden in someone’s car. Perhaps he helped someone with something; perhaps the other person leaned close and said a few words to him.”

“A perfume smell can explain a little. It cannot explain everything.”

Park Ji-yeon said, in a low voice: “But for it to stay on cloth, the distance shouldn’t have been too far.”

Boram nodded.

“On this I agree. Either the distance was close, or the time was not short. At the very least, it isn’t as simple as standing in an elevator for a dozen seconds.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at her at once.

“See — you think so too.”

Boram, caught by the handle of her own words, laughed.

“I am only analyzing according to your description. It does not mean I have already concluded he has a secret lover.”

Park Ji-yeon could not help laughing.

“I didn’t even say secret lover.”

“You didn’t say it. It doesn’t follow that the word failed to appear in your head.”

“It didn’t.”

“Then it’s a girlfriend.”

Park Ji-yeon went quiet again.

The cushion conference · voices lowered by degrees

There is a smell on Bae Do-yoon-ssi. — Explain, or I stop listening. — Not that kind of smell. Perfume. — Perfume is normal. — Not a man’s perfume. A woman’s.

Then what if it’s a girlfriend? — (The room, suddenly a little quieter; the gossip pressed down by three words.) — It is normal. Only the secret stops being fun.

A thread, caught: interesting, small. A thread, feared: with a life none of us knows at its far end. Distance close, or time not short — agreed, then denied, then agreed again in the same breath.

Neither said secret lover aloud after that. It does not follow that the word failed to appear in either head.

Boram looked at her for a while; then she gave a light sigh, suddenly.

“Ji-yeon-ah. Your reaction right now is more obvious than the perfume smell.”

Park Ji-yeon hugged the cushion tight.

“I’m only curious.”

Boram nodded.

“I know. You are curious. But curiosity also has many kinds. Some you hear and let go. Some make you look twice, without helping it, when you meet the person the next day. You are plainly the latter kind.”

Park Ji-yeon did not rebut.

She sat hugging the cushion, her line of sight resting on the low table.

The uncleared takeout boxes still stood there; a sauce pot open, chopsticks laid across a paper napkin — the clamor of just now seemed not yet wholly dispersed from the room.

And yet in her heart a very strange feeling suddenly arose.

Just now they had chatted far too naturally — as if Bae Do-yoon’s private life ought, of course, to be the focus of their evening.

She slowly lifted her head and looked at Boram.

“Unnie.”

Boram was still thinking about that perfume smell; hearing herself called, she answered without thinking.

“What is it?”

Park Ji-yeon’s expression was somewhat strange.

She looked at Boram, then looked once more at the door, as if at last realizing where the strangeness lay.

“Unnie — are we two sick?”

Boram stilled.

“What do you mean, suddenly, that we are sick? You were analyzing perfume just now, and now you begin analyzing ourselves?”

Park Ji-yeon did not laugh; she held the cushion, and her tone went rather flat.

“Tell me — we have only known him a short while. How is it that we have begun caring about his private life?”

The sentence fell, and the expression on Boram’s face, still carrying a little laughter, slowly stopped.

She did not answer at once, because Park Ji-yeon’s sentence was far too accurate.

Yes.

How long had they known Bae Do-yoon?

Park Ji-yeon went without saying: before tonight, the words she had truly exchanged with him were few.

As for herself — she had indeed known him a few days longer than Park Ji-yeon.

He had changed a light bulb for her; she had eaten a little ganjang gejang with him.

That evening’s air had been quite comfortable: he spoke little, but reached out a hand at the moment help was needed, and never deliberately made anyone feel burdened.

Even so — this progress was too fast.

She and Park Ji-yeon had been in the entertainment world for so many years; the people they had seen were too many.

Good-looking ones, gentle-tempered ones, clever and presentable ones, ones who looked after people well, ones who said beautiful things beautifully — every kind had passed before them.

Among those people no few were excellent; no few were dazzling at a single glance.

And yet they had rarely been like this.

Rarely had they, because a man known a short while came home late, begun to care where he had been.

Rarely had they, because of a little woman’s perfume on his coat, sat in a living room seriously analyzing that perfume, analyzing distance, analyzing time — even guessing, without noticing it, whether he had a girlfriend.

Boram lowered her eyes and looked at the water glass in her hand.

The water in the glass had gone cold.

Just now she had still been warning Park Ji-yeon not to think wildly; and yet she herself had plainly thought along a great deal — even thought no less than Park Ji-yeon had.

That was the strangest place of all.

What they have seen, and how rarely they are like this

Years in the entertainment world; a whole catalogue of people: good-looking, gentle, clever and presentable, careful of others, fluent in beautiful speech. Many excellent; many dazzling at a glance. And rarely — rarely — like this.

The ledger with the neighbor is short: one light bulb changed; one evening of ganjang gejang, quiet and comfortable, a hand offered at the right moment and no burden laid on anyone; fried chicken; tonight’s knock, embarrassment, explanation, burst of laughter. A short ledger — and already a cold glass of water in her hand, and a perfume analyzed three ways.

Park Ji-yeon, seeing her silent, made her voice lighter still.

“See — you also think it’s strange, right?”

Boram lifted her head to look at her; after a few seconds she spoke, slowly:

“It is a little strange.”

“While we were chatting I didn’t feel it. Now that you say it so — it is indeed a little too much. Bae Do-yoon-ssi is only a neighbor. We don’t even know what he likes to eat, and yet we have begun discussing whose body the perfume on him came from.”

Park Ji-yeon pressed the cushion closer to her chest.

“Just now I still thought I was only curious. But thinking it over — curiosity to this degree doesn’t seem that ordinary either.”

Boram did not take up the words at once.

She leaned against the sofa back, her line of sight falling on the door.

That door stood quietly closed; at a distance beyond it was Bae Do-yoon’s room.

Plainly only a neighbor.

Plainly only a chance of living near.

And yet this person, Bae Do-yoon, seemed inexplicably to have opened a small breach in their lives.

At first only a light bulb changed.

Then ganjang gejang, fried chicken.

Then tonight’s knocking, embarrassment, explanations, burst of laughter.

And then — even a little perfume smell left on him could keep them sitting in the living room, talking until now.

Boram suddenly found it somewhat funny, and at the same time a little indescribably absurd.

“The two of us, just now — we did look rather sick. One smells a perfume and can even say its name exactly; the other, who ought to have stopped her, listens more and more seriously, and ends up joining in to analyze how close one must be for it to transfer onto cloth.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at her.

“Unnie, you’ve finally admitted it.”

Boram sighed.

“I admit it. You are sick quite obviously; I am sick quite concealably.”

Park Ji-yeon, still in a somewhat subtle mood, could not help laughing at this line.

“Is there any difference?”

“There is. You look like one who will go and ask tomorrow. I look like one who will advise you not to ask — while wanting the answer in my heart all the same.”

Symptoms, self-reported · two patients, one living room

Patient onesmelled a woman’s perfume through jokbal, garlic and sesame oil; named the brand; prognosis: will ask tomorrow

Patient twoshould have stopped the analysis; listened more seriously instead; co-analyzed distance and time; prognosis: will advise against asking, while wanting the answer

Curiosity classthe second kind: heard tonight, looked twice at tomorrow; both patients admitted to the second kind by midnight

Relapse“But Pink Encounter really is obvious.” — uttered after the containment pact; classified by the calm one as the symptom itself

Differential diagnosis considered and discarded: neighborliness. Final note: the door across the corridor is quietly closed, and both patients keep looking at it.

Park Ji-yeon’s smile grew more plain; but after laughing she lowered her head again to the cushion’s edge.

“But why?”

Boram knew what she was asking.

Why care so much about Bae Do-yoon?

Why produce so much curiosity about a person known so short a while?

Why could this person, having only stood at a doorway saying a few sentences, leave them remembering his state, his clothes, his tone — even the smell on him?

Boram thought a while, and said slowly:

“Perhaps because he appeared too suddenly, and each time happened to land on some very subtle moment. Each time it is nothing like a big matter — and each time it leaves a deep impression.”

Park Ji-yeon listened, not interrupting.

Boram went on:

“And — this person is a little strange. He speaks little; he does not approach on his own; he does not deliberately court anyone. And yet when help is needed, he does not stand by with folded hands.”

“Say he is cold — he is not truly cold. Say he is warm — he always keeps a distance. Such a person, on the contrary, makes one want to know what he is ordinarily like.”

Park Ji-yeon nodded slowly.

“It is exactly this feeling. He plainly has done nothing special — and the more so, the more one wants to see the side he doesn’t show.”

Boram looked at her once.

“That sentence of yours is more dangerous than the perfume analysis just now.”

Park Ji-yeon stilled; then she reacted, ears going a little hot.

“I was only following what you said.”

“Mm. Followed very naturally.”

“Unnie.”

Boram laughed a little and did not go on teasing her.

She set the water glass on the low table and lowered her voice.

“But your warning just now was right. We have indeed been paying a little too much attention to him.”

“Curiosity is permissible. But if we begin taking out every detail of his and turning it over repeatedly in our heads — that is not good.”

Park Ji-yeon looked toward the door.

“So we should rein it in?”

“At least rein it in tonight.”

Having said it, Boram felt the sentence itself a little over-explanatory, and cleared her throat lightly.

“What I mean is: no more analyzing the perfume. The more we analyze, the more it looks as if we truly mean to solve a case.”

Park Ji-yeon nodded seriously.

“Mm. No more analyzing.”

The two were quiet for a few seconds.

Park Ji-yeon suddenly said, in a small voice:

“But Pink Encounter really is obvious.”

Boram closed her eyes.

“Ji-yeon.”

Park Ji-yeon hugged the cushion tight at once.

“Last one sentence.”

Boram looked at her, endured, endured — and still could not hold the laugh in.

“See. This is the symptom.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed out as well.

They did, indeed, care about Bae Do-yoon too much.

The containment pact · living room, past midnight, breached within seconds

I. No further analysis of the perfume tonight. Ratified by both patients; breached by patient one within seconds (“But Pink Encounter really is obvious”), breach classified by patient two as the symptom itself, and forgiven in laughter.

II. Tomorrow, if they meet him: act as if nothing is known. Supplementary clause, added at once: and do not put on any strange expression either. Patient one’s undertaking: I’ll try. Patient two’s review of that undertaking: your answer worries me more.

III. The whiteboard clause: no whiteboard, no photograph, no red thread, no caption reading mystery woman’s perfume. Status: not breached tonight; not promised for tomorrow.

Signed over a cold water glass and a stack of folded takeout boxes, by one patient sick obviously and one sick concealably.

The pact’s true object, unstated in its text: not the perfume. The thread — which by the signing had already wound itself, quietly, around them both.

Boram stood up and began clearing the low table.

“All right — let’s clear up first. If we keep talking, tomorrow the two of us will feel guilty the moment we see Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

Park Ji-yeon stood too, and stacked the takeout boxes one by one.

“Why feel guilty? We didn’t do anything.”

Boram looked at her once.

“You analyzed, three times over, how the perfume on the man got there.”

Park Ji-yeon paused.

“You analyzed it too.”

“Which is why I said the two of us.”

Park Ji-yeon was silent two seconds, and nodded.

“All right. The two of us.”

Boram lifted the trash bag, tone very light.

“Tomorrow, if we really run into him, we act as if we know nothing.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at her without thinking.

Boram added at once:

“And no putting on strange expressions either.”

Park Ji-yeon pressed her lips.

“I’ll try.”

“That answer of yours worries me more.”

Park Ji-yeon lowered her head and went on clearing, but the corners of her mouth curved a little all the same.

The sound of water soon rose in the kitchen.

In the living room the low table was gradually cleared; the smell of jokbal and sauce thinned away too.

After · the cleared low table, the quiet door

One door across the corridor, quiet as if nothing had happened

A clean, soft scent remaining in one head like a small thread, caught by chance

Park Ji-yeon stood where she was, and could not help looking once more toward the door.

That door was quiet, as if nothing at all had happened. And yet in her head that faint scent still remained.

Clean, soft — like a small thread she had caught hold of without meaning to.

She had only meant to pick it up and look at it once.

And then Boram had reached out and touched it too.

And so the two of them discovered, at the same moment, that the thread seemed already to have wound itself, quietly, around them.

You are sick quite obviously; I am sick quite concealably.

On threads, and on the hands that touch them

A scent through a doorway is not evidence; it is an invitation. They knew this — the calm one said it outright, glass in hand, water going cold: you may be curious, but you may not assign a man an entire storyline. And then they assigned distance, duration, brand and top notes, and stopped one word short of girlfriend because the room went quiet at the sound of it. The question that finally landed was not whose perfume it was. It was: we have only known him a short while — how is it we are like this? No answer was filed tonight. Only a pact, breached in seconds, and a thread neither will put down, wound twice around two wrists that both swore, seriously, to rein it in tonight.
