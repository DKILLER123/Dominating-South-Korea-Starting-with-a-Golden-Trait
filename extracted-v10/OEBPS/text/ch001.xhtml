Chapter One

Favor of Rich Women (Gold) and Idol Killer (Purple)

Morning, fog not yet lifted

The Shilla · a suite on the hillside

A parallel world, and one enormous building that owns its own slope

Seoul, in a world that was not the one he knew.

The morning fog had not finished burning off, and out on the hillside a vast building stood very quietly in the middle of it.

The Shilla.

Bae Do-yoon was lying on his side.

The first thing he saw when he opened his eyes was a back: pale, slender, unguarded.

The woman had her back to him. Brown hair lay loose across the pillow. Her body rose and fell with a steady breath, and by the look of it she had not woken yet.

Bae Do-yoon watched her for a moment.

What the night had left behind on her skin was impossible to miss.

He looked away, sat up slowly, and when he lifted the duvet his hand stopped by itself.

On the white sheet, one bright red mark had opened in silence, like a plum blossom that had gone and flowered in the middle of snow.

Bae Do-yoon looked down at it for a long time.

In the end he sighed.

It took being born twice to make a grown man of him. In his previous life he had died a virgin.

Both secrets were useless to him and priceless to anybody who found out. That is the whole difficulty of a secret.

The first one: he is a reincarnator.

The previous life, told in one breath

In his first life Bae Do-yoon had been air force. Not the service with jets and a flag — the other one. The word for a man no woman had ever been in the way of.

And how had he gone? Casting a fishing line under a high-voltage power line, which is the sort of decision only a man with no history of being kissed makes. The line, the wire, and him: all three went at once.

He woke up here.

The place he woke up in was not China. Not Japan. Not Europe, not America.

Korea.

How those novels open

Reborn on the Peninsula, and never had a sweet little romance with an idol? Then the rebirth was a waste of a good life.

For a certain kind of reader this is not a joke. It is a statement of working conditions.

Bae Do-yoon had taken it as one.

He stood in front of a mirror and looked the new body over.

Red lips, white teeth. Clean brows, bright eyes. Shoulders wide, legs long, and a frame that made a cheap shirt look like it had been chosen.

Put him in a crowd and the crowd found him first.

A face like this and you don’t at least try the entertainment industry? That’s not humility, that’s waste.

Bae Do-yoon

Prosecutor, one week old at the desk

Seoul National University · College of Law

Woke up in this life with two secrets and one plan he never got to use

The face was supposed to be the whole strategy. It turned out to be the reason somebody decided he was worth the trouble of raising.

And so he had made a plan, the way you do when you are young and the world has not yet introduced itself.

Finish high school. Walk into SM Entertainment. Debut. Along the way, date one of the girl-group girls he had so far only seen through a screen, and live out the life his first one had only ever let him fantasize about.

The dream had barely stood up on its own legs before reality kicked it over.

He never reached the front gate of SM Entertainment. Somebody grabbed him first.

That was his first real lesson in what power means in this country.

No explanation. No small talk. Two options.

Chopped up and fed to the fish is, at least, a kind of career acceleration.

Bae Do-yoon was not a coward.

A man who has already cast a line under a live wire has, on some technical reading of the phrase, put life and death behind him.

But not being afraid of death is not the same as wanting it.

And nobody knows how much a pulse is worth quite the way somebody who lost one does.

So he compromised.

The Korean entertainment dream went into a drawer, labeled later. The prosecutor’s route was switched on, officially.

Yesterday had been the day he cleared the last assessment and became a prosecutor.

And the mystery person had said: I’m sending you a gift.

The gift was the woman currently in his bed. A girl-group name with real weight behind it, a former member of Girls’ Generation.

Bae Do-yoon knew that a gift shaped like that is never only a gift.

Sure enough. The woman had not been the only thing sent.

The reach of the hand

Everything below was already arranged before he woke up.

Hotels — a floor that knew his name and asked for none.

University — a record that followed him cleanly, a little too cleanly, from year one.

Airport — a counter that had his seat before he did.

Taxis — one driver, one question, one smile in the mirror.

His phone — every search he had ever typed, every call he never picked up, all of it read by somebody.

He had tried to run. Late last night, before any of it was consummated, he had put a cab on the airport road, planning to leave Seoul and — if the ticket behaved — the country.

Halfway out of the city, something felt wrong.

“Prosecutor Bae — Incheon Airport, or back to the Shilla?”

The driver had said it through the rear-view mirror, pleasantly, the way a man asks about the weather.

The mystery person’s hand was everywhere.

In this world Bae Do-yoon had no relatives, no backing, no one whose name would make a hallway change its mind.

He looked free.

From the day I woke up in this body I have been walking on one string, and I have never once seen the hand holding it.

He was not a student who had been lucky. He was an object that had been chosen — polished, wrapped, set down on a shelf, waiting for its owner to unbox it with their own hands.

Bae Do-yoon got up and went into the bathroom.

The man in the mirror was young and handsome, and there was nothing in his eyes of the swagger a first day at the office ought to leave behind.

He turned the tap on, put his face under it, and let the cold water run down his cheeks.

Then he lifted his head, looked at himself, and laughed once, short, at nothing in particular.

The first entry he ever received was gold.

It is still the one that makes the back of his scalp go cold.

Entry · Favor of Rich Women

GradeGold

EffectHe always draws the goodwill and the attention of wealthy women.

ScalingOlder, higher in rank, more controlling: the interest is stronger.

InstinctThey consider him worth cultivating · worth collecting · worth betting on.

Note: this is not necessarily a good thing. To be favored by rich women can mean you do not even get to keep your own plan for your life.

The first time he read it, Bae Do-yoon had assumed the ceiling of his life had just been taken off.

Because who, being young, has not fantasized about skipping a few decades of struggle?

A rich woman.

A mansion.

A sports car.

A black card.

One sentence is enough to keep an ordinary man lucid for three seconds at two in the morning.

The line everyone quotes

Little brother, if you are tired of trying so hard — come with unnie.

Three seconds of clarity, then the alarm goes off and he is back to being a student with a laundry bill.

Bae Do-yoon was, at that age, exactly one of those men.

It costs nothing to dream in somebody else’s currency.

It took him years to notice that the weight of the entry does not sit on the word rich.

It sits on the note. The last line.

Looking back now, the reason a mystery person picked him out of a crowd of students almost certainly had that gold line written all over it.

There was only one thing he could never get straight.

Since arriving in this world he had met very few people at all.

The director of the orphanage. His school teachers. The occasional well-dressed stranger who came to hand out scholarships for a photograph. After that, the professors at Seoul National University, and then the people who ran his prosecutor track.

Among those names, not one of them fits the description rich woman.

Not in his memory.

And that is the frightening part. If the woman never once appeared in front of me and had already decided the shape of my life, then her position and her reach are worse than anything I have been assuming.

Which was also why he had never been naive enough to think that getting into SNU law and taking a prosecutor’s seat had been a whim.

Over the years, other entries had come.

When he passed into Seoul National University’s law faculty, a blue line appeared.

Useful. Perhaps more useful than the gold one, in a country that reads a university name before it reads a face.

The same sentence out of an ordinary mouth gets heard as complaining.

The same sentence out of the mouth of a Seoul National University law student gets heard as an opinion, and somebody takes notes.

Later, when he was actually inside the justice system and had passed the prosecutor’s exam, another line arrived.

Entry · Judicial Monster

GradePurple

EffectExtreme sensitivity to statutes, evidence chains, the logic of a trial, and holes in procedure.

In practiceWorking a case, he is likelier to catch the point everyone else walked past.

CostHe begins to read people as argument. His ability to feel things, and to say them, declines.

That purple line had been stronger than he expected.

Going through a case file, he could pull a contradiction out of a sentence three witnesses had agreed on.

Watching surveillance, he noticed — without deciding to — where a suspect paused, where the eyes went, which way the shoulders were pointed when no question was being asked.

Listening to a person talk, his brain took the sentence apart on its own: motive, gap, and how much of it would survive being written down.

At first he had been delighted. Inside the justice system, that is what a man calls cheating.

Then he got around to the cost.

It had become hard to simply believe somebody.

A person in front of him could cry, and before the sympathy arrived a courteous little voice asked whether the crying was a performance.

A person could rage, and the same voice asked what the rage was for.

A person could say something tender to him, and his brain would sort it, patiently, into three boxes — testimony, bait, or consideration? Then it would go back to the conversation as if nothing had asked it anything.

So an entry is not a gift. Not all of them. Some are an invoice with your name spelled correctly on it.

And after one night, a new line had appeared.

Note: where the stage lights do not reach is usually where the hunt actually happens.

Bae Do-yoon stared at it for a long time and said nothing.

Five years earlier — even two — he would have called this the dream entry.

Idol Killer. What an absurdly handsome thing to be.

Dropped into the story he had originally planned for himself, right after the rebirth, this would have been a god-tier cheat built to order for a Korean entertainment power fantasy.

Now it landed on him with about half the joy it deserved.

He was a disgrace to his profession. Every man who comes back from death is handed one clean start, and his first woman had been arranged for him by a stranger, under coercion, in a room he had not chosen.

With a beginning this sorry, I can’t even do the usual thing: walk into a room, be doubted, be admired ten minutes later.

He left the bathroom. On the way out he looked at the woman in the bed one more time, and then he left the presidential suite.

It was not that he intended to pull his trousers up and pretend she had not been there.

It was that he had something on the schedule that mattered more than dignity.

Today he was going to meet the mystery person in person.

Which meant the identity behind all of it was about to stop being a rumor.

A note on the colours

White, green, blue, purple, gold, red — the serial uses the grading of a gacha game and expects the reader to react exactly as a man reacts to a pull he cannot refuse. Nothing here is decoration: every entry printed in this book was written because it has to be paid for later.

The purple line he just received is not a punchline either. It is the sound of a door being left open in a house with only one exit.
