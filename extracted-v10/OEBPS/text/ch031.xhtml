Chapter Thirty-One

A Spring Night’s Dream

Night, minutes later · inside the arranged room

Floor lamp only; warm light pooling on carpet and sofa edge; the door’s soft click still hanging in the air

A slow sweep: low table, tall window, curtain half-drawn — and on the distant sofa, a slender figure seated with its back to him

On the distant sofa sat a slender figure.

One of her hands rested on her knee, fingertips lightly holding her cuff — a very small motion, and yet as if pressing down emotion.

The night outside the floor-to-ceiling window reflected on the glass, faintly lighting a little of her profile: nose bridge fine and straight, jawline clear. The whole person seemed wrapped together by the night and the lamplight — cool, and fragile.

Bae Do-yoon remained at the threshold, not walking in at once.

By the back and the hair alone, he had already recognized her.

Jung Soo-yeon.

On the stage she was Jessica. In this room, unavoidably, she was Jung Soo-yeon.

Bae Do-yoon’s brow drew together, slowly.

What does Lee Boo-jin mean?

Why has she sent Jung Soo-yeon in front of him again?

The last matter is not yet wholly sorted out, and now this play again.

Standing at the doorway, Bae Do-yoon felt for a while an indescribable strangeness in his heart. He was truly beginning not to understand Lee Boo-jin.

By Lee Boo-jin’s usual style, when she wanted him to do something she would speak half-bright, half-dark: give a little intelligence, leave a little margin, make a person know clearly that they were being led by the nose and yet have to admit that what she gave was indeed useful.

But this time was different.

She had not made him do anything; had given him no clear task; had certainly not made him actively leave any handle behind.

She had only let the assistant bring him to this room, and left Jung Soo-yeon here.

On the surface it looked like purely sending this woman in front of him.

Which only made Bae Do-yoon more wary.

When has Lee Boo-jin ever done anything this simple?

This woman is bad — thoroughly bad.

Bae Do-yoon stopped in place a few seconds, then gave a light sigh.

He had come all this way; he could not stand at the doorway staring at a back forever.

He walked over.

Hearing the footsteps, the woman on the sofa finally reacted.

Jessica lifted her head slowly and looked at him.

Their sightlines met in mid-air, touching once.

Her eyes were very beautiful, the outer corners lifted faintly; even now, carrying wine, they still held a kind of cool, clean sharpness. Only there was not much wave in those eyes: calm, cold, neither sorrow nor joy — and even a little numbness.

Of course, Bae Do-yoon was also very clear: in them there was also dislike, directed at him.

On the tea table before the sofa stood a bottle of red wine. The bottle was already more than half empty; plainly she had drunk a good deal alone before he came. A flush lay on Jessica’s cheeks. That wine softened her originally rather cool air a little — and also made her look several parts more tired.

Bae Do-yoon stood before her and looked her over a few times, making no attempt to hide it.

Jessica did not avoid him either; she tilted her head up and looked at him, gaze flat, as if already too lazy to make any reaction to his sight at all.

Bae Do-yoon had to admit: this former member of Girls’ Generation was indeed very beautiful. In figure or in face, one could not pick out too many faults. Form slender, features fine, nose bridge straight, lip color dyed a shallow brightness by the red wine.

Iceberg princess.

Cat-type girl.

Set against those nicknames, the Jessica in front of him was plainly not easy to deal with.

The room’s two costumes · what was worn, what was removed

Hers, on arrival an elegant dark blouse, sleeves long enough to hold a cuff between fingertips; pressed, like everything else about her

Hers, later the coat taken off without explanation and tossed to the sofa armrest — a declaration, not a disrobing

Hers, morning the quilt, claimed to the chin; the only garment in the room still on anybody’s side

His a coat picked up for leaving, bent down for, and laid back beside her instead; worn out of the room only in the version of the night that did not happen

Also on record: one wine-flush on her cheeks, one lip color dyed a shallow brightness, and one coat on an armrest that neither of them will mention again.

The expression on Jessica’s face did not change much. To her, Bae Do-yoon remained always a very strange existence.

Apart from that one night, she had almost no complete impression of Bae Do-yoon. She did not know his origin, did not know his identity, did not know what kind of person he was on ordinary days. And yet precisely such a person had with her the one intersection least capable of being lightly described.

Because of it, seeing Bae Do-yoon produced in her heart an indescribable awkwardness.

He was like a reminder: standing there was enough to make her recall what kind of choice she had once made.

She did not regret it, and was not so fragile as to sink repeatedly into that bit of emotion. Adults must always make decisions for reality; she had understood this principle long ago. But understanding was understanding; seeing Bae Do-yoon again, there was still a little discomfort in her heart.

This man in front of her had seen the side of her she least wanted remembered — the most embarrassing side.

So when she looked at Bae Do-yoon, her gaze was always a little colder than toward other people: as if reminding herself, and as if reminding him, that some things having happened did not mean she was willing to let them become matter-of-course.

Jung Soo-yeon did not like falling to the lower wind before anyone — even if this person had once been the first man in her life.

She could not, in truth, say clearly what feeling she had toward Bae Do-yoon. Dislike was not the word; like was even more impossible. She could not speak with him naturally, and could not wholly push him into an unimportant position either. After all, some memories do not disappear because of one transaction; and some people do not, merely because they do not know each other, really retreat to the distance of strangers.

Looking at Bae Do-yoon, a very faint thread of irritation even crossed her heart. Irritation at Lee Boo-jin; irritation at why this person in front of her had to appear here again.

She had thought that once that night passed, the matter should be ended. The price had been paid, the exchange completed, each taking away what each wanted — only that was like Lee Boo-jin’s style. But now Bae Do-yoon had been sent in front of her again. It produced in her heart a displeasure, like having plainly already left the card table and being pressed back into her old seat.

And what irritated her most was not even here.

She discovered that, seeing Bae Do-yoon, she still instinctively paid attention to him. Attended to his expression; attended to his gaze. This instinct made her more displeased still, because it meant that Bae Do-yoon, with her, was after all different from other people.

She did not like admitting this.

So she could only press all the emotion back — press it into those clear, cold eyes, press it into a calm that would not grant one extra sentence. As long as she was cool enough, composed enough, many things could still maintain their surface decency.

Jessica, looked at by him until her heart grew irritable, finally could not help lifting her eyes, and said in a deep voice:

“What are you looking at? Is it very good-looking?”

Hearing this, Bae Do-yoon actually laughed a little.

He was not frightened off by the cold in her tone; rather, he nodded very honestly.

“It is quite good-looking.”

Jessica’s gaze grew colder.

Bae Do-yoon went on: “Much better-looking than on television. Original flavor, unprocessed.”

An expression of disgust appeared on Jessica’s face at once.

“Disgusting.”

The two characters were said without the least politeness; Bae Do-yoon, however, did not mind at all.

He reached over and took the other clean red-wine glass from the tea table, pouring himself a little red wine. The deep-red liquid swirled open along the glass wall, floating a dim red light under the lamp.

Jessica watched him with cold eyes. She would like to see how long this man intended to keep up his act.

The next second, Bae Do-yoon lifted the glass and drank a mouthful — and his expression suddenly froze. His brow drew together; there even appeared a bit of grimace.

Jessica was faintly stunned.

Bae Do-yoon set the glass down, lowered his head to glance at the red wine in it, his tone full of disdain.

“This red wine is truly awful…”

Jessica: “……”

The coolness on her face very nearly failed to hold.

She had thought: a person whom Lee Boo-jin arranges into this kind of setup must at least be a man used to high-end occasions — one who knows wine, knows women, knows how to gauge measure. The result: Bae Do-yoon drinks one mouthful of red wine with an expression as if he had drunk some poison.

Jessica looked at him, her brow-tip lifting lightly.

Bae Do-yoon noticed; he looked at her. “What?”

Jessica said nothing; she only took up the bottle and refilled her own glass a little more. Then she lifted the glass and, before Bae Do-yoon’s face, drained it in one gulp. The motion clean and decisive — as if telling him: the wine is fine; what is problematic is you, the man who cannot drink red wine.

Bae Do-yoon watched her finish, was not embarrassed either, and only smiled faintly.

“Your tolerance is good. Better than mine.”

Jessica put the glass back on the tea table and glanced at him coldly.

“Did you come here just to say this kind of nonsense?”

She spoke without the least politeness, because Bae Do-yoon’s easy attitude made that bit of pressed irritation in her heart surge up again. He seemed to know nothing, and seemed to care about nothing. And what made Jessica most uncomfortable was: the more he was like this, the less she could judge whether tonight’s meeting was his own intent or Lee Boo-jin’s arrangement.

Bae Do-yoon leaned in the sofa, swirling in his hand that glass of red wine he had disdained so thoroughly; the smile on his face faded a little.

“Then you?”

“Why are you here again? Did you come here only to drink?”

Jessica fell silent.

Bae Do-yoon’s sentence had struck exactly the place she had never been willing to bring up.

Of course she was not here to drink. Even having drunk more than half a bottle of red wine just now was only to make herself less sober in this room.

The intersection neither will name · carried into the room by both

That one night: the price paid, the exchange completed, each taking away what each wanted — only that is like Lee Boo-jin’s style, and only that was supposed to be the end of it.

He remains, to her, a reminder standing: recall what kind of choice you once made. She does not regret; she will not sink. But some memories do not disappear because of one transaction, and some people do not retreat to stranger-distance merely because they never knew each other.

The first man in her life — and the one before whom she will not fall to the lower wind, not even by a glance.

Tonight the card table, plainly left, has her pressed back into her old seat; and her own instinct, attending to his expression and his gaze, testifies against her better than any witness could.

What Lee Boo-jin had promised her had been done. Those troubles coiled around her were pressed down; the crisis over her brand temporarily eased; her sister returned to the stage again. Everything that had originally pressed her to the point of not breathing seemed forced back beneath the water’s surface by an invisible hand.

She had thought the matter ended there.

But two days ago, Lee Boo-jin’s assistant found her again.

It was a choice already laid in front of her, almost not permitting refusal.

What could she say? Say that Lee Boo-jin’s assistant had come to her two days ago. Say that the other had laid a new set of terms in front of her. Say that from now on, as long as she agreed to Lee Boo-jin’s terms, she would become one of Lee Boo-jin’s people.

Lee Boo-jin would settle for her the troubles that might appear in future. Whether crises over her brand, disputes over contracts, attacks of public opinion, or obstacles that might arise again on her sister’s side — as long as it lay within Lee Boo-jin’s reach, she would act.

This sounded like protection. But Jung Soo-yeon was very clear: in the world there is never protection without reason.

What Lee Boo-jin wanted was also simple.

Obedience.

From now on, whatever Lee Boo-jin made Jung Soo-yeon do, Jung Soo-yeon would have to do.

Of course, the assistant put it very decently.

“Ms. Jung — the President will not make you keep company with messy people.”

The assistant had spoken very clearly, as if removing her concerns for her.

“You need not worry about those low-class occasions, nor about being casually humiliated. What the President wants is your cooperation when it is needed in the future — not to throw you to some third-rate person.”

Jung Soo-yeon had only looked at her then, and said nothing.

The assistant paused, and added one more sentence.

“Of course — except for him.”

It was precisely this sentence that made Jung Soo-yeon go a little cold.

Lee Boo-jin would not push her onto others. Would not make her face those occasions she loathed — filthy, disgusting, vomit-inducing. But he was not inside that range. He was the exception.

Jung Soo-yeon had sat there then, and there had been a very long struggle in her heart.

She had not failed to think of refusing. Even at the moment the assistant finished speaking, her heart’s first thought was to stand up and leave. She did not want to be arranged again. Did not want to be like a chess piece that can be set on the table at any time. Even less did she want her name, her body, her choices all to become chips in other people’s negotiations.

But those materials lay right in front of her.

The materials on the table · the threat that needed no raised voice

01Her sister’s schedule, itemized

02The arrangements for her sister’s return to the stage, drafted

03Several collaborations, near confirmation

04Traces of her own brand side already being watched by people

Verdict, reached in silence: the documents on the table are the threat, and also the reality. She can gamble one breath for herself; she cannot gamble with her sister — her sister only just back on the stage, only just out of those messy storms. Signature executed without tears, and without a pen.

Looking at those things, Jung Soo-yeon suddenly realized: Lee Boo-jin did not need to threaten her loudly at all. Those documents laid on the table were the threat, and were the reality.

She could gamble one breath for herself. She could not take her sister to gamble.

Her sister had only just returned to the stage, only just walked out of those messy storms. She could not let her sister be dragged down again.

So in the end, Jung Soo-yeon agreed.

After the assistant left, she sat alone a very long time, and did not shed one tear. Crying was useless; regretting was useless. She had long passed the age of escaping reality by collapse. Only afterward did she suddenly feel very tired, as if one more invisible thread had been added to her body. The other end of that thread was held in Lee Boo-jin’s hand.

And tonight was the first command she received.

Come to the Shilla Hotel. Enter this room. No further explanation, and no room given for refusal.

Comparative assessment: this feeling is worse than being directly humiliated. Filed by her, silently, under the first command.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, waited a moment, and then opened his mouth to ask:

“What terms did Lee Boo-jin actually give you?”

Jessica’s gaze moved faintly. She lifted her head; the emotion on her face had already been gathered back very clean.

“That is none of your business.”

Bae Do-yoon watched her, a faint line forming between his brows. So she really had reached some transaction with Lee Boo-jin. And this transaction was by no means simple.

Jessica did not like Bae Do-yoon looking at her with that kind of gaze — like sympathy, and like already seeing through her situation. It made that bit of forcibly propped decency in her heart suddenly become very humble. She would rather Bae Do-yoon asked nothing, as last time, than be looked at by him quietly like this. It made her feel herself very wretched, very laughable — like someone who has plainly already placed herself on the transaction table and yet desperately pretends she has not lost.

The next second, Jessica suddenly stood up. The motion was somewhat abrupt. Bae Do-yoon raised his gaze to her as she rose.

Jessica did not explain; she only reached out, took off the coat on her body, and tossed it casually onto the sofa armrest beside her. She looked straight at Bae Do-yoon, her voice very cold.

“Hurry up and end it.”

Bae Do-yoon frowned faintly. “End what?”

A thread of mockery flashed in Jessica’s eyes.

“Last time, weren’t you quite good at tearing my clothes? What — now you’re putting on pure-love airs?”

Bae Do-yoon finally understood her meaning; looking at Jessica, the confusion in his eyes turned slowly into stunned shock.

She stood there as if she had already made every preparation. But Bae Do-yoon could see: she was not indifferent at all. She had only driven herself to the cliff’s edge, using the most thorned words to block off in advance every warmth, hesitation, and explanation that might appear.

Bae Do-yoon held his silence a few seconds, then suddenly asked: “Is this Lee Boo-jin making you do it?”

Jessica’s gaze moved imperceptibly. But quickly she gave another cold laugh.

“Does it matter?”

“It matters.” Bae Do-yoon’s voice sank.

Jessica looked at him, tone cold: “She made me come here and wait for you.”

Bae Do-yoon pressed: “And then?”

Jessica did not answer.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “She explicitly made you do this?”

Jessica’s lips pressed together.

This minute reaction was already enough to explain many things.

Lee Boo-jin had not put the words that plainly. She had only made Jung Soo-yeon come, arranging tonight as the so-called first command. The remaining part was left for Jung Soo-yeon herself to understand. This was the most disgusting place: she had not said it clearly, and yet made a person have no choice but to think in that direction.

Bae Do-yoon slowly let out a breath. He suddenly felt that this cup of red wine was not merely awful — even the air in the room had become somewhat uncomfortable.

Jessica, seeing him not speak, let the mockery on her face grow more obvious.

“What? Now you’re starting to find it troublesome again?”

“Or do you only like it when others arrange everything for you, and you yourself pretend to know nothing?”

Bae Do-yoon did not grow angry at this sentence; he asked one sentence:

“Are you Jessica?”

Jessica’s expression stiffened faintly.

This name, from Bae Do-yoon’s mouth, seemed to drag her briefly out of this room and back onto the stage. Jessica. That name standing under the lights, watched by countless people, remembered by fans, chased by cameras. That proud, beautiful, cool, decent name. But at this moment she stood in a hotel room, her coat tossed aside, facing a man she did not truly know, urging him in the coldest, hardest words to hurry up and end it. The two images overlapped — ironic to the point of breathlessness.

Jessica’s gaze went cold at once.

“So what?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, his tone carrying real incomprehension.

“Do you really listen to that woman in everything?”

He found it unthinkable. She had seen so many scenes, lived through so many storms; she was not someone who had never climbed up from the valley. How could such a person be pressed by Lee Boo-jin to this degree?

Jessica understood the meaning in his words — and precisely because she understood, an indescribable stabbing pain suddenly surged in her heart. She stared at Bae Do-yoon.

“So what if I’m Jessica?”

Jessica walked forward one step. She looked at him; the emotion in the bottom of her eyes was finally no longer wholly calm.

“Doesn’t Jessica have troubles?”

“Doesn’t Jessica get caught by her weak points?”

“Can Jessica solve everything, need nothing from anyone, shoulder everything past by herself?”

Bae Do-yoon watched her quietly.

Jessica seemed to realize she had said too much, and suddenly stopped. She had not, in truth, wanted to say these things; they were too wretched, like defending her own compromise. She hated this feeling. But Bae Do-yoon had still torn open, at one stroke, the place pressed long in her heart.

Yes. She was Jessica. So she must not fall, the more; must not let others see her panic, the more. Must not let her sister know what kind of terms she had agreed to, so that those troubles would not fall on them again.

Jessica turned her face away; her voice returned to cold.

“I agreed to Lee Boo-jin’s terms.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

Jessica did not look at him; she only went on: “From now on, whatever she wants me to do, I will listen. This is my own choice.”

Bae Do-yoon said, lightly: “You’re sure that’s called a choice?”

Jessica turned her head to look at him, gaze very cold.

“Then what is it called?”

Bae Do-yoon did not answer.

Jessica said it on for him.

“Called forced-with-no-alternative? Called a transaction? Called selling off a part of one’s own freedom?”

She laughed a little; the smile very faint, and very self-mocking.

“Interpret it however you like.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her and suddenly felt his chest somewhat tight.

Jessica, however, seemed already unwilling to continue this dialogue. She stood there, her tone turning sharp again.

“So don’t waste time.”

“Anyway, for you it isn’t the first time.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her a long while, then suddenly gave a light sigh.

“Truly no meaning.”

Jessica’s gaze moved faintly.

Bae Do-yoon, however, did not go on; he only bent down, took up the coat draped on the sofa armrest, and laid it back beside her, saying in a light tone:

“I’ll leave first.”

Jessica was stunned.

“You stay here tonight,” Bae Do-yoon said. “I won’t do anything to you.”

The words fell, and he stepped toward the door.

Jessica stood in place, watching his back, her fingers tightening bit by bit.

At first she only felt absurdity.

Then that emotion pressed dead-hard by her suddenly surged up. Shame, anger, embarrassment, unresignation — and a kind of grievance even she herself could not name clearly, as if surging to her chest all at once.

He says leave, and leaves. He says he won’t do anything to her. He says truly no meaning.

These words plainly sounded like sparing her; but falling in Jessica’s ears, they grated more harshly than any plain humiliation. She had already driven herself to this step, stepped her pride under her foot, made every preparation to face everything. But Bae Do-yoon withdrew lightly, as if all her cold hardness just now, all her resolve, all her wretchedness, were only one boring performance.

On what grounds?

On what grounds does he look at her like this?

On what grounds does he leave her alone in place, with that attitude like pity and like disdain?

Jessica’s chest heaved once.

The next second, she suddenly raised her voice.

“You — stand still!”

The voice was very loud.

Bae Do-yoon’s step halted; instinctively he turned his head to look at her. But he had just turned when Jessica was already striding up. Her motion was fast, even somewhat out of control. Bae Do-yoon had no time to open his mouth before she had seized his collar in one hand, risen on tiptoe, and kissed him fiercely.

It was not like a kiss. It was more like revenge.

Bae Do-yoon’s pupils contracted faintly. The next second, a stabbing pain came from his lip. Jung Soo-yeon had bitten him. Whether deliberately, or without holding back her force in the loss of control, Bae Do-yoon only felt a heat on his lip, and then tasted a little faint bloodiness. His gaze sank in an instant.

Jessica released him, breath somewhat disordered, but still lifting her chin to look at him. The rims of her eyes were somewhat red, yet her expression remained stubborn — like one who would rather crash herself head-broken and blood-flowing than lower her head at all.

Bae Do-yoon lifted a hand and wiped his lip corner; a little blood on his fingerpad. He lowered his head and glanced at it.

“Jung Soo-yeon!”

Jessica gave a cold laugh. “What? Now you’re not putting on airs again?”

Bae Do-yoon stared at her, the emotion in the bottom of his eyes pressing down bit by bit.

“What exactly do you want to do?”

Jessica did not answer.

In this moment, all the pretended calm between the two of them was torn open. She was not unafraid, nor indifferent; she was only too tired. Tired to the point of not wanting to explain again, not wanting to maintain decency again, not wanting to be pushed forward again by Lee Boo-jin, by the transaction, by the so-called command. But Bae Do-yoon wanted to leave. He wanted to stop all of this here, and leave her alone to face this absurdity. This was more unacceptable to her than anything happening.

Bae Do-yoon looked into her eyes; that anger pressed long in his chest finally surged up. He was angry at Lee Boo-jin, angry at this arranged setup, and angrier still at Jung Soo-yeon for dragging him back into this chaos in this near-self-harming way.

He suddenly reached out and clamped her wrist. Jessica’s body stiffened, but did not retreat. Bae Do-yoon’s voice was very deep:

“Think it through clearly.”

Jessica stared at him; in the bottom of her eyes a layer of flame seemed to burn.

“I’m very clear.”

Bae Do-yoon did not move at once, as if giving her one last chance to regret. But Jessica did not avoid his gaze. She even advanced half a step, her voice pressed very low.

“Don’t make yourself sound so innocent.”

This sentence was like the last fuse; Bae Do-yoon’s gaze darkened thoroughly. The next second he drew her close. Cloth gave a slight tearing sound in the pulling. Jessica’s breath caught, but she did not break free.

The night color outside the window was deep. The light in the room swayed once; the floor lamp cast overlapping shadows on the wall. Night-sound vast. Spring-song long. This night, no one mentioned Lee Boo-jin again, and no one mentioned the so-called command. As if all people were briefly shut outside the door, only the two of them left — in chaos, anger, and unresignation, pulling at each other, sinking with each other.

On exceptions, and on names said aloud

A protection clause is only ever as wide as its exception: no low occasions, no humiliation, no third-rate persons — of course, except for him, and the whole decency of the sentence rearranges itself around one man in one room. She signed without a pen and without a tear, because the documents on the table were the threat and the reality, and because a sister only just back on the stage is not a chip. And he, who would not take the case for a reward and would not take the position for trust, found the one setup he could not read: no task, no handle, no half-bright words — only a door, a floor lamp, and a woman pressing her cuff between her fingertips so that nothing in her would show. She offered him the coldest possible reading of the night (hurry up and end it) so that no warmer one could reach her first; he refused it by laying her coat back beside her and walking toward the door, and discovered too late that being spared can insult more deeply than being taken. What followed was not tenderness and will not be called love: it was two people who had each been placed on someone else’s table refusing, in the only language left to them, to be the only one embarrassed. In the morning an alarm rings on the wrong side of the bed, and the first sentence of the day is a complaint about the alarm — which is, of all the sentences spoken in that room, the only one neither of them will need to take back.
