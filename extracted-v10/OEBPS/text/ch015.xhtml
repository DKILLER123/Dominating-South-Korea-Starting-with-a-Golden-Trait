Chapter Fifteen

The Officer’s Discovery, Bae Suzy’s Anomaly

Still the same afternoon · questions finished

JYP meeting room · the moment before leaving

One invitation, and one investigative officer’s face betraying her

Bae Do-yoon judged there was nothing more worth asking, and stood up. “That is enough for today.”

Kim Mi-ju immediately set her records in order. Park Dae-jun rose with them: “Prosecutor — whatever material you need going forward, I will prepare it as fast as I can.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded, said nothing more, took up the files, and prepared to leave.

But just then, Park Jin-young suddenly spoke and stopped him.

“Prosecutor Bae.”

Bae Do-yoon halted and turned to look at him.

The seriousness on Park Jin-young’s face had thinned a little; his tone came a degree lighter than before.

“This must be your first time at JYP, I take it?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “That’s right. My first time at JYP.”

Park Jin-young smiled.

“Since you’ve come, don’t rush off. How about a look around?”

Bae Do-yoon did not answer at once.

By his habits: the questions asked, the material confirmed, one goes back to organizing the investigation’s direction. Touring an entertainment company was not in his plan. In an earlier life he might have held some interest in it; now — middling, at best.

He was about to decline politely when his glance went, by instinct, to Kim Mi-ju.

And there he found a rarely seen sight.

Kim Mi-ju was looking at him.

The meaning written in those two eyes could not have been plainer.

Wants to go.

Wants, very much, to go.

Bae Do-yoon stilled, faintly. Kim Mi-ju was always capable inside the Prosecutors’ Office; he had rarely seen her wear such a look. It was a look of utter expectation.

Kim Mi-ju seemed to realize her expression had grown too obvious: she gave a light cough at once, lowered her head, and set about arranging the notebook in her hands, attempting to restore her usual dead-serious manner.

Too late.

Bae Do-yoon had seen it.

Bae Suzy had seen it too.

A very shallow smile flashed in Bae Suzy’s eyes. The tension the case had kept in her seemed to loosen a little, at this small reversal in the officer.

Park Jin-young, as if noticing nothing, went on smiling: “It won’t take long. It also lets you both see the company’s basic flow lines — if the investigation later touches internal areas, you’ll know them that much better.”

The sentence was put cleverly. Not a mere tour: attached to the investigation.

Bae Do-yoon naturally heard that Park Jin-young was handing him a reasonable reason to stay.

He was silent a moment, and looked at Kim Mi-ju once more.

Kim Mi-ju had her head down and was working hard at appearing as if nothing had happened. But her fingers, closed around the notebook, had tightened a little.

Bae Do-yoon sighed, lightly, inside.

Fine. Since we’ve come, we may as well see the famous JYP. No chance in the previous life — this life can call it making up a regret, perhaps?

“Then I’ll trouble you.”

Kim Mi-ju lifted her head sharply.

She pulled the expression back quickly, but in that one instant her eyes had plainly lit.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

Kim Mi-ju immediately set her face straight and said, in a proper tone: “Understanding the on-site environment does indeed assist the later investigation.”

Bae Do-yoon did not expose her.

Bae Suzy, though, could not help the curve of her lips. She thought suddenly that this severe-looking Officer Kim was not, in fact, so hard to get close to. And that this Prosecutor Bae was not so unapproachable either.

Park Jin-young smiled. “Then I’ll show you around myself.”

Park Dae-jun was somewhat surprised. “Representative-nim — you personally…”

“It’s fine,” Park Jin-young waved a hand. “I happen to have the time as well.”

Bae Suzy stood at the side, assuming she should head back to the rest room.

But Park Jin-young looked back at her once.

“Suzy — if you aren’t tired, come along too.”

Bae Suzy paused, faintly.

By instinct she glanced at Bae Do-yoon; and Bae Do-yoon looked at her once at the same moment. Bae Suzy hurriedly moved her eyes away — she could not say why, but meeting his gaze made her heartbeat quicken by a great deal.

“I’m all right.”

She had been somewhat tired. Hearing that the company tour was on, she did not refuse at once. Perhaps because Bae Do-yoon would be going too — she could not explain it well herself. After the conversation just now, she had grown a few degrees more curious about this young prosecutor. He was not like any man she had known before: not warm, not ingratiating, not taking occasions to close distance. And precisely that sense of measure made a person want to observe him a little more.

A little later · the tour under way

JYP · corridors, the photo wall, the practice-room row

Three sets of eyes, one corridor, and one confessed loyalty

The several of them walked out of the meeting room.

Park Jin-young went first, Park Dae-jun half a step behind. Bae Do-yoon and Kim Mi-ju walked side by side; Bae Suzy fell not far beside them.

Corridor staff seeing the party stopped, one after another, to greet.

Corridor, as the party passes

Representative-nim. Representative-nim, hello.

And the two strangers walking with him and Suzy-ssi — especially that young man…

Nobody stares openly. Lines of sight drift past anyway, the way weather moves.

Inside JYP an entertainer appearing is ordinary. An unknown face worth looking at twice is not.

Kim Mi-ju kept, on the surface, an investigative officer’s severity. Her steps, however, were plainly a little lighter than before.

Passing a display wall hung with photographs of JYP’s artists across the generations, her line of sight finally could not resist staying a few seconds more.

Park Jin-young noticed, and smiled. “Officer Kim — do you listen to K-pop ordinarily?”

Kim Mi-ju’s body stiffened, very slightly.

“Occasionally,” she answered, with restraint.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

Occasionally?

Kim Mi-ju’s face did not change.

Park Jin-young smiled. “Which group do you like?”

Kim Mi-ju was silent for one second.

Professional instinct told her she must not behave too much like a fan inside a company under investigation. Personal instinct made it very hard to resist entirely.

In the end she said it, very small: “EXO.”

Park Jin-young blinked.

Bae Suzy also stilled, and then could not help laughing.

EXO is an SM Entertainment group. To say you like EXO in front of JYP’s founder — the answer carried a certain delicacy.

Having said it, Kim Mi-ju realized this too; the roots of her ears reddened by an imperceptible degree. She added at once: “Of course, I listen to JYP’s songs as well.”

Park Jin-young did not mind in the least; if anything his smile grew plainer. “It’s all right. EXO really is very popular.”

Bae Do-yoon said, lightly: “Officer Kim hides it well ordinarily, doesn’t she.”

Kim Mi-ju turned to look at him.

“Prosecutor — I only listen occasionally. Please don’t misunderstand.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Mm. Occasionally. I understand.”

Kim Mi-ju kept feeling there was a grain of tease inside that mm.

Bae Suzy, watching the two of them from the side, found the smile in her eyes a degree deeper. She discovered suddenly that Bae Do-yoon was not entirely without humor. His humor was only very thin; a person unfamiliar with him would miss it easily.

The party passed the practice-room row; inside, several young trainees happened to be drilling a dance, the music coming through the door with its rhythm clear.

Bae Do-yoon looked in once, and withdrew his gaze quickly.

Park Jin-young noticed, and smiled. “Prosecutor Bae does not look like a man visiting an entertainment company for the first time.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “Only the angle I look from is a little different.”

“Indeed,” Park Jin-young laughed. “Other people coming to JYP for the first time look at practice rooms, recording studios, artists’ photographs. Prosecutor Bae looks at cameras and access control.”

Kim Mi-ju, hearing this, lowered her head to hide her expression. Because she was precisely the one who had wanted to look at practice rooms and artists’ photographs.

Bae Suzy, standing beside them, also could not help a light curve of her lips.

The same corridor, read three ways

What each pair of eyes took from the tour

Source: one corridor, one photo wall, one practice-room row

Kim Mi-juthe artists’ photo wall; the music leaking from practice rooms — professionally withheld, personally noted

Park Jin-young saw all three readings and commented politely on two of them. The founder’s eye for people has not dulled in all his years.

Park Jin-young, after so many years in the entertainment world, naturally kept a good eye. He said, half in joke: “Seriously — if Prosecutor Bae entered the entertainment world, he would do exceedingly well too.”

At that sentence Kim Mi-ju glanced at Bae Do-yoon by instinct. Bae Suzy also looked at him; she had, in fact, held a similar thought just before.

Bae Do-yoon heard Park Jin-young’s words and showed no embarrassment.

He only smiled.

“Actually, I once had plans to enter the entertainment world myself.”

Park Jin-young’s step halted.

Kim Mi-ju also stilled.

Bae Suzy simply lifted her eyes, surprise added to them.

Bae Do-yoon went on: “I even got as far as SM Entertainment’s door, back then.”

Park Jin-young froze first, then wore the expression of a man hearing something immensely dramatic. “SM Entertainment?”

Bae Suzy was truly surprised; she looked at Bae Do-yoon and could not help asking: “Really?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Really.”

Bae Suzy’s curiosity was hooked all at once. She had assumed Bae Do-yoon and the entertainment world were people of entirely different worlds. And now he said, lightly, that he had once planned to enter it — even walked to SM Entertainment’s door. The gap made it very hard for her not to be curious.

She asked: “Then why did you become a prosecutor instead?”

Kim Mi-ju also looked at Bae Do-yoon. This question she actually wanted answered too. A man like him, entering the entertainment world, should not have done badly; and yet he had chosen an entirely different road. She did not understand it.

Bae Do-yoon only looked at Bae Suzy, a very faint smile at the corner of his lips, and said:

“Secret.”

Bae Suzy: …

She had been waiting for the answer seriously, and what arrived was those two characters.

“Prosecutor Bae really does enjoy jokes,” Bae Suzy pressed her lips together.

Bae Do-yoon, expression level: “Occasionally, at most.”

He can hardly tell them Lee Boo-jin intercepted him, can he. He might dare say it; they would not necessarily dare believe it.

“And you call that occasionally?”

“Compared with often, it does count as occasionally.”

Bae Suzy did not know, for a moment, how to answer that.

She found that Bae Do-yoon was not entirely the man of her first impression in the meeting room. He was indeed calm, professional, and kept his distance. But he was not without emotion. His emotion sat hidden very deep, and when a little of it surfaced now and then, it caught people off guard the more — like that secret just now: plainly only two simple characters, and yet it seemed to pluck, deliberately and lightly, at the string of her curiosity, and leave it humming.

Park Jin-young took it up, smiling: “It seems Prosecutor Bae and SM have quite the fate between them.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “I only passed by the door.”

Park Jin-young raised a brow.

“You got as far as the door, and that counts as passing by?”

Bae Do-yoon, lightly: “Not going in counts.”

Kim Mi-ju finally could not help saying, in a low voice: “That definition is very rigorous, actually.”

Bae Suzy, hearing it, laughed again despite herself.

Three questions she did not ask aloud

01Why did he walk as far as SM Entertainment’s door?

02Why did he not go in?

03What, exactly, is the secret he answered with?

In her past life this kind of curiosity was rare, because most of the time other people were the ones curious about her. She is used to being the one looked at. Today, for the first time, she had the impulse to know a person instead.

Park Jin-young walked on, the smile still in his tone.

“But honestly — if SM had kept you back then, the entertainment world might have one more extraordinary person in it now.”

Bae Do-yoon smiled lightly: “It is a little regrettable, yes.”

Bae Suzy, hearing that sentence, looked at him once.

The party did not stay long on the question of whether Bae Do-yoon suited the entertainment world. Jokes are jokes; the true purpose of their coming to JYP today remained that stalking matter. Park Jin-young brought the topic back, soon, to introducing the company’s internal areas.

Bae Do-yoon listened seriously. But his seriousness was entirely unlike an ordinary visitor’s, and Kim Mi-ju’s excitement about the tour settled back, by degrees, into working state.

Even so, she discovered one thing.

The way Bae Suzy looked at Bae Do-yoon was a little strange.

Not obvious; an ordinary person might not see it. Bae Suzy was, after all, an entertainer — a person long accustomed to controlling her expression in front of lenses; she would not, like an ordinary girl, write all her feelings on her face. But Kim Mi-ju was also a woman. Some things need no evidence; intuition admits them.

When Bae Suzy looked at Bae Do-yoon, her gaze carried a small unconscious lingering. Not an open appraisal — more like a person who knows she should not look more, and still wants one more look. Especially when Bae Do-yoon spoke with Park Jin-young: Bae Suzy’s line of sight would settle on him quite naturally. And when Bae Do-yoon turned his head toward her, she would move it away as if nothing at all had happened.

The reaction was very fine. Had Kim Mi-ju not been standing exactly beside her, she might not have caught it.

Kim Mi-ju could not help looking at Bae Do-yoon once more.

This prosecutor of mine really does carry no small charm. Even the Nation’s First Love, at the very peak of her fame, seems to have grown a little different feeling.

Then again, not strange. Kim Mi-ju seldom spent attention on such matters, but she had to admit: Bae Do-yoon had the capital to make women look twice. Young, handsome, composed, with a good road ahead. More importantly, he carried none of thatfrivolity of a man hurrying to display himself. Many men, facing a beautiful female entertainer, unconsciously show flattery, excitement, or a self-believed humor. Bae Do-yoon showed none of it — if anything, an excess of calm. And precisely that calm, at certain moments, is the likelier thing to draw interest. Especially from someone like Bae Suzy, accustomed to being watched by ten thousand people at once.

Kim Mi-ju lowered her head and pretended to arrange the papers in her hands, while a thought rose in her head, furtive and bright.

The headline she wrote, and then crossed out, in one corridor

A prosecutor and a top female entertainer. If anything at all came of those two, it would be big news — and not ordinary big news. One young prosecutor with no ceiling anyone can name; one Nation’s First Love at the height of her wind. The two identities set side by side would keep media excited for a long while.

And then she shook her head at it, internally. She did not rate it. The reason was very realistic: they seem close in age and matched in face, but their worlds are not one world. His road runs upward, and will run closer and closer to power, justice, politics, connections. She is enormously famous — and however glamorous, she remains a person of the entertainment world. An entertainer’s fame arrives fast and changes fast: loved by countless people today; pushed tomorrow to the tip of an opinion storm by one report, one rumor, one exposed romance. The public’s love is never stable. And a man like Bae Do-yoon needs stability above most things: stable identity, stable image, stable path.

A brief fondness, perhaps. But truly walking together? Too hard.

And yet: a young, beautiful female entertainer meeting a handsome, reliable prosecutor, exactly while she herself is standing inside fear and unease — a little dependence and heart-stir arising in that soil is not hard to understand at all.

Heart-stir is heart-stir. Reality is reality.

Verdict: too hard. Filed nowhere. Crossed out in one corridor, between two doorways of a photo wall.

And still, when Bae Suzy listened to Park Jin-young explain the practice-room rules, her attention was not wholly on Park Jin-young. Whenever Bae Do-yoon opened his mouth, she looked across. Even for an instant.

Kim Mi-ju sighed, very softly, inside.

Later · the tour’s end

JYP · first-floor lobby

One business card, one twenty-four-hour promise, and one parting tease

When they had seen enough, the tour reached its end. The party returned to the first-floor lobby; a good deal of time had passed. The JYP lobby still had people coming and going.

Kim Mi-ju kept her surface severity as a prosecution officer, but her eyes were plainly more satisfied than when she had arrived.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

“Looks like today’s harvest was not small.”

Kim Mi-ju said at once, in a proper tone: “Yes. A preliminary understanding of JYP’s internal structure — convenient for the later investigation.”

Bae Do-yoon, lightly: “Was that what I meant?”

Kim Mi-ju: …

Bae Suzy could not hold it in; she lowered her head and laughed a little.

Kim Mi-ju gave a light cough and pretended not to have heard.

Park Jin-young also smiled. “Thank you both for today. For any material you need later, contact Lawyer Park or the company’s administration directly.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“We’ll list a checklist soon.”

Park Dae-jun said: “I will cooperate.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at Bae Suzy.

Bae Suzy had been standing quietly; seeing him look over, her bearing grew a degree more serious without her willing it.

Bae Do-yoon took a business card from his pocket and held it out to her.

“This is my contact information.”

The business card, as handed over

Name · title · contact number. Nothing else printed on it, no decoration at all. And with it, one sentence: if you later recall any new detail, or meet any new situation, you may tell me directly. My phone stays open twenty-four hours.

Bae Suzy stilled, faintly.

She reached out and took it.

The card was very simple. No surplus ornament; only name, title and contact details printed on it.

Bae Suzy looked down at the card.

A thin single sheet of paper — and yet, inexplicably, it made her feel she had something she could hold onto. All this while she had had many people around her. Every one of them said they would protect her; every one of them told her not to worry too much. Heard too often, those words turned, rather, into a kind of helplessness.

Bae Do-yoon was different. He said it very simply: if there is a situation, contact me; my phone stays open twenty-four hours. No excessive comfort, no exaggerated guarantee — and steadier than any single don’t worry.

Bae Suzy closed her fingers lightly around the card, and then bowed, slightly.

“Thank you, Prosecutor Bae.” Her tone was very serious.

No longer an entertainer’s polite thanks to an outsider. From the heart, this time.

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “This is my work.”

Bae Suzy lifted her head and looked at him.

She knew it was indeed his work. And yet, she did not know why, hearing him say it so, a small indescribable sense of loss rose in her chest instead. As if, to him, all of it sat inside the scope of duty: protecting her, investigating the case, leaving his contact — all because he is a prosecutor, and not because she is Bae Suzy.

The thought had barely surfaced when she found it inexplicable herself. They had met for the first time today. How could one businesslike sentence from him produce such a subtle feeling in her?

Bae Suzy lowered her eyes and pressed that feeling down.

When she lifted her head again, she had returned to calm.

“If there is a new situation, I will contact you.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “Good.”

Park Jin-young stood beside them, his gaze resting between the two for an instant; but he said nothing more. He only smiled: “Then I’ll see you both out.”

“No trouble,” Bae Do-yoon said. “We can see ourselves out.”

Park Jin-young did not insist.

“Then — thank you for your trouble.”

Bae Do-yoon and Kim Mi-ju turned and left.

Kim Mi-ju walked behind him; before passing out through JYP’s main door, she could not help turning to look back once.

Bae Suzy was still standing in the lobby. The card held in her hand, her line of sight resting on the direction Bae Do-yoon had left by.

Kim Mi-ju withdrew her gaze, and could not help sighing inwardly once more.

It seems this case may not stay merely a case, after all.

Bae Do-yoon, walking in front, spoke suddenly.

“Officer Kim.”

Kim Mi-ju started.

“Yes.”

Bae Do-yoon did not turn his head. “What were you thinking just now?”

Kim Mi-ju, face unchanging: “I was thinking about the materials we need to pull next.”

Bae Do-yoon’s steps did not pause.

“Is that so?”

“Yes.”

“Including EXO’s materials?”

Kim Mi-ju: …

Bae Do-yoon, lightly: “Next time, hide it a little deeper.”

Kim Mi-ju was silent for several seconds, and finally said, in a low voice: “Prosecutor — you really do enjoy jokes.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

“Occasionally.”

Kim Mi-ju understood, suddenly, why Bae Suzy had been left speechless earlier.

This man was indeed skilled at making people not know how to answer, precisely while being perfectly serious.

On occasionally

He meters his jokes the way he meters evidence: sparingly, precisely, and never twice in the same place. Two women filed the same small laugh under two different case numbers this afternoon — one crossed hers out in a corridor, one folded hers around a business card. Neither filing is in this book’s evidence list. Both are in its margins, which is where margins earn their keep.
