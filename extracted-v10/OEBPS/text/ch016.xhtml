Chapter Sixteen

The Department Chief’s Hint

Nightfall · back from JYP

Seoul Central District Prosecutors’ Office · Criminal Division 3

Where the corridor is quieter and the lamps are not

By the time they were back at the Prosecutors’ Office, the sky had already gone dark.

Kim Mi-ju sorted through once more the records they had brought back from JYP, and set out as a list the materials Park Dae-jun had promised to provide.

Bae Do-yoon sat at his desk and wrote down, item by item, what they had learned today.

The paper, that evening

01Public schedules: comments, emails, gifts — presence manufactured where anyone can look

02Non-public routes: at least several appearances where no schedule was published at all

03Each item alone: not enough to lock a suspect. All of them together: a direction

The stalker is not simply following Bae Suzy through public schedules and the open internet. At least a few times, he held her unpublished movements in his hand.

Kim Mi-ju looked up at him.

“Prosecutor — should it go up to the Department Chief now?”

Bae Do-yoon picked up the file.

“I’ll carry it up myself.”

Kim Mi-ju nodded.

“Then I’ll keep working on the materials list.”

“Mm.” Bae Do-yoon rose and left the office.

The Prosecutors’ Office corridor was a good deal quieter than in the daytime, but most of the offices still had their lamps lit.

For prosecutors, night is only another working hour.

· · ·

The same evening · a knock answered at once

The Department Chief Prosecutor’s office

A man unsurprised, a file flipped through, and one word that had been said before

Bae Do-yoon knocked at the Department Chief Prosecutor’s door.

A voice came from inside at once.

“Come in.”

He pushed the door open.

The Department Chief Prosecutor sat behind his desk, several case files stacked on its surface. He looked up at Bae Do-yoon once; his expression held no surprise, as if he had known all along that this visitor would come.

“Back, are you?”

“Yes, Bu-jang-nim.”

Bae Do-yoon stepped forward and set the file on the desk.

“What we learned at JYP today, and the preliminary investigative direction I have drawn from it.”

The Department Chief took up the file and flipped a few pages, casually.

After a moment he closed it.

“Since this matter has been handed to you, you will own the investigation and the evidence from here. Catch him quickly.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Yes, Bu-jang-nim.”

The case was in his hands now; its next steps would naturally be his to drive. And from what they had found at JYP today, this was no longer an ordinary harassment case. If the harasser had truly held Bae Suzy’s unpublished schedules, then behind him there must exist a chain of leaked information. Until that chain was laid bare, catching one surface-level harasser would not end the matter.

Bae Do-yoon accepted the order — and did not leave.

The Department Chief had already begun to return to his case files; seeing the younger man still standing where he was, he lifted his eyes.

“Something else?”

Bae Do-yoon was silent for a moment.

Then he said: “Bu-jang-nim — I have only just arrived at this office. Why do you trust me enough to hand me the case directly?”

The Department Chief looked at him once; then he gave a light laugh.

“You think I trust too easily?”

Bae Do-yoon said, evenly: “At least it does not look like the normal procedure.”

The Department Chief did not refute him at once — and that very reaction moved something faintly in Bae Do-yoon’s chest.

If this were ordinary favor, the Chief could have brushed past with a few ceremonial words.

He did not.

The Department Chief set the file back on the desk and leaned a little into his chair back.

“I have read your record.”

“Your grades at the Judicial Research and Training Institute, your practical evaluations, your ranking in your cohort, the records of the cases you joined before this — I have read them all.”

Bae Do-yoon’s gaze moved, very lightly.

The Chief spoke too specifically for a man who had merely flipped through a newcomer’s file. It looked more as if he had studied Bae Do-yoon seriously before the man ever badged in.

The Department Chief, as if noticing nothing, went on: “In grades and in practical experience both, you stand at the top of your cohort.”

He paused, and added one more sentence.

“Rarer still — your record is clean.”

Clean.

Hearing that word, Bae Do-yoon’s mind stopped for an instant.

It was not the first time he had heard it. And the first time, it had been Lee Boo-jin who said it.

The word, and the woman who said it first

Clean. Lee Boo-jin had said it looking at him the way appraisers look at porcelain: no cracks, no repairs, no fingerprints but his own. Said by her, it had been a verdict on a life she intended to use. Said tonight, in this lamplit office, by a man whose favor he did not ask for — it sounded like something else entirely, and he could not yet name what.

Two speakers, one word, no overlap between their reasons. That is the part worth keeping: a clean record is never only about what you did. It is about who has been counting on you doing nothing else.

The Department Chief said, lightly: “No tangled connections, no hurrying to attach yourself to anyone. Young prosecutors like that are not common now.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at him.

“So that is why Bu-jang-nim gave me this case?”

“That is one of the reasons.”

The Department Chief did not deny it. He took up the fountain pen on his desk and turned it once, gently.

“The case is not large. But it suits you well.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Suits me?”

“It suits seeing whether a newcomer can actually do the work.”

The Department Chief’s tone was flat.

“Bae Suzy is an entertainer at the height of her wind, and JYP is no small company. On the surface this is one harassment case. Dig downward, and it will touch the company, public relations, the media — even people who do not wish to show their faces.”

The Chief looked at him once.

“You went to JYP today. You must have felt it too, I take it?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded. “They are not unwilling to cooperate. They simply do not want their internal problems fully exposed.”

The Department Chief smiled.

“That is the habit of big companies.”

He said it casually, in the manner of a man who has seen it far too often.

“With their mouths they say cooperate. In their hearts the first reaction is always to control risk. What they can hand you, they hand you; what they cannot — either they say they don’t have it, or they say it can’t be found, or they say it touches the entertainer’s privacy and the company’s secrets.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “So I intend to start from the informed-person list for the non-public schedules, the access-control records, the parking-lot CCTV original files, and the outsourced security rosters.”

The Department Chief looked at him.

“And the basis for that judgment?”

Bae Do-yoon opened the file to the page he had organized himself.

“Across the several abnormal incidents, the public schedules and the non-public ones behave differently.”

He pointed at the table.

Behavior comparison · public routes vs. unpublished routes

Public schedulesPresence manufactured through online comments, emails, gifts — noise where anyone may look

Non-public routesAppearances at parking lots, temporary shoot locations, near hotels — silence where nobody was told

Precision patternSometimes exact, not every time — too loose for a core-team leaker, too sharp for a fan

He had her routes when the cameras were not told. That is not fandom. That is a supply line.

Working hypothesis, spoken aloud in this office: peripheral leakage — outsourced security, event-company staff, temporarily authorized personnel, anyone whose hand touches a schedule one step removed.

The Department Chief lowered his head and looked at the page once.

Bae Do-yoon continued: “This shows the other party is not merely an ordinary fan. At least some of the time, he held part of Bae Suzy’s unpublished movements.”

The Chief asked: “You suspect someone inside JYP?”

“I cannot confirm a core insider,” Bae Do-yoon said. “If a core member of the management team were leaking, the information he held would be steadier and more precise. What we see is: several appearances exactly right, but not every one followed.”

He paused.

“So I lean toward peripheral leakage — outsourced security, event staff, temporarily authorized personnel, or someone who can touch schedule arrangements indirectly.”

Hearing it through, a flicker of satisfaction crossed the Department Chief’s eyes. He gave no praise; he said only, lightly: “The direction is acceptable.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Tomorrow I will first have JYP submit the related materials for the past three months.”

“If procedure is needed, go through procedure,” the Chief said. “If they drag their feet, use formal documents.”

“Yes.”

“But do not make noise at the start,” the Department Chief turned the blade of the talk. “Before there is clear evidence, do not let everyone inside the company know the prosecution suspects a leak source. Otherwise the people who truly have a problem will process their traces at once.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “I will control the circle of those who know.”

The Department Chief looked at him.

After a moment he said, suddenly: “That is also one of the reasons I gave you the case.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his eyes.

The Chief said, slowly: “You are young, but not impatient. Compared with newcomers in a hurry to prove themselves, you know when to hold the tempo down.”

Reaching there, his tone thinned a degree.

“But that alone is not enough.”

Bae Do-yoon did not answer at once.

The Department Chief went on: “Later you will find that some cases are not impossible to solve. They are cases many people do not want you to keep investigating.”

The air in the office seemed to sink a little.

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Is Bu-jang-nim referring to this case?”

The Department Chief looked at him, and suddenly smiled.

“I am referring to later.”

Bae Do-yoon heard it clearly, and heard more than one layer inside it.

On its surface this conversation was about Bae Suzy’s harassment case. But the Chief’s words had already stepped over the case itself. He did not seem to be merely reminding a newcomer how to run a file. It looked more like a man telling Bae Do-yoon that the roads inside the Prosecutors’ Office are far more complicated than anything written on a case file.

Bae Do-yoon was silent a moment, then said: “I walk only where the evidence walks.”

The Department Chief looked at him.

“Young prosecutors all like to say that.”

Bae Do-yoon did not argue; the Chief did not press further.

He said only, lightly: “Walking by the evidence is not wrong, of course. But understand this: sometimes the evidence is laid right there, and still not everyone wants you to see it.”

Bae Do-yoon’s eyes moved, faintly.

The Department Chief said no more.

Some words, stopped here, are enough. One step further and they become explicit instruction.

After a moment the Chief took up the report again, and his tone returned to official business.

“One more point.”

“Sir.”

“Keep a necessary distance from Bae Suzy.”

Bae Do-yoon’s expression did not change.

The Chief said: “She is a public figure and a party to the case. Any single private contact between you and her, if the media photographs it, will be written up as a different story.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“I understand.”

“Have Officer Kim present as much as possible. Keep records of phone communication. Texts, calls, emails — whatever should be archived, archive it.”

“Yes.”

Department Chief Prosecutor · standing order on the Bae Suzy matter

One: maintain necessary distance between the handling prosecutor and the party Bae Suzy.

Two: investigative officer Kim Mi-ju present at contact wherever possible.

Three: texts, calls and emails relating to the party retained and archived without exception.

Given verbally, at night, without ceremony — and therefore, in this office, the kind of order that is remembered word for word.

The Department Chief looked at him for a while, then gave a light, faint laugh.

“It is not that I distrust you.”

Bae Do-yoon said nothing.

The Chief went on: “It is only that the eyes watching you now may be more than you imagine.”

Bae Do-yoon checked, faintly.

“Watching me?”

The Department Chief did not answer directly.

He lowered his head and opened another case file at his side, as if the conversation were already over.

“Go. From tomorrow, push it forward formally.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at him.

The Chief did not lift his head again; he said only: “First find the information-leak chain. Do not hurry to catch a man.”

Bae Do-yoon was silent an instant, then took up the file.

“Yes, Bu-jang-nim.”

He turned to leave.

At the door, the Department Chief Prosecutor called him again.

“Bae Do-yoon.”

Bae Do-yoon stopped and looked back.

Only then did the Department Chief lift his head.

“Make this case beautiful. Many people in this office do not know you yet.”

He paused.

“And many people are waiting to know you.”

The sentence sounded like encouragement.

But Bae Do-yoon heard a different meaning inside it. He nodded.

“Understood.” He pushed the door open and left; the office door closed behind him.

The eyes watching you now may be more than you imagine.

Walking back to his own office with the file in hand, the Chief’s words stayed on in his head.

A very clean record. Many people will not want you to keep investigating. The eyes watching you are more than you imagine. And that last sentence…

Bae Do-yoon did not think these were meaningless reminders.

The Chief had handed him this case because he had the ability, certainly.

But not only because of ability.

Perhaps from the very start this case carried a flavor of testing. Testing whether he can do the work; testing whether he understands measure; testing, even, where he will stand in the end.

Bae Do-yoon’s steps did not stop.

He did not like being arranged into somebody’s board game.

But he knew just as clearly: while he stands inside the Prosecutors’ Office, he cannot remain outside the game forever.

At least for now, he did not need to hurry and answer anyone’s questions.

What he had to do was only this: investigate this case clearly, first.

When Bae Do-yoon returned to the office, Kim Mi-ju had already finished organizing the materials list.

Seeing him enter, she lifted her head at once.

“What did the Chief say?”

Bae Do-yoon set the file on the desk.

“The case is ours to run.”

Kim Mi-ju was not surprised.

“Then tomorrow we start on JYP’s schedule-notification chain?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“First, every non-public schedule from the past three months.”

He drew out his chair, sat, and opened his notebook.

“Weight on the outsourced security, the temporarily authorized personnel, and the parking-lot entry records.”

Kim Mi-ju wrote it down quickly.

“Understood.”

Bae Do-yoon thought a moment and added: “At JYP’s side, do not notify broadly at first. Only Park Dae-jun and the administration head cooperate. The smaller the circle the materials travel in, the better.”

Kim Mi-ju heard his meaning.

“You are worried someone will process the traces in advance?”

“Mm.”

Bae Do-yoon said: “If a leak source truly exists, the quieter we are now, the easier it is for them to show a flaw.”

Kim Mi-ju nodded.

“I’ll be careful.”

Then the Chief’s words surfaced in his mind again.

Make this case beautiful.

Bae Do-yoon’s eyes cooled, slowly.

Whatever intentions stood behind the Chief: the victim in this case was real. Bae Suzy being harassed was real, and happening. Someone had walked through JYP’s protection systems, peered into her life, held her movements in his hand, and tried by that means to manufacture fear and a sense of control.

That was the problem he had to solve now.

As for the invisible gazes and tests inside the Prosecutors’ Office — those could wait, for the time being, behind.

· · ·

Meanwhile · the lamp still lit

The Department Chief Prosecutor’s office, after the door closed

A number dialed without ceremony, and a verdict delivered in halves

After Bae Do-yoon left, the Chief did not at once return to his case files. He sat in his chair, his gaze resting on the preliminary report on the desk.

After a while he picked up the telephone and dialed a number.

The line connected quickly.

A call placed after the door closed · neither end named anywhere

The case has already been handed to him.

[what the other end said is not written down anywhere]

As it stands, his direction is gripped very accurately.

[one more sentence, from the other side]

Ability — he has it.

What remains is to see whether he is clever enough.

[a brief silence on the line]

This office does not lack people who can investigate a case. What it lacks is people who know how to investigate, how far to investigate, and where to stand when they finish.

The Chief’s voice had grown lower by the last sentence. The report stayed under his hand the whole call, open at the page a young prosecutor had drawn his table on.

On hints, and chessboards

A hint is an instruction that refuses to be quoted. Say it one word further and it becomes an order a man can repeat; stop one word earlier and it was never said at all. The Department Chief walked that line all evening, and the young prosecutor walked it back — each of them hearing two meanings and admitting to one. Neither likes chessboards. Both keep counting the squares anyway.
