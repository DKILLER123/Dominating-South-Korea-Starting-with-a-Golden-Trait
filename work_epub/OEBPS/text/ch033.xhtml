Chapter Thirty-Three

By the Way, What on Earth Is That Bastard’s Name?

The next morning · the Prosecutors’ Office, his office

Morning light, a desk with files waiting, and one lip that has something it cannot say

One prosecutor in a new suit, one clerk who has noticed it before he has noticed it himself

Bae Do-yoon had only just reached the Prosecutors’ Office, his bottom not yet warm in the chair, when Kim Mi-ju found the anomaly.

“Huh, Prosecutor Bae — why is your mouth broken?”

Kim Mi-ju stood by the desk holding a bundle of files, her eyes on Bae Do-yoon’s face, looking him over carefully, twice. She felt, all in all, that today’s Bae Do-yoon was a little different from usual.

Kim Mi-ju looked at him for a moment, and a rather outlandish phrase surfaced in her mind.

Love-struck?

The moment the thought was out, she herself flinched a little at it. But on second thought, this particular feeling had, as it happened, appeared once before. That time, Bae Do-yoon had been like this too: nothing on the surface, and yet the whole person seemed to carry a measure of looseness that could not be named, a few parts less of the usual coldness around the eyes, a few parts more of a lazy quality in them.

“Hm?”

At the word, Bae Do-yoon raised an eyebrow, then lifted a hand to his lower lip. When the pad of his finger found the small wound there, his motion paused a beat. That was exactly the place Jessica had bitten, last night. It had stopped bleeding, but the mark was still there. And Bae Do-yoon’s complexion, to begin with, ran pale, so that small break at the corner of his mouth stood out all the more. Come a little closer, and it was nearly visible at a glance.

Bae Do-yoon drew his hand back. His expression did not change, in the least.

Of course he was not going to tell Kim Mi-ju the truth. So he thought a moment, gave a light smile, and said: “I met a little wild cat last night.”

“Was only going to play with her — and got scratched by accident.”

Kim Mi-ju’s eyes opened a little, as if she actually believed some of it.

“And where is this little wild cat of yours?”

Bae Do-yoon looked up at her, and his expression went, at once, a little exasperated.

“Rather than the little wild cat, should you not be more concerned about me?”

Hearing this, Kim Mi-ju could not help a light laugh. She set the files on the desk, swept her gaze once more over Bae Do-yoon’s face, and said, light-toned: “Aside from that cut on your mouth, everything else about you looks quite fine, actually.”

As she spoke, she added, with great seriousness, one more line: “Your spirit is good, too. Your complexion is good. You don’t look at all like someone who needs anyone’s concern.”

“… ”

Bae Do-yoon had nothing to offer the sentence but an ellipsis of exactly three points.

Kim Mi-ju’s smile deepened, as if she had at last caught hold of one of the rare handles. “But a wild cat who can hurt you that badly must be a pretty formidable one, no?”

Bae Do-yoon leaned back against the chair and gave her a light sideways look.

“Pretty formidable, indeed.”

He said it lightly, but the corner of his mouth lifted, of its own accord, a little. Kim Mi-ju caught that, sharply, and her eyes went, at once, a measure more subtle.

Was what he was saying, actually a cat?

She had only meant to tease with two casual lines — but after she had said them, her gaze did not leave Bae Do-yoon’s face right away. Holding her files, she scanned him once more, without making a sound. The suit was in order, the tie was straight, the shirt buttons buttoned to the proper position with not the least carelessness. On the outside alone, Bae Do-yoon was still that somewhat cold prosecutor. But precisely because it was like that, the more it was like that, the more certain details stood out, strangely, as out of place.

Kim Mi-ju’s gaze moved down, slowly. The next second, her eyes stopped, hard.

The clerk’s read · compiled in the space of one lowered look

The wound, offeredone small break at the corner of the lip; the story supplied: a little wild cat, played with, scratched by accident

The marks, observedone side of the neck, near the collarbone; several, pale red, at the place the collar half hides and half shows

The story’s survival ratelow; a cat does not work in patterns, and a cat does not aim at the collarbone

The conclusion, reachedlast night, there was a woman; the man knows it, and is currently managing his collar with the calm of a man who has decided on his sentence

Disposition: nothing. The clerk files it, and chooses, for the moment, the sentence with the best delivery.

Bae Do-yoon, still looking down through his files, sensed that her gaze had lingered a little too long, and raised his head.

“What is it?”

Kim Mi-ju’s gaze lay, unblinking, on one side of his neck. Her expression first stilled a little, and then a meaningful smile surfaced in it. Bae Do-yoon, being looked at like that, felt a little ill at ease, and sat up straighter, without meaning to.

“Is there something on my face?”

“On your face, no, there isn’t.”

Kim Mi-ju smiled, her tone sounding very normal — but that smile, no matter how he looked at it, was a little strange.

Bae Do-yoon’s brow rose a beat.

“Then what are you looking at?”

Kim Mi-ju drew her gaze back, hugged the files in her arms a little tighter, slowly and deliberately, as if she had just thought of something, and gave a light nod.

“Nothing.”

Having said that, she looked at Bae Do-yoon once more, and her smile deepened, further.

“I just feel that Prosecutor Bae must have slept well last night.”

“?”

The sentence had come out of nowhere. Before he could ask what it meant, Kim Mi-ju had already turned and walked out. At the door, she added, very considerately: “I put the files on your desk. The morning meeting starts at ten — don’t forget.”

When the words landed, she left the office, crisply and decisively.

Bae Do-yoon sat in his chair, his expression rarely a little blank.

Slept well?

What on earth did that mean?

Bae Do-yoon looked down at himself, then raised a hand to his face.

Aside from the wound on the lip, there shouldn’t be anything else strange, right?

But that look Kim Mi-ju had given him just now had clearly not been looking at his mouth.

What had she actually seen?

The more Bae Do-yoon thought, the less it added up. A moment later, he put down the files in his hand, stood, and walked out of the office. In the Prosecutors’ Office corridor people came and went; Bae Do-yoon, his face as usual, passed through the corridor and went straight to the restroom.

Standing before the mirror, he first looked at his own face. The break on the lip was indeed very obvious. But this he already knew from just now. Bae Do-yoon frowned, moved his gaze down, then turned his head a little and loosened his shirt collar, a little.

The next second, his motion stopped.

In the mirror, at the place on his neck near the collarbone, several pale red scratch marks sat there, clearly. The position was not especially low — exactly the place where the shirt collar half hid them and half let them show. If you did not pay attention, normally, you might not see them. But come a little closer — especially from the angle where Kim Mi-ju had been standing just now — and they were nearly visible at a glance.

Bae Do-yoon raised a hand and touched them. No pain. Almost no feeling at all.

The problem was that the marks were very obvious. And there was no way, at all, to fudge past them with an excuse like got scratched by a little wild cat. After all, anyone who was not an idiot could see that these scratch marks were by no means left by a cat’s claws.

More like…

Bae Do-yoon’s expression froze, subtly, for a beat.

The morning damage report · compiled in the restroom mirror

01The lip — one small break, no longer bleeding, visible at a glance from close range

02The neck — several pale red scratches, at the collar’s half-hidden place

03The cat story — survival rate: none; any witness who is not an idiot rules it out on sight

04The rigorous image, as built in one particular clerk’s heart — collapsed, completely, sometime before the restroom

Assessment: still dressed to the last button. Still, somehow, a few more holes in the armor than the outfit should have.

No wonder Kim Mi-ju had worn that expression just now, and had said he must have slept well. She had, by all probability, already guessed that there had been a woman by his side last night.

Bae Do-yoon stared at himself in the mirror for a few seconds, then closed his eyes, helplessly.

That Jessica had bitten him last night was one thing — but to have left this kind of mark was another. And precisely because he had left in such a hurry that morning, he had not noticed it at all. Now it was too late. One wound on the mouth, several scratch marks on the neck. Even if he wanted to pretend nothing had happened, it was, by all accounts, already too late.

Bae Do-yoon set his collar back in order, trying hard to cover those few marks. But the shirt collar was, after all, limited in its coverage; as long as the motion was even a little bigger, a measure of mark would still show.

Looking at the self in the mirror — still impeccable in dress, but somehow carrying a few more holes in the armor — he could not help a light sigh.

“It seems…”

Bae Do-yoon said it low, to himself, a measure of frustration in the tone.

“The rigorous image I had built in her heart has completely collapsed.”

The rigorous image I had built in her heart has completely collapsed.

Having said that, he looked at the mirror once more.

“Jung Soo-yeon — you really do know how to make trouble for me.”

The same morning · Jessica’s home, back from the suite

Her own apartment, door closed; the air still carrying the cold of the car ride

Sneezes, one sister at the door, and a company that is about to cost her

Ah-choo — ah-choo —

Not long after getting home, Jessica had been sneezing in a row. Krystal, who was about to go out to the company, stopped at the sound, turned to look at her, and her brows knit a little.

“Unnie, are you okay?”

She set her bag down to the side and came a few steps closer. “Did you catch a cold?”

“Maybe.”

Jessica answered casually. Her tone sounded no different from usual, her expression still light, as if she had only caught a little chill and it was nothing at all. But in fact, she had already cursed that person a thousand times in her heart. The scenes of last night flashed through her mind, uncontrollable, and the more she thought, the more the color in her face grew subtle. That bastard had had his fill, worked her until she had no strength left, and afterward did not even think to look after someone. The air conditioning in the room had been that strong, and he had, precisely, left her there, not even putting a blanket over her, so that as soon as she woke this morning her head felt a little heavy, and her nose was not very comfortable either.

Thinking to here, Jessica could not help a cold little snort, in her heart. Really only thinking of himself — not a bit considering of other people.

But complaining or not, once some of the details of last night surfaced, the root of her ear was still, beyond her control, a little hot, and her cheeks followed, tinged with a faint flush. Although at the beginning she had been pushed to that point, and many things had truly not been in her expectations, at least… that person was not someone she found unpleasant.

Of course. She was referring to his looks, and his build.

The self-assessment she refuses to complete · running in the background

Expectedirritation, flustered anger, the second night’s proper accounting — the reaction the morning was supposed to deliver

Actuala faint heat at the root of the ear, a light flush on the cheek, and one thought she would not admit twice

The looks, assessed against the termsnot unpleasant; and, in a sense, grown exactly on the aesthetic point

Verdict, quietly filed: not that bad. If it had really been someone else, there would have been no yielding, no matter what — let alone coming this far.

The moment the thought surfaced, Jessica herself froze a beat, then, a little awkwardly, moved her gaze, as if even she did not quite want to admit it. But in her heart she also understood: this truly counted as one of the few things worth being grateful for.

“Huh, Unnie, your face is all red.”

Krystal, standing beside her, looked visibly a little more worried. “Are you really okay? Want to take some medicine?”

Jessica only then came back to herself, raised a hand to her face, and said, keeping her composure: “It’s nothing — I’m probably just a little tired. Resting a bit will fix it.”

She said it lightly, clearly not planning to explain much. Krystal looked at her for two seconds, and seeing that she really did not seem to want to say more, she did not press, only nodded.

“Then remember to drink more water.”

“Mm.”

Jessica answered lightly, sat down on the sofa, and her expression finally, slowly, recovered to usual. Krystal picked up her bag, then, as if something had just come back to her, turned her head and asked one more line:

“By the way — how are things at the company?”

Hearing this, Jessica’s gaze paused a beat. She was silent a moment, then let out a light sigh.

“A little troublesome.”

Krystal had only asked casually — but hearing this, her expression immediately became serious.

“What’s going on?”

She frowned and looked at Jessica. “On Kwon Young-il’s side?”

Jessica nodded.

“Mm. Has something to do with him.”

Krystal’s face changed at once.

What the older sister said, in the order she said it

Source: one sigh, a pause, and a tone that dropped a few parts

The ledgerbrought in a while back, by the man in question; the feeling at the time — some of it was not quite right

The professionalasked, privately; the scope — a look at the whole book, not one page

The full report was not yet on the table. Only the verdict was — and the verdict was enough to change the weather in the room.

Jessica continued: “He brought the ledger over a while back. At the time I felt something was a little off in some places, so I asked a professional to take a look for me.”

Having said this, she paused a moment, and her tone dropped a few parts.

“Turns out the ledger has a lot of problems.”

The ledger, after the professional’s eyes · what the review found

Entriesstretches of figures that do not add up, line after line, each one patient enough to look innocent

Invoicesamounts with no support behind them; documents that end exactly where the money began

Roundingsums that come out suspiciously even — the kind a clean book does not produce

The patternnot one slip, but a habit; and the practice has a length to it

The moment the words landed, Krystal’s whole face sank, the fire in her eyes nearly unbearable.

“I knew it!”

Her tone carried obvious anger — and even a measure of frustration. “I told you before, that person is unreliable — you just wouldn’t listen!”

Jessica raised her eyes to look at her younger sister, her expression complicated. This time, she did not rebut. Because she knew: what Krystal had said was not wrong. Many of the company’s affairs before, she had indeed left to Kwon Young-il to manage. Investment, connections, operations — even some of the key ledgers, she had once, reassuredly, let him touch. At that time, she had truly believed in Kwon Young-il. Believed he was helping her, believed he could stand on the same side as she could. Even when her sister had reminded her, again and again, she had still always felt that Krystal simply had a prejudice against Kwon Young-il.

But now, looking back, those words Krystal had said back then had, as if by appointment, come true.

The sister’s warnings · coming back, one by one

Krystal had, more than once, complained of Kwon Young-il in front of her: the way he smiled was not sincere; the way he looked at people always made one uncomfortable.

And once, without the least courtesy: that shovel-shaped face of his — put him in a TV drama and he is a proper villain face, every inch of him.

Back then, hearing this, Jessica had only thought her sister was throwing a tantrum, and had even quarreled with her over it, a few times. She had been so sunk in it, so deep inside it, that no one’s advice could get in.

The record, re-read this morning: every warning, one by one, had come true.

But now that things were at this point, the facts stood in front of her, and she discovered how ridiculous her own former trust looked. As if she had been slapped hard, in public — it would have hurt if it had not hurt.

Jessica leaned back against the sofa, a measure of self-mockery crossing her eyes.

“Now that I look at it — what you said before wasn’t wrong, either.”

Hearing this, Krystal had originally wanted to keep firing, but seeing her sister in this state, the words at her mouth were, in the end, forced back down her throat. Of course she was angry. But she knew, more than that, that the person who was suffering most right now was not herself — it was Jessica. After all, the one who had been deceived was her, and the one who had been betrayed was also her.

Krystal pressed her lips, her tone easing a little, but still she could not help saying: “Unnie, you just trusted him too much.”

Jessica did not deny it, only said, low: “Mm.”

That she admitted it so quietly, on the contrary, made Krystal’s heart even less at ease. That older sister before, proud to the point of stubbornness, rarely bowed her head like this. And now, she had truly been woken up by reality.

Seeing that Krystal had been standing beside her all this while, Jessica’s heart warmed a little, and at last she managed, with effort, to squeeze out a measure of a smile.

“All right,”

she said softly. “I’ll handle these things. Hurry to the company — don’t keep Sulli and the others waiting.”

Krystal glanced at the time, and as if only now remembering that she really was about to be late, could only nod.

“All right, Unnie — we’ll talk tonight.”

She paused a beat, her tone a few parts more serious. “Whatever happens, I’m standing with you.”

Jessica looked at her sister’s solemn expression, and the heaviness that had been pressing on her heart scattered a little. She raised a hand and waved, saying with pretended distaste: “Mm, all right — don’t say things that corny.”

Krystal immediately hehe-laughed, and the heavy atmosphere from just a moment ago was washed away, quite a bit.

“Then I’m off.”

She lifted her bag and walked toward the door, and as she was going out still remembered to turn back and give one more reminder: “Unnie, remember to take the medicine — I really don’t want to come back after work and see you unable to get up.”

Jessica was a little helpless, but still nodded.

“Understood.”

The door was closed, quickly.

After her sister left, Jessica sat on the sofa and spaced out for a while — and then, in the end, stood and went to get the medicine. She poured a glass of water, tipped the pills into her palm, and looked down. The small white pills lay quietly in her palm; nothing could be more ordinary — and yet, after staring at them for a few seconds, she suddenly felt an indescribable strangeness. Like… she had forgotten something important. That feeling was very blurred at first, only lightly crossing her mind, leaving her for a moment unable to catch the thread. Jessica stood where she was, her brows knitting a little, trying hard to remember.

A moment later, her face changed, suddenly, as if she had been struck by something; the whole person straightened up, at once.

“The medicine…”

She murmured the one word, her pupils contracting a little. The next second, she finally reacted, completely, and in her expression even a measure of panic she could not hide had entered.

“Right. The medicine… it’s the medicine!”

She had finally remembered what she had forgotten. Everything last night had been too chaotic, too sudden, completely beyond her expectation. Then, coming back in such a hurry this morning, her head had been full of company matters and her body not feeling well — and the result was that she had actually forgotten the single most important thing.

Last night, they had not taken any precautions at all. And after coming back this morning, she had not at all remembered to handle it, right away.

Thinking to here, Jessica’s heart leapt up, and even her breathing went a few parts out of order.

“No way…”

She said it, low; the hand holding the medicine box tightened, without her meaning to.

“No way — I have to go buy it, right now.”

Everything that had happened between her and that bastard was, originally, one mistake. A mistake that should not have begun, and should not have continued. If that was so, then absolutely no second mistake could be allowed to appear. A consequence like that, she could not bear it. And she absolutely could not accept it.

By the way — what on earth was that bastard’s name?

Jessica only now let it reach her: two nights, with him — and she still did not know his name.

On the name, not yet said

The chapter ends on a question no one in the story has answered, because the story has not yet allowed it to be answered: by the way, what on earth is that bastard’s name? Two nights have passed, and the name has not. The morning’s arithmetic is all done — the medicine, the meeting at ten, the ledger, the sister’s warnings coming back one by one — and the one number still missing from the sum is the most ordinary kind, the kind every other person in the building already has. She has slept twice with a man, cursed him a thousand times, assessed his build against her own aesthetic point, and arranged none of it under a name. For her he exists only as a pronoun: him, the man, that bastard — and the gap between those three words and the thing they should be pointing at is, in this book, where the next payment comes from. A name, once it is learned, will not behave the way a pronoun does. A name is a handle. And in a house built on leverage, a handle is the first thing anyone reaches for.
