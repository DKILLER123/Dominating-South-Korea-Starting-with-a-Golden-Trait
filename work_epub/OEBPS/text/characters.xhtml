In this story

His first life ended with a simple ambition: finish high school, become an artist at SM Entertainment, quietly date an idol, and never explain himself to anybody again. The second life started the same way and then lost its lane, because a stranger offered two options and the second one was the Han River.

He chose the first option, took a prosecutor’s seat, and inherited the golden trait entry that had been quietly steering him the whole time. The chaebol houses began calling him a wolf. The older men in the room began calling him Yama’s judge. He is still, by his own account, a man who wanted a girlfriend and got an empire instead.

Born, age, height, family and office number are deliberately absent from this card: this book fixes each of them in the chapter that first says them out loud, and no card is allowed to invent ahead of that.

In this story

She recruits the way her family negotiates: by making refusal expensive. The serial’s version of her is a character in an alternate-history story, and the biography of the real person of that name is not this book’s material to correct or to flatter.

Real people appear here as dramatis personae of a fictional serial; that is a storytelling choice, not a claim about anyone’s life, conduct or opinions. Portraits, once installed, are press photography or commissioned illustration and are labelled on the card that carries them. New cards are added the cycle after a figure first appears, and bios grow by append, never by rewriting what a previous chapter settled.
