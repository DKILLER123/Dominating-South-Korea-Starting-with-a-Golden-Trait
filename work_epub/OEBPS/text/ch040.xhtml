Chapter Forty

He Likes a Girl Like Yoonah-Ooni

Still the same room · the same evening

The griddle cycling; a fresh round of meat; the small plates already full

The second person to know

“I really can’t understand.”

“For someone to be confessed to by you, like that, and still refuse — he’d have to be extraordinary.”

Irene’s ears went warm again, the way they had been.

“Unnie, please don’t—”

“Why can’t I?” Taeyeon watched her, smiling — her eyes full of a frank, unapologetic admiration.

“You’re already beautiful, you know.”

Then, as if afraid of scaring her, she let her voice go a little softer.

“And it isn’t only the face.”

“The way you are, too. It’s good.”

The denial Irene had been about to give stopped on the way to her mouth.

Taeyeon laid a fresh round of meat onto the griddle and, as if simply continuing where they had been, asked one more thing.

“So — are you still in touch with the one who turned you down?”

Irene did not deny it. She nodded, lightly.

“Still in touch.”

“What we have didn’t grow distant, just because the confession was refused.”

It had been a question off the cuff. The answer made Taeyeon’s hand stop.

She turned to her; surprise had added itself to her eyes.

“Really?”

“Mm.”

Irene looked down, tidying the small dish in front of her, a faint smile in her face.

She hesitated — then went on: “Some time ago, we met, too.”

The moment that was out, Taeyeon went still.

“You met recently?”

“Ya — your secret-keeping is really well done.”

Irene laughed at the overblown reaction. “I wasn’t keeping it on purpose, you know.”

She looked up, a measure of seriousness in it. “I’m just telling Unnie — now.”

The corner of her mouth curved, lightly.

“So Unnie is the second person to know. Keep it for me, all right.”

Taeyeon nodded along at once — and, perfectly serious, raised her hand, as if sealing a formal promise.

“Don’t worry.”

Her tone was solemn.

“Heaven knows, earth knows, you know, I know — and he knows.”

II. Earth knows.

III. You know.

IV. I know.

V. And he knows.

Witness: the second person to know. Sealed with one raised hand and zero paperwork.

Irene’s smile went deeper.

“Unnie, you sound like you’re exchanging classified intel.”

“That’s exactly what it is.”

She said it with total straightness — then could not help pulling the thread back.

“But honestly, I’m curious now.”

Chin propped, she watched Irene; the probing in her eyes was obvious.

“So — what kind of person is he, in the end?”

A pause. Her voice lighter, and straighter.

“I can tell, even now, you still carry him in your head. Don’t you?”

Irene had reached for a finished slice; her hand stopped, a little, at the words.

A familiar face surfaced in her mind almost against her will — the outline of it so clear it was as if she had seen him yesterday.

Irene lowered her eyes; the faintest smile, uninvited, at the corner of her mouth.

“Face and figure — that part goes without saying.”

At that, Taeyeon raised a brow.

“Oh?”

Irene, as if not registering the look, went on, slowly.

“Personality — very calm. Very restrained, too.”

“He isn’t the kind to wear his care on his lips. He doesn’t say many pretty things.”

“But in the moments you don’t expect, you feel the warmth he gives.”

The teasing had been sitting on Taeyeon’s face; it left. She went quiet, for real.

Because the way Irene said it — the face, the voice — was too earnest.

You could see, in one glance, that this person had been kept in her mind all along.

Irene went on.

“When I was still a trainee, he gave me a great deal of encouragement.”

“I could say it plainly: that I kept going afterward, and walked forward, all the way to today, without turning back — he is a very big part of the reason.”

The trainee years · as kept

The years before the stage, when the stage was still a rumor.

In those years, one voice, given often enough to become a fact: keep going.

The way the griddle is a fact, the heat is a fact.

The result, filed: she walked forward, all the way to today. He is part of the reason.

The name is not in the memory. The warmth is.

When that landed, Taeyeon looked at her, and a little envy surfaced in her eyes.

“That’s really something.” She said it softly, a nearly-there smile at her lips.

“To have someone like that with you, supporting you, in the trainee years — that’s really something good.”

Then, almost at herself, she laughed.

“Unlike me — still a lone wolf, to this day.”

Irene laughed, softly, at that.

“How can Unnie be a lone wolf.”

Her voice, gentle.

“Unnie is this excellent. Someday, you’ll certainly meet someone very excellent too.”

Taeyeon laughed once — then sighed, resigned.

“It’s hard.”

“There aren’t that many good men out there.”

Irene did not pick that up. She looked down and smiled.

Taeyeon, clearly, was not done asking. The curiosity she had for this person was growing, by the minute.

“Oh — right.” She looked at Irene again. “Is he still in Seoul, now?”

“Mm.”

“And what does he do?”

Asking it, Taeyeon was only simply curious.

After all, from the way Irene had described him, the person in question could not possibly be so ordinary as to leave nothing to remember.

But Irene, hearing the question, stopped — a rare pause.

She seemed to be weighing whether the answer, spoken out loud, would be giving too much away.

Then, on second thought — a profession, it seemed, was not exactly a secret that could not be named.

So, at last, she spoke.

“A prosecutor.”

Taeyeon blinked. “Hm?” The next second, something lit in her eyes. “A prosecutor?”

“That’s a wonderful profession.”

The one answer the room could not have priced: the most expensive one on the table.

For most people in Korea, the word prosecutor already carried weight.

Dignity, seriousness, and a gravity that could not be ignored — the profession came with them by nature.

Taeyeon understood, plainly, what the profession meant. The interest in her went up another gear.

“Which prosecutor’s office?”

She asked it, watching her, as she asked it.

The same title, in different offices, did not carry the same weight. Everyone knew that.

Which was why the curiosity had grown, exactly.

Seeing how far the question had gone, Irene went on.

“He graduated from the law department at Seoul National University.”

A pause. The addition, more specific.

“Top of his class.”

“So after the internship that followed graduation, he went straight into the Seoul Central District Prosecutor’s Office.”

That was the end of the sentence.

The tongs in Taeyeon’s hand hung in the air. Several seconds passed before she came back.

“Wait.”

She looked at Irene; the look was no longer curiosity. It was shock, solid.

“Seoul National law, top of the class — and then, directly into the Seoul Central District Prosecutor’s Office?”

Irene nodded.

“Mm.”

The file, as rebuilt in the room

The credentialsSeoul National law — top of the class

The postingthe Seoul Central District Prosecutor’s Office — the office with the weight

The availabilitysingle, per her — the one fact that changed the temperature of the room

The verdicta different category — the word “excellent” retired from service

Filed by the captain. For no one. For everything.

Taeyeon could not decide which part to marvel at first.

A prosecutor was already not a profession anyone could simply do — and that one, above all.

It had passed the point where “very excellent” could do the summing up.

She did not know every layer inside the legal system, but she understood clearly enough: a record like that meant the person had started, at the very start, from a very high place.

A small place and Seoul were never the same concept to begin with.

The Seoul Central District Prosecutor’s Office — the weight of that, on top of everything.

Taeyeon could not help drawing a soft breath.

“Joo-hyun-ah.”

“The person you like — he really is extraordinary.”

Irene did not deny it. She only smiled.

Taeyeon leaned back against the chair, as if re-filing what she had just heard.

If that person never made a mistake, and stayed as excellent as he was, the height he would reach, in time, would probably far exceed what ordinary people imagined.

Forget ordinary people — probably even many of the so-called big names of the entertainment world, in front of a person like him, would have to be polite.

Their chairman, for instance — Lee Soo-man, of SM Entertainment—...

Taeyeon watched Irene for a while, digesting, slowly, in her head.

The more she thought, the more complicated her face became.

A moment passed. Something had come to her; the corner of her mouth rose, slowly, into a sly smile.

Irene noticed the change, and, for no reason she could name, a not-very-good feeling surfaced in her.

“Unnie?”

Taeyeon did not answer at once. She simply watched her, all smiles.

“Joo-hyun-ah.”

“Hm?”

Irene looked at her, puzzled.

Taeyeon set her chopsticks down, leaned in a little, and her voice, suddenly, went close.

“Unnie wants to talk you into something, all right?”

Irene blinked.

“What?”

The smile in Taeyeon’s eyes grew more visible.

“He’s still single, I take it?”

Irene went a little still — she had not seen this one coming.

“...Yes.” She answered, hesitating.

At the answer, something lit in Taeyeon’s eyes.

“Since he turned you down back then — how about I have a turn?”

Irene did not catch it, at first. She watched Taeyeon, a little lost.

“Try what?”

“What else could I be trying? Introduce him to me.”

The more Taeyeon said it, the better the idea seemed. She even began to analyze it, seriously.

“Think about it — a man this excellent is how rare, exactly.”

“A good face, a good figure, a good personality, a good education, a good job — and a prosecutor, at that.”

“To let a person like that slip away, like that — wouldn’t that be a waste?”

Irene opened her mouth — and could not find, at first, what to say.

Taeyeon went on.

“He’s single, anyway. And you said the two of you are just friends.”

She looked at her, smiling — the words half a joke, and yet with one thread of real seriousness in them.

“Better we keep him in the family, among us sisters, than let someone who doesn’t know the value of him get him later.”

Irene came back to herself, at last.

“Ah—”

“Introduce him to you?”

Taeyeon nodded.

“Exactly.”

“Didn’t you say the two of you are on good terms now? A little introduction shouldn’t be hard, right? And what if I win him over?”

Irene looked at the small dish in front of her.

The way Taeyeon said it sounded like a joke. And yet, for no reason, a strange feeling had risen in Irene.

As if something she had kept, carefully, in a corner of herself, had suddenly been seen by someone else.

She knew, perfectly well, that Bae Do-yoon had never been hers to own.

She had no right to stop anyone from going near him — and even less right to show displeasure over a single joke.

But when the thought — introducing Bae Do-yoon to Taeyeon — surfaced in her mind, something in her, without consulting her, went uncomfortable.

Taeyeon watched her go quiet, and the smile in her eyes paused, a little.

As if she had felt something, she dragged her voice out, on purpose.

“What is it?”

She watched her, half smile, half not.

“You don’t want to part with him, do you?”

You don’t want to part with him— (half a smile, half not; the question left hanging, the way a hand is left raised)

A tease aimed at the exact place. The room’s verdict: it landed.

Something in Irene’s chest gave a light beat.

“It’s not—”

The denial came without her deciding to; her voice was not as steady as usual.

Taeyeon raised a brow; the look on her face became more worth reading.

Irene, aware that her own reaction had shown, gave a small cough and worked her voice back to natural.

“It’s just that it came a little suddenly, I think.”

“Suddenly?”

Taeyeon laughed.

“I think it’s fine.”

Chin in her hands, she went on teasing her, on purpose light.

“Didn’t you say he’s wonderful? If he’s that good, letting Unnie meet him can’t hurt.”

Irene pressed her lower lip.

Of course she knew Taeyeon was teasing her.

Only — this thing was not entirely impossible, either.

Taeyeon was beautiful, excellent, mature, with presence — and she, too, had been through a great many things.

If she were truly interested in Bae Do-yoon, a person like her might very well move first.

And Bae Do-yoon, then?

Would he like a woman like Taeyeon?

She did not know.

The moment the thought was out, that strange feeling in her had grown, a little more, obvious.

Seeing her this way, Taeyeon could not help laughing.

“Ya, Joo-hyun-ah.”

“That face you’re making right now — it’s very interesting.”

Irene went a little rigid.

“Unnie, what face?”

She said it, all smiles.

“Like you don’t want to give him up — but you’re too embarrassed to say so. Wouldn’t you agree?”

Irene, read to the core of it, felt her cheeks go warm at once.

“Unnie—”

The call came out of her, helpless.

“Unnie — it’s not that I don’t want to part with him.”

Taeyeon blinked.

“Then what is it?”

Irene paused; her face had grown complicated.

“It’s just—”

She looked up at Taeyeon, her voice as tactful as she could make it.

“He probably doesn’t much like women of Unnie’s type.”

Taeyeon went still for a moment.

“Ah?”

She had clearly not expected the answer.

She was not angry, though. In fact, the laughter came quickly — as if the sentence were more interesting than the refusal itself.

“Is that so?”

Irene nodded; her face a little more serious than before.

Taeyeon raised a brow at her.

“How do you know that?”

Irene thought of an old conversation, with Bae Do-yoon; the corner of her mouth curved, without asking.

“I once made a joke with him, a long time ago.”

“What kind of joke?”

“I told him — if he’s still single forever, would it be all right if I introduced the pretty women of the company to him, since our company has a lot of them, after all.”

Hearing that, Taeyeon’s interest came back, at once.

“And then?”

Irene looked at her, a little helpless in her voice.

“And he said—”

Taeyeon leaned in, a little, and pressed.

“What did he say?”

Irene stopped.

For no reason — it was only a joke, retold from long ago, and yet, said under a look like Taeyeon’s, it had become, somehow, strange.

She pressed her lips, lightly — and, at last, told the truth.

“He said — he likes a girl like Yoonah-Ooni.”

On the type he likes

The chapter’s last line is a joke retold — and it lands like a fact. The captain who ran the interrogation, who rebuilt the file, who raised her hand over the oath of five witnesses, gets her answer at the very end, and the answer is not a person. It is a type. Yoonah-Ooni’s type. A type is a direction, and a direction is a road; what the room does not yet know, the reader may already have started to follow.

The secret sworn on heaven and earth, on two women and one absent man, now has its second keeper. The griddle is still hot, the meat is still turning, and the small dish holds everything of value a little at a time, without a sound. This is the book’s economy for the chapter: the confession was old news, the refusal was old news, and even the prosecutor’s office was, by the end, almost old news — the only thing that arrived fresh was the type, at the door, with the meat still sizzling behind it.
