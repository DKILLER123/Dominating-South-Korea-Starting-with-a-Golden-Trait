Chapter Twenty-Two

Rekindled?

The same night · the side door

Basement one’s other exit · hat, mask, and work mode restored

Where a tongue-out gesture escapes before work mode finishes loading

The night deepened by degrees.

When Irene saw Bae Do-yoon out, she had already put her hat and mask back on.

Just now, in the rest room, she had been hugging a cushion on the sofa, letting slip from time to time small moods that were neither the leader’s nor an artist’s.

But the moment she reached the door, she seemed to switch back into work mode, flawlessly pressing the hat brim low, pulling the mask a little higher, until only a pair of eyes remained showing.

Bae Do-yoon stood beside her watching, and could not help a laugh. “So fast?”

Irene tilted her head to look at him. “What?”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his chin, gesturing at the way she had wrapped herself up so thoroughly.

“Just now you knew me. The moment we step outside, you don’t know me again?”

Irene blinked; and then, very lightly, she put out the tip of her tongue at him.

“I can’t help it either.”

Her voice, muffled by the mask, came out a little stuffy — and yet could not hide one small note of pride.

“Who made me a popular artist right now? I have to watch out for the tabloids at all times.”

Having said it, she deliberately pressed the hat brim down a little further, as if her professional conscience were truly exemplary.

Bae Do-yoon watched the motion; he had meant to answer with some remark, but the words stopped a moment at his lips.

This side of her was too rare.

On stage, before lenses, Irene was always beautiful, proper, composed.

And yet that small tongue-out gesture of just now carried a childishness that only someone familiar would ever be shown.

Bae Do-yoon suddenly thought: she is probably not like this in front of everyone.

So his smile grew more obvious. “An old friend of a popular artist — sounds rather high-risk.”

Irene nodded, perfectly straight-faced. “So you should have self-awareness.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “For instance?”

Irene glanced once at the outside, and said in a small voice: “For instance — when walking, stay a little farther from me.”

Bae Do-yoon, very cooperatively, shifted one step to the side.

Irene: …

She looked at the distance that had opened between them all at once, eyes going slightly wide.

“Not that far.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her again. “Is the safe-distance standard for you popular artists really so hard to calibrate?”

Irene’s ears heated; she said, with perfect legitimacy: “It is inherently hard.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Then I apply for a written explanation.”

“There isn’t one.”

“A verbal one will do.”

Irene glared at him once. “Bae Do-yoon-ssi, you talk a great deal now.”

Bae Do-yoon laughed softly.

“I thought old friends held the right to ask questions.”

“They do,” Irene said, pushing open the side door; night wind slipped in from beyond it. “But the popular artist holds the final right of interpretation.”

Having said it, she stepped out first.

Bae Do-yoon followed behind her, the corner of his lips never quite pressed down.

Beyond the side door was a not-very-wide little alley. On a weekend night the neighborhood was much quieter than usual.

Irene walked a little ahead, looking left and right in a properly businesslike way.

She had no schedule today; coming out to meet Bae Do-yoon had been decided on impulse. By rights her manager should already have gone home to rest at this hour.

Even so, she kept an artist’s alertness by instinct.

Bae Do-yoon did not walk too close.

He had teased her with his mouth just now, but once truly outside he kept a distance unlikely to draw notice.

Irene walked two steps, and could not help looking back at him.

Bae Do-yoon caught her line of sight.

“What is it?”

Irene turned back at once.

“Nothing.”

Bae Do-yoon did not press her.

But the less he pressed, the more Irene felt a small itch in her chest.

A few steps more, and she could not help looking back again.

This time Bae Do-yoon simply stopped walking.

“Joo-hyun-ssi.”

Irene stopped too, and blinked.

“What?”

Bae Do-yoon regarded her.

“You have looked back at me twice now.”

Irene said at once: “That is because I am checking behind us for paparazzi.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at himself. “So I am the paparazzi?”

Irene paused a moment; then she nodded, very seriously.

“It is not impossible.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted a brow.

“And what would I be shooting candid photographs of you for?”

Irene thought about it; then she lowered her voice, as if reading out a news headline:

Tabloid headline · recited aloud in a lowered voice by its supposed subject · the alley, tonight

Top female idol in midnight secret tryst with mystery man; the two behave intimately; old flame, apparently, rekindled.

Recited to the end, the reciter grew embarrassed first at her own last words, her eyes drifting lightly aside. The accused party’s evaluation of the copy: the paparazzi’s imagination is not bad. The reciter’s correction: this is the sort of headline tabloids scribble at will, and does not represent fact. The accused party’s counter-correction, offered as professional habit and an analysis of wording, was ruled by the reciter to be really annoying.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “Old flame rekindled?”

Irene explained at once: “That is the kind of headline the tabloids scribble at will. It does not represent fact.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“The paparazzi’s imagination is not bad.”

Irene glared at him.

“You are still evaluating it?”

“Professional habit. Analyzing wording.”

“This professional habit of yours is really annoying.”

Bae Do-yoon gave a laugh.

“Then next time I will analyze a little less.”

Irene muttered, in a small voice:

“You say that every time.”

Bae Do-yoon heard it, and turned his head to look at her.

“I was like this before, too?”

Irene’s steps paused, faintly.

Before…

After a few seconds she gave a light hmph.

“More or less.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her profile and did not press further.

Minutes later · the mouth of the alley

One familiar black business van · its lights flashing once, as if someone unlocked it

Where the intelligence department fails and the evasion protocol begins

They went on toward the alley mouth; a short way out from the side door, Bae Do-yoon stopped.

“I’ll see you off to here.”

Irene stopped too.

Bae Do-yoon glanced at her hat brim, swaying a little in the night wind, and said:

“It’s cold outside. Go back in.”

Irene lifted a brow.

Just that?

She had thought he would at least say something more. Message me when you get home, perhaps; or be careful next time; or failing everything else, even one question — when shall we meet again? — would have done.

And yet this person only told her to go back in.

Irene suddenly wanted a little to laugh, and felt a little unreconciled.

Slow-burn type.

No.

Super slow-burn type.

She had just opened her mouth when her peripheral vision suddenly caught a familiar black business van parked at the alley mouth.

Irene froze, whole body, in an instant.

She blinked, as if not quite believing it, and stealthily poked her head out for one more look.

The next second she snatched herself back.

Bae Do-yoon noticed the change in her. “What is it?”

Irene did not answer at once.

She lowered her voice: “Don’t move.”

Bae Do-yoon followed her line of sight.

“That car?”

Irene nodded, her voice so small it seemed afraid of being carried off by the wind:

“My manager’s car.”

Bae Do-yoon paused a moment.

“Didn’t you say he had gone home to rest?”

Irene turned her head to glare at him, her voice urgent and low:

“I said I thought so!”

Bae Do-yoon looked once at the alley mouth, then at her.

“Your intelligence department is not very reliable.”

Irene was so urgent she nearly stamped her foot. “Now is not the time for assigning blame!”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Then is now the time for fleeing?”

Irene gritted her teeth: “More or less!”

Just then, out in the distance, the business van’s lights flashed once, as if someone had unlocked it.

Irene’s breath caught.

The next second, almost by instinct, she reached out and seized Bae Do-yoon’s sleeve, dragging him sideways.

Bae Do-yoon, pulled, stumbled a small step forward.

“Wait a moment…”

“Shut up.”

Irene dragged him back behind a narrow wall corner beside the side door.

There stood a row of vending machines and old storage shelves, enough to block the line of sight from the alley mouth — only the space was truly not wide. Once two people stood inside it, there was almost no room left to turn.

Irene put her back against the wall; Bae Do-yoon stood before her, slightly to one side.

The distance between them became, all at once, too close for reason. Irene could smell the very faint clean coldness on him; if she lifted her head a little she could see Bae Do-yoon’s lowered lashes.

06Reconnaissance through machine-wall gap: door opened, yes; approaching, not yet; breathing resumed, sleeve grip maintained

Intelligence department rated unreliable (forecast: manager at home; actual: manager at alley mouth). Accountability postponed until such time as the operatives get out alive.

Bae Do-yoon looked down at the sleeve she had seized, then at her.

“Are you sure this is hiding and not kidnapping?”

Irene lowered her voice: “Speak more quietly.”

Bae Do-yoon lowered his voice too: “I am already speaking quietly.”

“More quietly still.”

“Then I may have to use breath-voice…”

Irene glared at him. “Bae Do-yoon-ssi!”

Bae Do-yoon nodded at once. “Good. I’ll shut up.”

He truly went quiet for two seconds. Two seconds later, he said again, in a low voice:

“But this secret operation of yours has a few too many holes.”

Irene was nearly laughed into anger. “Didn’t you say you would shut up?”

Bae Do-yoon looked very innocent.

“I am only issuing a risk advisory.”

“No advisories now.”

“Then when do I advise?”

“After we get out alive.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her face — serious as if she were truly executing a life-and-death mission — and could not help one low laugh.

Irene at once stretched out one finger and tapped his sleeve with it, warningly.

“No laughing.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at her finger. “Your way of threatening people is quite cute.”

Irene stilled; her ears went hot in an instant. She drew the finger back at once and pretended not to have heard.

Outside: a car door opens. Inside: two people, one seized sleeve, and a silence enforced at whisper volume.

Outside came the sound of a car door opening. Irene held her breath at once and started to poke her head out a little toward the alley mouth.

Bae Do-yoon raised a hand and loosely shielded her hat brim.

“Don’t poke out.”

Irene drew back and asked, in a small voice: “Did he get out?”

Bae Do-yoon tilted his head slightly and looked out through the gap between the vending machine and the wall.

“Mm.”

“Is he coming this way?”

“Not yet.”

Irene let out a breath — but her fingers still gripped his sleeve tightly.

Bae Do-yoon looked down once.

“The strength of your grip on my sleeve is more persuasive than your saying just now that you weren’t nervous.”

Only then did Irene realize she had been holding onto him all along.

She let go at once.

But once released, her hand felt empty, and the tension, on the contrary, grew more obvious.

So she hesitated a moment, and then pinched, very lightly, one corner of Bae Do-yoon’s sleeve with two fingers.

Bae Do-yoon watched the small motion.

Irene pretended not to see his look, and explained in a small voice: “This is to prevent you from suddenly walking out.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “Why would I suddenly walk out?”

Irene said, with perfect legitimacy: “Who knows whether you slow-burn types might suddenly short-circuit?”

Bae Do-yoon: …

He was silent a moment.

“Slow-burn and short-circuit do not seem to be problems of the same system.”

Irene said, in a small voice: “Anyway — don’t move.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at her and could not help a laugh.

“Good. I won’t move.”

The space was too narrow.

Irene could feel Bae Do-yoon’s breath, when he spoke, falling lightly against the side of her face.

Very light, very short, carrying warmth.

Her heartbeat turned suddenly violent.

Once, and again.

As if it meant to burst its way out of her chest.

Outside there was still her manager; now of all moments she ought to be tense about being discovered. And yet every bit of her attention landed, uncontrollably, on Bae Do-yoon.

He was too close.

If she leaned forward only a little more, her forehead would touch his jaw.

Irene raised her eyes without meaning to; Bae Do-yoon was also looking down at her.

The corner’s light was dim, but his gaze was very clear.

It rested on her face — quiet, and focused.

Irene’s breath disordered, lightly.

She suddenly remembered when she had first known Bae Do-yoon: he had said one thing, very solemnly.

He had said he was a slow-burn type.

Slow-burn type · a declaration from the first meeting

She had nearly failed to keep from laughing on the spot. It was the first time she had heard a man describe himself that way. Wasn’t that a sentence girls kept in their mouths for refusing blind dates, or for explaining why they never made the first move?

And yet placed on Bae Do-yoon, it was inexplicably reasonable. He truly was slow-burn: the atmosphere inside had grown as thick as it had, and out the door he could still say only it’s cold outside, go back in. Now, hidden in a corner this narrow, he could still observe the manager with one eye and seriously evaluate the holes in her secret operation with the other.

Slow to the point of — Her thought stopped there, because the slow-burn type appeared to be closing the last few centimeters on his own.

Because she discovered that Bae Do-yoon seemed to be drawing nearer to her, bit by bit.

Not obviously — only his silhouette a little closer than before, his breath nearer.

Irene could feel her ear tips begin to burn.

Bae Do-yoon’s gaze fell, as if past her eyes, onto the side of her face.

Irene froze, whole body.

No way.

Wasn’t he a slow-burn type?

Do slow-burn types also launch a sudden attack at moments like this?

Her mind went into complete chaos; her heartbeat grew louder and louder.

Bae Do-yoon leaned in a little more; Irene held her breath by instinct.

She even made her readiness — slowly closing her eyes, her lashes trembling lightly.

The surroundings were extremely quiet.

There was only the manager’s voice on his phone in the distance, and her own nearly uncontrollable heartbeat.

Bae Do-yoon’s motion paused for an instant.

He had originally only seen a little spiderweb caught beside her hat brim, and meant to take it off for her.

But Irene had suddenly closed her eyes.

Her lashes trembled lightly; she was plainly tense beyond bearing, and yet she did not move away.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her; his throat moved, very lightly. For one second he too could not move his line of sight away at once.

He restrained himself.

Outside there was still her manager; the alley mouth might have passersby at any moment; between the two of them it should not happen this hastily…

And… on his own side there was one large trouble…

Lee Bu-jin…

He was afraid of dragging Irene into his own trouble.

So Bae Do-yoon endured; and in the end he only reached out, his fingertips touching the ends of her hair lightly.

Not a kiss.

He brushed aside that strand of hair by her hat brim, tangled with spider silk, and flicked it away lightly with his fingertip.

Irene opened her eyes, somewhat puzzled.

Bae Do-yoon withdrew his hand and explained:

“There was a spiderweb.”

…

Irene looked at Bae Do-yoon, blank.

A moment later, a half-smiling, not-quite-smiling meaning surfaced in Bae Do-yoon’s eyes.

Irene went embarrassed in an instant.

He had seen it. He had absolutely seen her close her eyes just now.

Mortification, disbelief — several emotions surged up at once.

She lowered her voice and asked, almost biting the words: “Just now… was it only because of the spiderweb?”

Bae Do-yoon’s tone was very innocent. “What else would it be?”

Irene stared at that smile in his eyes, and anger rose in her at once.

Without any thought at all, she reached out and pinched Bae Do-yoon’s thigh, hard.

Bae Do-yoon, caught utterly off guard, very nearly drew a cold breath straight in.

He forced the sound back down into his throat; his brow furrowed obviously, and his whole body stiffened for an instant.

“Joo-hyun.” His voice was pressed very low, carrying a little disbelief. “Isn’t your hand a bit too ruthless?”

Irene looked at his face, twisted with pain, and at his not-daring-to-make-a-sound appearance; the mortification in her heart finally balanced a little.

She said, in a small voice: “Who told you to laugh at me?”

Bae Do-yoon glanced down at his leg. “I think I need an injury examination.”

Irene rebutted at once: “You are a prosecutor, not a staged-accident fraudster.”

“But right now I am the victim.”

“The victim please maintain silence.”

Bae Do-yoon laughed in exasperation. “Fine. You are very good at using legal terms.”

Irene’s eyes curved.

“Learned from the prosecutor.”

Bae Do-yoon could not help saying, in a low voice:

“Right now you really don’t look like a popular artist.”

Irene stilled.

“Then what do I look like?”

Bae Do-yoon thought about it. “Like a child whose secret has been seen through, and who is mortified-angry about it.”

… Irene reached out at once.

Bae Do-yoon this time pressed his own leg down in advance.

Irene said, in a small urgent voice: “You still dare say it?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded at once: “I don’t dare.”

“You plainly dare.”

“Now I don’t dare.”

Irene hmph’d.

She wanted to put on an imposing air; but because her voice was pressed so low, it sounded instead rather like petulant coquetry.

She realized it herself, at once cleared her throat, and worked to straighten her face.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her: “Which thing?”

Irene’s eyes narrowed.

Bae Do-yoon corrected himself at once: “I forgot.”

Irene was a little satisfied.

But two seconds later she was not quite reassured; she leaned a little closer and confirmed in a small voice: “Truly forgot?”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at her.

“Starting now, I forget.”

…

She wanted to pinch him again.

Starting now, I forget.

On slow-burn types, spiderwebs, and other pretexts

A slow-burn type closes the last few centimeters at exactly the speed of a man removing a spiderweb, and stops at exactly the point where a closed pair of eyes makes honesty dangerous. He had a manager at the alley mouth, a passersby’s alley, and a large trouble of his own wearing a very powerful name — three excellent reasons, and not one of them the reason his fingertip only flicked silk from her hat brim. She pinched his thigh for the smile he did not quite hide. The file on tonight will be opened, reviewed, and forgotten starting now, in perpetuity, by order of the popular artist, who holds the final right of interpretation and, as of the corner, two fingers’ worth of sleeve.
