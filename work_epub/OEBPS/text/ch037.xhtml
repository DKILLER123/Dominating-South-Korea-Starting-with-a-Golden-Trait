The Girl Group About to Debut

“The plan is roughly this.”

Having said the last of the details, Bae Do-yoon closed the notebook and looked at Bae Suzy again.

“If there’s nothing else on your side, that’s all for today.”

Only then did Bae Suzy seem to come back from somewhere inside her own thoughts. She blinked, moved her eyes off his face, and was quickly, ordinarily herself again.

“Mm, nothing.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded, got up, and put the phone and the notebook away.

“When the photos are ready, send them straight to me. I’ll run the account on the plan; if anything changes, I’ll contact you.”

“Okay.”

Bae Suzy got up after him, her tone a shade softer.

“Then — Prosecutor Bae, Miss Kim. Careful on the way.”

A pause, then the explanation: “I have practice in a bit, so I won’t see you down.”

Kim Mi-ju smiled and nodded.

“It’s fine, Miss Bae — just go to your practice.”

Bae Do-yoon did not mind either way.

“Mm. If anything comes up, call.”

And the two of them left the meeting room.

The case, the exit, and the collision

The staff were already waiting outside, ahead, to lead them out through the less exposed passage. But they had not made it out of JYP when Bae Do-yoon noticed that Kim Mi-ju, beside him, was off.

Her face was wrong. Lips pressed hard, shoulders tight — someone holding something in by main force.

Bae Do-yoon glanced at her and stopped.

“What’s wrong?”

Kim Mi-ju stopped with him and looked up.

Bae Do-yoon frowned.

“Not feeling well?”

Kim Mi-ju’s face had gone slightly red, as if she had already reached the limit of holding it in; even her words came out hard.

“No… it’s not that.”

Lips pressed, her voice low.

“It’s just…”

He watched her.

Kim Mi-ju took a deep breath, and finally squeezed out one line:

“I can’t hold it in anymore…”

“…”

Bae Do-yoon was silent.

Then his expression turned, a little, to out-of-his-depth.

“Then why don’t you hurry to the toilet?”

Looking at her, on the edge of collapse, his voice carried a grain of exasperation.

“Do you want me to carry you?”

Hearing that, the red on Kim Mi-ju’s face went a shade more obvious. She shook her head fast — no one could have said whether she was trying to explain, or simply denying the being-carried part.

The next second she turned and ran, quick, down the other side of the corridor.

Fast enough to vanish in a streak.

Bae Do-yoon stood where he was, watching the back disappear round the corner, and let out one quiet sigh.

The staff had wanted to keep leading the way; Bae Do-yoon raised a hand in a small gesture.

“No rush. Wait for her to come back.”

The staff, naturally, did not ask anything; they simply stepped aside first, with good sense.

So Bae Do-yoon stayed in the corridor, waiting.

This JYP building had seen a few years. It was cleaned well, and the corridors carried a good number of artists’ promotional posters — but the walls, the floor tiles, some of the corner fittings: a little of the age still showed in them.

Compared with the glittering entertainment company the outside imagined, the place carried, instead, a few parts of lived-in life.

Bae Do-yoon leaned against one side of the corridor and let his eyes run, idly, over the posters on the wall.

And just then, from the other side of the corner, came a burst of hurried footsteps.

Then, a call, frayed at the edges:

“Mina!”

“Slow down — wait for us!”

The sounds had barely reached his ears when Bae Do-yoon turned his head, on instinct, toward the corner.

He had only just turned when a figure shot out of it, straight and fast.

She had obviously been running hard — no time, no thought, for who might be ahead.

The next second, she hit Bae Do-yoon, solidly, in the chest.

Thud.

The impact was not large — but it was sudden.

Bae Do-yoon was tall, and he stood steady. Her speed was no slow thing, but this much was not going to take his balance. His shoulder gave a small sway, and he was firm again.

The one who had run into him, on the other hand, had not reacted at all — as if she had met a solid wall of muscle head-on.

Her body went back, her feet lost the floor, and she sat down on the ground in one piece.

“Ah…”

A very light cry of pain with it.

Bae Do-yoon looked down.

The girl on the ground wore practice clothes; her long hair hung a little disheveled at her shoulders; her face was in panic. She seemed stunned by the hit — two seconds on the floor — and only then slowly lifted her head.

Bae Do-yoon, in the same moment, finally saw her face.

He bent his head, just a little, toward the girl on the ground, and asked one line:

“Are you okay?”

The one on the ground was a very young girl. That fall had clearly not been light; she had not quite come back to herself — one hand down, instinctively, on her bottom, teeth gritted with the pain, her whole face scrunched.

And even so, she forced herself up from the floor, fast.

“I’m fine, I’m fine…”

Saying it, she looked — by reflex, first of all — at Bae Do-yoon, her voice carrying tension and apology together.

“Are you all right?”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at her once.

Once the girl was standing, she finally lifted her head fully.

And stopped, clearly, a beat.

Only now, at this moment, was she truly seeing what the person she had just hit looked like.

Bae Do-yoon: tall, features cold and sharp, bearing clean and crisp — standing there, the presence of someone who was not, naturally, an ordinary staff member.

And this was JYP’s interior, to begin with. By instinct, the girl had sorted him into some senior artist of the company.

Bae Do-yoon did not think much of it, and answered, faintly: “I’m fine.”

Hearing it, the girl seemed to let out a breath — then her tension came back, more. She stepped back half a step and gave Bae Do-yoon a real, proper bow.

“Senior, I’m so terribly sorry.”

Head down, her voice full of sincerity.

“I was running too fast, and I bumped into you.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, his brow giving one almost-imperceptible move.

Senior?

Could this girl… have taken me for a JYP artist?

With that thought, he found he did not correct her, at once.

At the same moment, the other girls chasing from behind the corner caught up.

They had only arrived when they saw the girl who had done the hitting standing in front of the man, head lowered, apologizing with complete seriousness — a scene that looked, in its way, odd.

The group froze, a beat — then opened their mouths, all at once, seven ways.

“What are you all doing?”

“Oh — Mina, what’s wrong?”

“Did you fall?”

“Mina, you’re not hurt?”

The girl called Mina straightened up at last, a flush on her face from the pain and the panic together; she waved her hands fast — nothing serious.

But the way she looked at Bae Do-yoon stayed reserved; she was clearly not yet back from the shock of having hit a “senior.”

And the girls around her, at this point, finally let their eyes land on Bae Do-yoon.

One look — and their faces went subtle with it. Surprise, or puzzlement. They clearly did not know who he was either; but by the same instinct, they had filed him into the “senior of the company” category.

Just as the air had settled into its subtle pause, footsteps came from the other end of the corridor.

And Park Jin-young, walked over with his assistant, came into the group’s line of sight.

He had apparently only been passing. His first eyes took in the girls in the corridor, and his brow drew together, on instinct.

“You all, what’s going on with you…”

Halfway through the line, his gaze passed over them and landed on Bae Do-yoon.

The next second, Park Jin-young’s face changed, visibly.

“Mr… Bae!”

He had swallowed the address that was almost out, almost by force.

The “Prosecutor Bae” that had nearly come out was changed, at the final moment, into the steadier “Mr. Bae.”

And still — the pause inside that one second of tone made everyone present blink, on instinct.

Especially Mina and the girls around her. They looked at Park Jin-young, then at Bae Do-yoon, and went visibly more lost.

What Park Jin-young was, they knew better than anyone.

The person the president greeted like that was clearly not going to be some ordinary senior artist.

Bae Do-yoon, for his part, was as usual; he only nodded, calmly.

“President Park.”

Park Jin-young came over quickly. His eyes rested on Mina a moment, then ran over the scene — he had plainly already guessed what had happened.

“This is…”

Mina went tense at once, and hurried to explain again.

“President, I’m sorry — I was running too fast, and I bumped into this senior… ah, no, this gentleman.”

Halfway through, she had caught the wrong address herself; her voice dropped with it, and her face went more embarrassed still.

Hearing it, the corner of Park Jin-young’s mouth twitched once.

Senior?

He looked at Bae Do-yoon, on instinct, his expression a little complicated. This misunderstanding, he thought, had grown rather large.

Bae Do-yoon, on the other hand, stayed calm as ever; he gave Mina one sweep of his eyes and said, levelly:

“It’s nothing. She didn’t mean to.”

Hearing that, the weight over Mina’s chest finally dropped, a little.

Park Jin-young, in the same motion, nodded, and gave the girls one line first.

“That’s enough. Since you’re all fine, go to practice — don’t crowd here.”

The girls responded at once. But before they went, each of them stole another look at Bae Do-yoon.

Plainly all curious: this young man — the outstanding face, the cold bearing, the one the president himself greeted with formality — what exactly his identity was.

Once the girls were gone, the corridor was quiet again.

Park Jin-young glanced at the assistant at his side, then waved at the staff member who had been leading the way, over there.

“You all go about your work. No need to stay here.”

The assistant and the staff did not stay; they withdrew, with sense, and left the space to the two men.

Only then did Park Jin-young put his full attention back on Bae Do-yoon, a polite smile on his face.

“Prosecutor Bae — is it just you today?”

Bae Do-yoon shook his head.

“No.”

“Kim Mi-ju came too. She went to the toilet.”

“I see.”

Park Jin-young nodded, his expression relaxing a shade. He had thought Bae Do-yoon had come alone; hearing Kim Mi-ju was there too, it all seemed considerably more ordinary.

The two of them stood side by side along the corridor, and Park Jin-young drew the topic, in the natural motion, back onto business.

“Then — how’s things on Suzy’s side, now?”

Clearly, compared with that little incident just now, what he cared about more was the progress of the harassment case against Bae Suzy.

Bae Do-yoon gave the rough shape of the current arrangement, simply, without a detour.
