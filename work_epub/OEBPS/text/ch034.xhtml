Chapter Thirty-Four

Prosecutor Bae Must Love Irene

The Prosecutors’ Office · his office — the case that will not wait

Morning files, one phone at an angle nobody is supposed to see, and a comment section that has been watching back

The Bae Suzy harassment case, and the man who keeps almost following the victim

Bae Do-yoon had a great many things to handle every day, all of them mixed together and piled up, and he almost never had a truly free moment. But what sat in front of him right now, for the time being unable to be gone around, was still the Bae Suzy harassment case.

Compared to some cases that had already caused real harm, this one did not, on its face, look all that serious. From what had been grasped up to now, the harasser had lately done nothing more extreme than frequently leave messages for Bae Suzy on the social platform, send her private messages, and entangle her, maliciously.

Bae Do-yoon sat at the desk, his eyes and brows carrying no expression.

This kind of person was the most troublesome. The other party’s behavior had already caused Bae Suzy distress, but if the other party kept on hiding behind the network, doing the harassing with words that were half-ambiguous — disgusting, and yet not clearly, specifically illegal — then pushing the handling any further would require more evidence.

The Bae Suzy harassment case · the file as it stands

The charge, so farnone; the behavior: messages, private messages, malicious entanglement on the social platform — frequent, persistent, and nothing, yet, that crosses the line

The character of the manthe most troublesome kind: already causing distress, and still fully able to stand behind a network and do it

The languagehalf-ambiguous, disgusting, and not clearly illegal — the kind a charge cannot reach and indifference cannot dismiss

The requirementevidence; more of it, before anything else in the file is allowed to move

Thinking to here, Bae Do-yoon picked up his phone, opened the social platform, logged into the account, and tapped into Bae Suzy’s personal homepage.

Bae Suzy · the account, as it stands

Opened on a prosecutor’s phone, from a prosecutor’s account, for a case

The feed — the sky, shot on the fly; a coffee cup and a book; a selfie taken in a work break; bright, natural, a clean smile that looks as if the outside world’s filth has never once reached it

The other posts — the retouched endorsement images, the promotional shots, the event styling; mixed into the life photos, in the image she has always shown the public

The content of her account was not complex. Most of it was life photos. Sometimes it was the sky, shot on the fly; sometimes a coffee cup and a book; sometimes a selfie from a work break. In the photos she looked bright and natural, her smile clean, as if she would never be touched by those dirty emotions of the outside world. Of course, from time to time she also posted some brand-endorsement-related photos: retouched images, promotional shots, event styling, mixed together with the life photos, in line with the image she had always presented to the public.

Bae Do-yoon scrolled down a few pages, and his gaze quickly landed on the comment section.

According to what Bae Suzy had said before, the person harassing her had a great many accounts. At first she would still block them. But block one, and the other would very quickly switch to a new account and surface again. The names different, the avatars different, the tone almost the same. Like bugs in a gutter: crush one, and another crawls out from somewhere no one can point to. By the end, Bae Suzy had grown tired of it. She had simply stopped paying attention. Let the other party say whatever nonsense it wanted to say — she would treat it as unseen. That way of handling things was, saved effort, and it also kept her from sinking, again and again, into negative emotions.

He scrolled down a little further. Most of the comments were fans’ normal messages: praising her beauty, asking after how she was doing, or simply sending a few emoji. But among the comments that looked, all together, so lively, a few of them caught Bae Do-yoon’s attention quickly.

The comment section · the part that does not belong

floor 12

The sky photo is so clean. This is the kind of calm my feed needed.

floor 47

I’ve been watching your posts for a long time. You don’t have to answer — I know you see them.

floor 89

The new drama’s trailer is out. Can’t wait to see you in it.

floor 113

Some people can only post their lives. Some people can just live them. You are the second kind.

At first glance those messages were not intense — there was not even any obvious cursing or threat in them — but between the lines a feeling of discomfort seeped out. Like unilaterally declaring some kind of relationship, and like a reminder to Bae Suzy: that he had been watching her, the whole time.

Bae Do-yoon was still looking down at Bae Suzy’s social account when Kim Mi-ju walked over, holding a stack of materials. She had originally only meant to set the files on his desk, but out of the corner of her eye she caught a glimpse of the phone screen. On the screen was a woman’s photo. Only, because of the flip, she had not clearly seen who it was. Kim Mi-ju’s step paused, and a measure of teasing entered her eyes, at once. She set the materials at the edge of the desk and asked, with a smile: “Prosecutor Bae — looking at your girlfriend?”

Bae Do-yoon did not even lift his head. His finger was still slowly scrolling down the comments section, his tone calm and cold. “Why do you think I’m looking at a girlfriend?”

Kim Mi-ju, struck back by the counter-question, stilled a beat, and then raised an eyebrow.

“Oh? So it’s not a girlfriend, then?”

“Of course not.”

Bae Do-yoon said, lightly. “What girlfriend do I have?”

The sentence was said very naturally, without even a little pause. Kim Mi-ju had originally only meant to tease casually — but hearing this, her gaze moved of its own accord upward, and landed on Bae Do-yoon’s neck.

Hm?

The smile on her face paused a beat, and her eyes became, at once, a measure more subtle.

“If it’s not a girlfriend…”

Kim Mi-ju’s gaze paused at the place of Bae Do-yoon’s neck, her tone carrying a measure of meaningfulness.

“Then what is this on your neck?”

Bae Do-yoon’s finger, scrolling the screen, stopped.

Dang it.

He judged the situation, internally, to be not good. With so many things, he had actually forgotten this one. Just now he had said he did not have a girlfriend. Then the scratch marks on his neck — who, exactly, had made them?

Bae Do-yoon slowly raised his head, and sure enough saw Kim Mi-ju looking at him with a strange face. That expression was, on the contrary, not the kind of gossiping that was too obvious. A moment later, Kim Mi-ju, as if she had thought of something, gave a light smile.

“Prosecutor Bae — as your assigned clerk, I have to give you a reminder.”

Her tone sounded mild, but her eyes clearly carried a measure of teasing.

“Playing around outside is fine — but you’d better not get caught with a handle. If you do, it will be very troublesome.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. Kim Mi-ju must have misunderstood something, and he sighed, a little helplessly.

“Don’t worry. I’m not out hunting for fun.”

Kim Mi-ju neither affirmed nor denied, her gaze sweeping his neck once more. Bae Do-yoon paused a beat, and continued: “As for this on my neck… it’s a little complicated. I can’t tell you.”

Hearing this, the smile on Kim Mi-ju’s face became, on the contrary, more settled.

“Of course. That’s your private life, and I have no right to interfere.”

She tidied the materials in her hand and set them on one side of his desk, her tone returning to the usual capable and calm.

“Only, when necessary, I will give you a little advice — in case you go astray.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, and could not help smiling.

“Thank you.”

Kim Mi-ju gave a light nod, with an air of this is my duty.

Bae Do-yoon did not continue the explanation either. Some things, the more you explained, the more muddled they became — and moreover, he truly had no way to explain. He looked down at his phone, then flipped the screen over and held it out in front of Kim Mi-ju.

“I’m looking at Bae Suzy’s social account.”

Kim Mi-ju looked down. On the screen, it had stopped just below the latest post Bae Suzy had put out; below the photo was a dense thicket of comments. Bae Do-yoon said: “I want to see if there’s any breakthrough in here.”

The teasing in Kim Mi-ju’s face only then slowly converged, and she returned, again, to work mode.

“Then did you see anything?”

Bae Do-yoon shook his head.

“No clue yet.”

He took the phone back, his gaze landing on those messages in the comments section that looked ordinary but made one uncomfortable, and was silent a moment.

“Mm…”

He was as if thinking, and as if talking to himself.

“Do you think — why does one person have to harass Bae Suzy?”

Kim Mi-ju did not answer right away. Holding the materials, she stood at the edge of the desk and thought, seriously.

“Bae Suzy, as a top idol, is a Miss A member, and she has acted in many TV dramas as well — her name is very high.”

She analyzed, in a steady tone: “On top of that, she has always held the title ‘Nation’s First Love’; the fan base is very large. Many people like her, and many people watch her.”

Bae Do-yoon raised his eyes and looked at her.

Kim Mi-ju continued: “If I were her fan, normally, I might just want to support her, watch her, or hope she would see my message.”

She paused a beat, her tone sinking a little.

“But if it’s an abnormal fan — then the thought becomes another kind.”

Bae Do-yoon asked: “What kind?”

Kim Mi-ju looked at the phone screen and said, lightly: “To want to make her notice.”

“Even if it’s in a way that makes her uncomfortable.”

The fan · normal and abnormal, as the clerk analyzed it

Two branches, from the same root

Source: one standing clerk, a steady tone, and a phone screen between them

The normalto support, to watch, to hope the message is seen; the base of a top idol — Miss A, many dramas, the title “Nation’s First Love” — many who like, and many who watch

The abnormalthe attention has to be made to happen; the method for making it is not required to be comfortable

The line between the two branches is not drawn by love. It is drawn by whether the attention still counts when the other person has to be made uncomfortable for it to be delivered.

Hearing Kim Mi-ju’s words, Bae Do-yoon was silent a moment. His gaze was still stopped on the phone screen. The words in the comments section were not intense, and some of the messages, at a glance, still looked like an ordinary fan’s confession — but if you looked a few more times, you could detect, from inside, a distorted stubbornness.

That was not liking.

Bae Do-yoon frowned a little, and said, shaking his head: “I really cannot understand the thoughts of these extreme fans.”

“Obviously they love their idol to death — but in order to make the idol notice, they can do things that hurt the other.”

“What logic is this?”

Bae Do-yoon put the phone on the desk, moved his gaze off the screen, and his tone went a few parts colder.

“This is a sickness.”

Hearing this, Kim Mi-ju, on the contrary, gave a light smile. She had not thought the sentence was exaggerated. As an assigned clerk, she had seen too many obsessions that looked absurd on the surface but were actually full of danger. Some people approached in the name of love; some people packaged controlling desire as liking; some people even treated hurting as a way of proving their own existence. This so-called “liking,” once it crossed the line, had only pathology left.

“Exactly.”

Kim Mi-ju said, calmly. “This is a sickness.”

She paused a beat, then added one line: “So it has to be treated.”

This is a sickness. — So it has to be treated.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her, and naturally understood her meaning. But understanding or not, the problem in front of him did not become simple because of that. He leaned back into the chair, raised a hand to rub his brow, and let out a low sigh.

“But the problem right now is… we can’t catch this patient.”

He looked at the phone on the desk, the screen still lit, Bae Suzy’s life photo stopped in the center of the interface, her smile bright, as if she did not know at all how much malice was hidden in the corner.

The two of them, for a moment, did not speak again. Kim Mi-ju stood at the edge of the desk, lowered her eyes and looked at the phone, then looked at Bae Do-yoon, and opened her mouth in a casual tone to ask, as if the question had been waiting in her for a while:

“Prosecutor Bae — so, do you like K-pop?”

The question had come a little out of the blue. Bae Do-yoon raised his head and looked at her, but did not think much of it, only answering lightly: “So-so.”

He paused a beat, then added one line.

“If there’s a song that’s really very good to listen to, I’ll pay a little attention.”

Kim Mi-ju nodded, as if confirming something.

“Then do you have a group you like, now?”

“A group, huh…”

Bae Do-yoon repeated it, instinctively. The question was actually not complex — but he really did think about it, seriously. What swept through his mind was not a stage, and not an album cover.

The smile that answered the question · no stage, no album

A smiling face. Clean, soft, carrying a little coldness that was not easy to approach — and precisely because of that, made a deep impression.

A side door, a half-open gap, and a voice that had learned to say the ordinary thing in a way that was not ordinary. The alley, and one sentence that had sounded like a statement of fact and landed like a verdict.

No amount of thinking was needed. That is why the answer came out before the search did.

“Red Velvet.”

The moment the words landed, Kim Mi-ju blinked, and the expression on her face became, at once, a little meaningful.

“Oh — your taste is pretty good, huh.”

She looked at Bae Do-yoon with a smile, then, as if her interest had come, said: “Then let me guess — the member you like most is definitely Irene, right?”

Bae Do-yoon’s motion paused. He raised his eyes and looked at her, and rarely showed a measure of surprise.

“Why do you think I like her the most?”

Kim Mi-ju, seeing his reaction, smiled, on the contrary, more naturally.

“Because she’s the face, of course.”

“There are quite a lot of people who like Seulgi too — but I just feel, someone with your kind of personality would definitely like the Irene type.”

Bae Do-yoon fell silent. For a moment, he did not know how to counter. It was not that what Kim Mi-ju had said had no basis; on the contrary, precisely because she had said it so accurately, there was no way to put words to it. He looked at her, and for a long while did not pick up the conversation.

This woman is really scary. Just a few casual sentences, and she could steer the topic here — and even a preference he himself had never seriously thought about, as if she had lightly, easily, pointed it out.

To be honest, he himself also could not clearly say the two words like. But if he really had to choose one from among the Red Velvet members — then he, probably, would still choose Irene.

“Hey — we’re off topic, though.”

Bae Do-yoon finally came back to himself, looked at Kim Mi-ju, and his tone became, accordingly, a few parts more serious.

“It’s work time now — not K-pop time.”

Kim Mi-ju, looking at that deadpan face of his, could not help laughing. Of course she could hear it out: Bae Do-yoon was not going to continue this topic. But she did not continue to press either; some things, stopping at the point, was enough.

“Understood, Prosecutor Bae.”

Kim Mi-ju gave a light nod, and returned, again, to the capable look an assigned clerk should have.

“Then I’ll go sort out the materials first.”

Having said that, she turned and left. The sound of high heels on the floor grew farther, and farther, away.

Bae Do-yoon drew his gaze back, and his attention returned, again, to the phone screen. Bae Suzy’s social account interface was still stopped there. Her latest post was a life photo: in the photo she wore a simple white top, her hair draped casually, holding a cup of coffee in her hand, smiling at the lens, naturally. The comments section was still lively. Fans expressed their liking enthusiastically, various praises and emoji constantly refreshing their own presence.

Bae Do-yoon looked for a while, and tapped the screen, casually. The next second, the state of the button changed. His account had followed Bae Suzy.

Bae Do-yoon’s fingertip paused.

He had originally only tapped, without thinking — but when he saw the word Following, a blurred train of thought suddenly flashed through his mind.

Following…

The thing the harasser wanted was, actually, simple — from the very beginning. He kept switching accounts, kept leaving messages, kept appearing in Bae Suzy’s field of vision in ways that were more and more uncomfortable — nothing more than wanting Bae Suzy to notice him. As long as Bae Suzy produced an emotion because of him, for him that perhaps already counted, in some sense, as a “response.” But Bae Suzy could not respond to him, and would absolutely not follow him. That was, originally, the most correct way to handle it. But the problem was this: for a person with this kind of obsession, the more he could not get a response, the more his unwillingness would be aggravated. The more Bae Suzy did not look at him, the more he would want to make her see. The more Bae Suzy ignored him, the more likely he was to push, and push, further.

If that was so — couldn’t the thinking be switched?

If the harasser’s core need was to be followed by Bae Suzy, then what would happen when he discovered that Bae Suzy had given that following to someone else?

Jealousy. Anger. Losing control. And — the transfer of the target.

The reversal · the plan, as the thought clarified

01The core need, identified — to be noticed by Bae Suzy; every account, every message, every uncomfortable appearance, all in service of it

02The current state — no response, by the victim’s design; and the no-response, for an obsessive, is not a wall but fuel

03The reversal — give that attention to someone else: a fan Bae Suzy replies to, likes, and follows on her own motion

04The reaction predicted — jealousy, anger, losing control, and the transfer of the target; a man who no longer sits behind a network

05The hope — approaching, attacking, and leaving traces a case can hold; the breakthrough, if the bait is controlled properly

Condition, noted at the bottom of the thought: the bait must be safe, and it must be controllable. A probe that cannot be recalled is not a probe. It is a wound.

Bae Do-yoon’s gaze swept the comments section, line by line, and his train of thought gradually became clear. Draw the harasser’s emotion onto someone else. For example… make Bae Suzy follow a “fan.” And make her show, toward that fan, an enthusiasm and a favoritism that were obvious enough. A person who was suddenly noticed by her. A person who was replied to by her, liked by her, even followed voluntarily by her. For an ordinary fan, this might just be luck falling from the sky. But for that pathological harasser, this was very likely a kind of stimulation. He would feel he had been replaced. He would feel that that “lucky fan” had snatched away what should, originally, have been his. As long as the emotion was detonated, he very likely would no longer be satisfied with hiding behind the network, sending those ambiguous messages. He would approach voluntarily, try to attack, and leave clearer traces. If it was controlled properly, using a safe and controllable bait to draw him out of the shadow might, perhaps, be the most effective breakthrough at the present moment.

Bae Do-yoon reopened Bae Suzy’s fan comment section, and his gaze swept those accounts, bit by bit. A moment later, he slowly leaned back into the chair.

The question was: this “fan Bae Suzy favors” — who should play it?

On the face that will be the bait

The plan is finished, and the one missing from it is a face. That is the state of the chapter at its end: a bait that must be real enough to be believed, safe enough to be recalled, and — above all — available. And a prosecutor’s office in which the question of who will wear that face has not yet been asked aloud, because the answer, in that room, is already sitting in the chair, scrolling the comment section with the calm of a man who does not yet know he is the plan. The arithmetic of the case does not need the answer yet. The arithmetic of two of the people in the room already does.
