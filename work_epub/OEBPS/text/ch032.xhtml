Chapter Thirty-Two

The Similar One, Her Shadow

The same morning · the Shilla suite, after the alarm

High floor, east window; the morning light coming through the curtain gap, a line of gold across the shoulder

Two people, one bed, and a night neither of them has agreed on yet

Jessica was, as last time, with her back to him.

The morning light leaked through the gap in the curtains and fell on the shoulder and back that rose and fell, just a little. Her hair lay loose in the pillows, half of her profile hidden in it, and all that showed was a small snow-white ear and the fine line of her neck.

Bae Do-yoon sat up against the headboard and watched her, quietly, for a while.

The air still held the trace of last night, not yet dispersed. The bed was a mess, the thin duvet half fallen. Honestly, the relationship between the two of them was something even Bae Do-yoon himself found a little absurd. They were not a couple. They were not husband and wife. And yet, once again, they had woken in the same bed, like some kind of entanglement no one could put a name to: not something that should have happened, and yet, plainly, already had.

Before, he would have carried a little of that unnameable guilt. Now, that feeling had long since faded. Since he had understood that a deal already existed between Jessica and Lee Boo-jin, it had become hard to reduce last night, simply, to an accident of who owed whom anything.

What he cared about now was something else: what on earth Lee Boo-jin wanted to do.

Putting Jessica in front of him was a thing worth sitting with for a while. If it was flattery, it was too deliberate. If it was the beauty trap, it was too plain. A person like Lee Boo-jin did not run a board this shallow, and if she had pushed things as far as this, there had to be a deeper design underneath.

The question he actually has · the readings, ruled out in order

01Flattery — too deliberate to be flattery; a favor of this size does not arrive smiling

02The beauty trap — too plain to be a trap; a trap shaped this way announces itself from the doorway

03A shallow board — not her way; Lee Boo-jin does not push a thing to this point for a shallow reason

04A deeper design — the only reading left; the one the silence confirms

He does not write the list down. He does not need to; it sorts itself, and it is the last line that stays.

Bae Do-yoon lowered his eyes and looked at the person beside him, with that particular look of a man thinking.

Asleep, Jessica was quieter than she was awake.

He watched her back for a while, and in the end moved his gaze away. There was no point in continuing to think about a thing that would not think back.

He drew back the duvet and got out of bed. When his foot came down on the carpet, his eyes swept past the edge of the bed, unhurried, and found several pieces of torn clothing scattered on the floor, disordered past any description. In that instant, the out-of-control scenes of last night swept, most unseasonably, across the surface of his mind.

Bae Do-yoon paused a beat, then withdrew his gaze without any expression at all, turned, and went into the bathroom. By the time he came out, washed and done, his expression had recovered to usual.

He dressed unhurriedly, buttoned his cufflinks, took up his phone and checked the time. Before going out the door, he looked back at the bed once.

Jessica had not moved. Still that posture, her back to him.

Bae Do-yoon did not see through it. He gave her one light look and turned and left.

The door was drawn shut, softly. A click, and the room was quiet again.

A few seconds later, Jessica, who had made no sound all that time, slowly opened her eyes.

She had in fact been awake a long while. It was simply that, before Bae Do-yoon left, she had not wanted to move at all. She did not want to turn around. She did not want to meet his eyes. Everything last night had come too fast, and now that morning had come and all the impulse had receded, what was left could only be embarrassment.

Jessica pressed her lips together, supported herself, and slowly sat up. The duvet slipped a little from her shoulder; she caught it with an instinctive hand and looked down at the bed. The sheet was a ruin, the creases crossing each other, the pillow rolled off to one side.

Her gaze went lower, and found the few pieces of clothing on the floor that were nearly un-wearable, and the color in her face went, at once, ugly.

She stared at that pile of wreckage for a long while, and finally could not help a low curse.

“Bastard…”

A pause. Then, teeth together, one more line:

“You tore all my clothes like that — how am I supposed to go out?”

After the cursing, the room held, as before, only her own voice.

Jessica sat on the bed, her hair a little disordered, her expression carrying a measure of self-reproach and a measure of irritation that would not name itself. She looked down at her own fingers, and after a while let out a light sigh.

Impulse, after all, was the devil.

The door · the second that decided everything

Last night was not supposed to turn out that way. If she had not let the feeling take her, if she had not called him back at the door as he was leaving, if she had not lost control in that one second — the story would have stopped at the doorway.

But she did not hold back. That is the whole of it, and it is the part she cannot re-argue: the night had been arranged, the arrangement had an end, and the door was hers. The second in which she called him back was the only unarranged thing in the room, and it spent the rest of the night making itself into facts.

She could have told herself that last night had merely been executing Lee Boo-jin’s order. The trouble was that she knew, quite clearly, that the thing was not entirely so. Lee Boo-jin had indeed pushed her here. But the person who had actually lost control was her. At the very beginning, she could have left. And in the end, the arrangement that was supposed to end had taken on a different flavor, and even she could not say which step it had left the original track on.

The more she thought, the more it felt as though she had taken a loss — and the particular kind of loss where she could not even say who to hold it against.

Jessica raised a hand and rubbed her temple, forcing the messy scenes of last night down out of her head. But her thoughts turned, quickly, to another thing.

What, exactly, was the relationship between the two of them and Lee Boo-jin?

Over this stretch of time, Lee Boo-jin’s attitude toward him had long since passed what the two words importance could explain. It was not ordinary care, and not simple favor; it was more like tending something, very carefully. If Bae Do-yoon were only an ordinary man, he would not be worth this from Lee Boo-jin.

Jessica leaned back against the headboard and ran the period through her mind, again, slowly.

Subjectthe relationship between Bae Do-yoon and Lee Boo-jin; status: unsolved, and getting more expensive every day

The little-puppy optionexcluded first, without ceremony; Lee Boo-jin would not spend this much effort on a puppy, and certainly not drag her into the board personally; and his composure, his measure — nothing about him says a man who lives attached to someone

The ordinary-man hypothesisinsufficient; if he were only an ordinary man, none of this would be happening to him

The wild onea younger brother?; the Lee family’s front is a short list, but chaebol family business has always been a mess under the surface, and what is kept below the surface, no one can say; if blood really tied him to the Lees, a great many unreasonable things would, at a stretch, become explainable

The version that sounds absurdan unknown illegitimate son of Lee Kun-hee, of Samsung; absurd on the first pass — and not, on the second, without possibility

The question that will not leave: otherwise, why would Lee Boo-jin treat him so specially — and why did even she end up as a piece in the board?

She frowned, and let the thought that had surfaced sit there a moment — a thought she found absurd even to her own mind. Then she was silent a while, and her eyes grew, if anything, more complicated.

Jessica looked down at the torn clothes on the floor once more, pressed the corner of her mouth lightly, and her expression became, if anything, more subtle. What had happened last night was, plainly, not that simple. And this person, Bae Do-yoon, was far more troublesome than she had first thought him.

A while later. Jessica drew back the duvet and went to get out of bed. But the moment her feet touched the floor, her motion stopped, hard; the whole person drew in a light breath, and the tips of her brows knit together.

She steadied herself on the edge of the bed and looked down — and found that the state of herself was more ragged than she had imagined. Long hair, disordered, hanging at the shoulder; and on the skin, the ambiguous marks left by last night, still there, scattered here and there.

Jessica stared at them for two seconds, and the expression on her face went, at once, a little strange.

After a while, she let a low mutter get out of her:

“Is this guy… a dog? Why does he bite everywhere…”

Halfway through the sentence her ears went, for no apparent reason, hot, and the second half of the line was, in the end, pressed low — half to herself, half to the empty air of the room.

But complaining or not, she froze suddenly at the complaint itself. Because, arriving a beat late, she discovered that her own mood was far calmer than she had expected. Not only was there no irritation and no flustered anger — it was as if some feeling that had been pressed on her chest had, without a sound, opened a crack.

Bae Do-yoon had said nothing. No explanation, no comforting, no performed tenderness of any kind. And yet she could not have told you when, exactly, the knot in her chest — the one that had been tightening, and tightening — had loosened, just a little, without her knowing.

The feeling she cannot name · self-report, unverified

Expectedirritation, flustered anger, the second dose of hatred the second night was supposed to deliver

Actuala calm she did not order; a long-held breath, finally, slowly let go; the whole person clearer by so much

The knotit had been tightening for years — and had just, without ceremony, loosened one notch

Verdict, against all arithmetic: the dislike is fading, little by little — and she finds it incredible herself.

Jessica stood where she was, her expression a little blank.

She found the change herself to be highly contrary to reason. This man had, after all, already twice slept with her, and by rights she ought to hate him more than before. But the fact was precisely the reverse. Her dislike of Bae Do-yoon was, in fact, fading, bit by bit. She found it incredible herself.

Jessica pressed her lips lightly; a complicated emotion crossed her eyes, and even her brows, without her permission, knit together.

“I must be insane…”

She said it low, and did not know whether she was saying it of Bae Do-yoon, or of herself.

Afterwards, not long · Lee Boo-jin’s office

A wide office, morning sun off the floor-to-ceiling glass; the city’s hard high-rise lines reflected on the screen

One woman at a computer, one woman at the door, and a frame on the desk corner nobody has touched in years

“President, the two of them have already left.”

Not long after, the assistant pushed the door open and came into Lee Boo-jin’s office, and stopped at the edge of the desk, bowing her head a little to report. The office was wide; Lee Boo-jin sat before the computer, her expression calm in the usual way, her eyes on the screen. She did not even lift her head.

“All right, I know.”

The assistant stood to one side and did not leave at once. She had been by Lee Boo-jin’s side for many years, and counted as one of the old hands she truly trusted. Ordinary work, much of it, never needed Lee Boo-jin to open her mouth; one glance, and the other woman understood. But only on the matter of Bae Do-yoon was she, in truth, unable to read it.

The assistant’s observation · filed without her permission

The thing she cannot read, itemized

Source: many years of service, one office, and a question that has been waiting a long time to be asked

Attentionfar beyond the ordinary range; not the range a person gets for usefulness alone

The dealstruck on his account, with Jessica; the President does not strike deals on speculation

The stylenothing like Lee Boo-jin’s usual; the woman who signs in silence has been asking, in practice, for a long time

What the assistant cannot yet say: she does not know what Bae Do-yoon is. She only knows that her President has stopped being economical about him.

The assistant hesitated a moment, and in the end could not help asking. “President, may I ask one question?”

Lee Boo-jin’s hands did not stop. “Ask,” she said, lightly.

The assistant weighed her words, and went carefully. “Why do you care about him so much? Even to the point of striking a deal with that woman?”

The fingers Lee Boo-jin had over the keyboard stopped, half in the air. After a few seconds, she drew her hand back, slowly, and moved her eyes from the computer screen to a corner of the desk.

There, a frame stood.

The photograph inside the frame was some years old, but it had been kept very well.

The frame on the desk corner · two sisters, one photograph

A younger Lee Boo-jin, and beside her a girl laughing so brightly it looks like light; the arrogance of the pampered sitting easy and total in her eyes and brows.

The girl is Lee Yoon-hyang. Lee Boo-jin’s sister. The little princess the whole Samsung family once held in its palm.

For many people the name is already past. For the woman who keeps the frame, it never was.

The assistant’s expression shifted a little, and her eyes grew a measure more complicated.

The name Lee Yoon-hyang, for many people, had long since become the past. But for Lee Boo-jin, it never had been. The two sisters had been very close. And then, ten years ago, over the matter of personal feelings, Lee Yoon-hyang had in the end walked another road; and from that on, the two of them were separated by yin and yang, and never met again.

Lee Boo-jin looked at the frame, quietly. After a long while, she raised her hand, and her fingertips came to rest, lightly, on the edge of the frame. Her motion was very slow, as if afraid of startling the person in the photograph.

“Because…”

Lee Boo-jin paused a moment. Her voice was much lower than before.

“In some ways, he resembles Yoon-hyang.”

The assistant did not speak. She had been by her side many years, and she knew, of course, what Lee Yoon-hyang meant in that heart. It was not simple nostalgia, and it was not an old story that could be picked up and set down easily; it was a thorn buried deep in the bottom of the heart. Invisible normally, and never touched — but still, at the lightest touch, capable of pain.

Lee Boo-jin gazed at the photograph, her expression still calm. Only in that calm had entered a measure of blankness that others would have had difficulty noticing at all.

“They are actually very different.”

Said lightly: “Different sex, different status, different experience. One is the Lee family’s little princess; the other is only a young man who came out of an orphanage.”

To there, her fingertip passed, lightly, across Lee Yoon-hyang’s face in the photograph.

“But all these years, I have been watching him.”

“Sometimes, looking at him, I feel…”

Lee Boo-jin’s voice stopped for one second.

The assistant looked up at her.

Lee Boo-jin was still looking at the photograph. In her eyes, something very faint seemed to surface — and was, very quickly, pressed down again.

“I think I have seen Yoon-hyang’s shadow in him.”

I think I have seen Yoon-hyang’s shadow in him.

Outside the window the sun was just right. The floor-to-ceiling glass held the cold hard outlines of the city’s high towers, and Lee Boo-jin sat at the place where light and shadow met, her expression light. But the assistant, at that moment, suddenly understood.

The reason Bae Do-yoon was special was not only profit, and not only some value on him worth using. At least, for Lee Boo-jin, it was not all of that. In Bae Do-yoon she had seen a person who had left, years ago. She had seen a past that could not be taken back, and she had seen, in her own heart, the regret she had never truly let go of.

Lee Boo-jin had pushed Jessica in front of Bae Do-yoon not only to hold him.

Last night she had told Bae Do-yoon that she regarded him as her own younger brother. The sentence had sounded like an easing of the air, like the customary soothing move of someone who sits higher — but in fact, what Lee Boo-jin had said was true.

Bae Do-yoon’s temper, his pride, his stubbornness, and that particular danger of his — the looking clear-headed in love while actually being the kind who goes straight in — all of it made Lee Boo-jin think of Lee Yoon-hyang. The matter of love, especially.

Lee Boo-jin knew, better than anyone, what a man like Bae Do-yoon was like: once he had truly moved his heart, he was actually harder to pull back than an ordinary person. He was not the kind of man who let a woman lead him by the hand, and he was not the young man several sweet sentences could move. But precisely because of that, once someone really got past his defense and walked into his heart, he was more stubborn than anyone. And that stubbornness, sometimes, was not deep feeling. It was a disaster.

Lee Yoon-hyang, back then, had been exactly so. She had had everything others could not reach — status, wealth, family, future — and in the end, still, over the matter of love, she had walked a road no one could pull her back from.

That matter, Lee Boo-jin had never truly let go of.

So when she saw a similar shadow in Bae Do-yoon, she became wary, almost by instinct. Bae Do-yoon was now at the Prosecutors’ Office, his future without limit; with his ability and his background, as long as he made no fatal error, sooner or later he would stand in a higher place. A person like that should not be dragged into the mud over a woman, and not over so-called love. But Lee Boo-jin knew equally clearly that she could not cut off, on his behalf, all of his feelings. A person is not a machine, and the more violently you interfere, the more likely it is to work backwards. She could influence him with resources, hold him with profit, and pave his way at the crucial moment — but she could not truly control his heart. And Bae Do-yoon, for his part, was not a man to be arranged by others.

So Lee Boo-jin changed her method.

If Bae Do-yoon was, sooner or later, going to meet trouble in love, then let him taste it early, and build the antibody early. Let him know that a woman can be gentle, and can be sharp. Can be pitiable, and can be dangerous. Can become comfort, and can become weakness. And Jessica was the piece in this arrangement that could not be spared: beautiful enough, proud enough, complex enough. Not the kind of woman who only obeys and fawns, and the kind who would never, easily, bow her head before him. In her there was desire, and ambition, and grievance, and thorns. A woman like that was exactly best fitted to teach Bae Do-yoon one thing: love has never once been pure. It can be mixed with deals, with profit, with probing, even with calculation. If Bae Do-yoon could not even get through the checkpoint of Jessica, then when he met someone more dangerous in the future, he would only lose worse.

The arrangement · the antibody plan, as the one woman who heard it understood it

I. The premise — he will, sooner or later, meet trouble in love; only the when, and the who, are open.

II. The method — an early taste, and an early antibody; the curriculum, as the office already stated it: gentleness and its sharp edge, pity and its danger, comfort and its weakness.

III. The piece — Jessica: beautiful enough, proud enough, complex enough; desire, ambition, grievance, thorns; never the kind who bows her head easily.

IV. The lesson — love has never once been pure; what it can carry, as the office already counted: the deal, the profit, the probe, and the calculation.

V. The standard — if he cannot get through Jessica, then with someone more dangerous he will lose worse; the checkpoint is set accordingly.

The day on the calendar that must not arrive: a second person, resembling Yoon-hyang, walking the same reason to the same no-return end.

On Lee Boo-jin’s own ledger this entry is not filed under harm. It is filed under rescue, delivered in advance — and the delivery method is cold, forceful, and without the least tenderness.

Lee Boo-jin did not want to see that day.

So she had moved, herself, first.

She had already lost Lee Yoon-hyang once. She did not want to stand there, eyes open, and watch another person similar to Yoon-hyang walk, for the same reason, toward an ending with no way back.

On the cold rescue

The last line of the chapter is not spoken in the office, because it is not a line for the office. In Lee Boo-jin’s view, she is not harming Bae Do-yoon; she is rescuing him in advance — and that is the sentence the room leaves standing, because it is the sentence no one else in the room can contest. It is also the sentence that makes the arrangement what it is: not a trap laid by a greedy woman, but a rescue delivered by one who has already lost, of the kind that is cold, forceful, and without the least tenderness. The woman at the desk does not raise her voice, does not raise her hand, does not even raise her eyes from the photograph. She has simply decided, in the way she decides everything, that someone who looks like her sister must not be allowed to love the way her sister loved. And the blade, once more, is not the thing it looks like: it is the instrument of the rescue, and the rescue is the instrument of the grief, and the grief, for ten years, has been doing exactly what grief does, which is waiting, at the desk corner, for the next face that resembles the first.
