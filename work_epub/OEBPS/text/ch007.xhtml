Chapter Seven

Not the “Future Brother-in-Law” Candidate

Still that morning, the ring not yet finished

Jessica’s home · the living room, one phone between two sisters

A name on a screen, and everything it has been costing

Jessica looked at the phone screen.

Kwon Young-il calling at this hour: the purpose was not hard to guess. Perhaps something had gone wrong at the brand. Perhaps he had heard her resources moving. Perhaps he wanted, again, to talk her into a dinner, a room, an investor.

Before, she might have endured it patiently. After last night, she was suddenly, enormously tired.

She began, from the top, to re-audit every relationship standing near her.

The audit she owed herself

Question oneWho loves her, and who loves what she brings?

Question twoWhich collaborations help her, and which keep pushing her into a mire that gets harder to walk out of?

Kwon Young-ilNot Lee Boo-jin’s level. Consumes her all the same — her name, her career, her sister’s face at the sound of his

A consumption is still a consumption when it arrives wearing concern.

A private reckoning, labeled as one. No ledger pinged; nothing here is an entry’s work. It is a woman at thirty-odd taking inventory of her own life.

The ringtone went on.

Jessica was silent a moment. Then her thumb crossed the screen.

Declined, outright.

Krystal’s eyes went round; she plainly thought she had misseen.

“Eonni? You hung up?”

Jessica set the phone aside. “Mm.”

Krystal looked at her in open disbelief.

“What is wrong with you today?”

“You always answered before. Even with me sitting right here, you’d take it out on the balcony.”

She narrowed her eyes, like a woman who has stumbled onto a state secret.

“Did you two fight?”

Jessica shook her head. “No.”

“Then why hang up on him?”

Jessica glanced at her sister. “You hate him, don’t you?”

Krystal froze.

Jessica’s voice stayed level. “Then I won’t answer. I’d rather not spoil your good mood.”

Krystal’s mouth opened and stayed open.

She had come into this with a belly full of complaints about Kwon Young-il, and her sister had simply defused her with one sentence.

In the old days, the moment she said anything against him, Jessica went silent. Or changed the subject. Sometimes she even took offense.

Today her sister had hung up on the man herself, and given Krystal’s own hatred as the reason.

Krystal’s eyes lit, slowly.

“Eonni.” She asked it carefully, the way one asks a stray cat to stay. “Have you finally come to your senses?”

Jessica looked at her. “Come to my senses about what?”

Krystal came fully alive.

“About that man being wrong for you, obviously!”

“He does not look one bit like my future brother-in-law.”

“His face fails, his manner fails, his conduct fails.”

“Every time I see him, I swear his eyes are full of abacus beads.”

Jessica said, half helpless, “Is he really as bad as all that?”

Krystal said it with total seriousness:

“He is.”

“Let me tell you — a woman’s intuition is extremely accurate.”

“The way he looks at you is not the way a man looks at his girlfriend. It is the way a man looks at company assets.”

The way he looks at you is not the way a man looks at his girlfriend. It is the way a man looks at company assets.

The sentence stopped Jessica for a second.

Company assets.

She thought, suddenly, of the Samsung woman’s eyes on her across a tea-lounge table.

So this same look — her sister had seen it in Kwon Young-il all along. She herself had simply refused, until now, to admit it.

Jessica lowered her eyes and did not argue.

Not arguing, Krystal grew more excited still. She climbed onto the bed with a pillow hugged to her chest, in the posture of a committee of one convening a serious meeting.

“Eonni, I have wanted to say this for so long.”

“Since you got with him, you have been worse and worse. Brand, brand. Investment, investment. Collaboration, collaboration.”

“It all sounds like it’s for your good. And at the end every bit of the pressure lands on you.”

“When something goes wrong, the media curses you, the fans curse you, and the company uses it to hit you with.”

“And him? What exactly has he carried?”

Jessica was silent.

In the old days she could not have heard these sentences. Back then she had needed an exit too badly: the opinion weather after leaving the group, the pressure of rebuilding a career, a young brand’s raw ambition, doubt arriving from every direction at once.

What he offered, and what it cost to believe it

Kwon Young-il had given her the illusion of a second door: I can open another road for you.

Looking back now, some of it had been wrong from the start. She simply had not wanted to admit it while the door was still the only one she had.

The phone screen lit again.

Kwon Young-il, calling back.

Krystal glanced at it and her face fell at once. “He’s calling again?”

Jessica looked at the screen, her eyes a shade colder.

This time she did not decline. She answered.

Krystal stared. Jessica raised one hand: don’t speak.

Call · Kwon Young-il · second attempt, connected

Jessica? Why did you hang up just now?

Hurried, and a little aggrieved — the tone of a man who expects his calls to be taken.

Is something the matter?

Flat. The other end pauses, plainly not prepared for that temperature.

I’m worried about you, of course. Where were you last night? I couldn’t reach you at all.

You were looking for me?

Yes. I called several times. You didn’t answer.

She glanced at the call log. Last night his missed calls were indeed there; at the Shilla her phone had been on silent since before dinner.

There was a business dinner last night. I drank too much.

What dinner? With whom?

Two questions, too fast. Concern, yes — and under the concern an interrogation’s urgency, the fear of her slipping out of his grasp.

You called to ask me this?

No. The brand just received word: two channels suddenly want to reopen talks. A department store’s head contacted us on his own. Jessica — did you get someone to help?

Her fingers tightened on the phone. Again: her sister’s resources and her brand’s channels moving in the same hour. Samsung was faster, and more precise, than she had guessed.

This is good news. If these close, everything we were stuck on solves itself. Tell me — which side? Some investor you knew?

His voice, excited. Her chest, cooling by the sentence.

I will handle this matter.

You’ll handle it? Jessica, we are building this brand together — this kind of thing of course we do toge—

I said: I will handle it.

Silence at the other end. A few seconds. Then, a degree cooler:

What is wrong with you today? Did something happen?

She looked out of the window and did not offer him her face.

Send me the brand’s recent books. By noon tomorrow.

The books?

Mm. All channel outlays, investment lines, inventory costs, external contracts. I want to see all of it.

Two seconds of quiet; then a laugh, carefully placed.

Jessica, what is that supposed to mean? You don’t trust me?

I only want to see clearly where the brand stands.

I have always handled all of this. You don’t need to worry.

Precisely because you have always handled it, I want to look through it myself now.

Total silence at the other end. Beside her, Krystal’s eyes growing rounder by the line.

Jessica — have you been listening to somebody?

Send it by noon tomorrow. If it is inconvenient, I will have my lawyer collect it.

You will take it as far as that?

I only want to see the books. That is taking it as far as something?

He choked on it. She let him.

That’s all.

She ended the call. This time, she was the one who ended it.

Krystal bounced up off the bed.

“Wow.”

She stared at Jessica as at a miracle in household scale.

“Eonni, you were so cool just now.”

Jessica almost laughed; nothing in her chest felt light at all. She set the phone down. “Don’t talk nonsense.”

Krystal crowded in at once.

“No — I mean it seriously. That was finally the eonni I know.”

Jessica stilled, a little.

Krystal looked at her, voice dropping.

“Before, when you were with him, I always felt you weren’t you.”

“You’re so proud. And every time it was his business, you seemed to be stepping backward. I didn’t like that version of you.”

Jessica lowered her eyes and said it properly: “I’m sorry.”

Krystal blinked. “What are you apologizing to me for?”

Jessica reached out and hugged her, lightly.

Krystal went rigid at once. This sister of hers was not a person fluent in intimacy; even care arrived wrapped in an awkward tone, and an unprompted hug was rarer than an apology.

Jessica put her chin on her sister’s shoulder, low: “These past months — I made you bear too much.”

Krystal blinked again. The excitement drained out of her slowly, and something nose-stinging came up in its place.

Her mouth stayed hard anyway.

“I haven’t borne anything. I was just on holiday at home.”

“A holiday until you go moldy?” Jessica said softly.

Krystal choked, then said, small: “That isn’t your fault either.”

Jessica did not answer.

Krystal hugged her back, and for once her voice was entirely serious:

“Eonni. Don’t carry everything alone.”

Jessica’s eyes went hot; she closed them and pressed the feeling back down.

“Mm.”

But some things, she cannot know.

Krystal may go back on stage. She may keep being the Krystal the cameras see as cool and a great many people love. The dirty things — I hold them outside the door myself.

After a while Krystal let her go, then lit up again, as if struck by a thought.

“So — Eonni, you really weren’t just impulsive just now? You’re really going to audit that man’s books?”

Jessica nodded. “Mm.”

Krystal applauded on the spot.

“Perfect. I always thought he was unreliable — I’m telling you, he definitely has problems.”

Watching her sister’s excited face, Jessica finally let out a real smile. “Why are you so happy?”

Krystal said it with total self-righteousness: “Because my future brother-in-law candidate list just lost one wrong option.”

Jessica, helpless: “Who told you he was a candidate?”

Krystal seized the point instantly. “So he isn’t anymore?”

Jessica did not answer; she turned to fetch the hair dryer.

Krystal took the silence for a default and wore a deeply satisfied face.

“Good. I declare today a day worth commemorating.”

Jessica switched the dryer on; warm air roared.

Krystal went on narrating beside her:

The committee of one, setting terms for the next candidate

01Handsome — at minimum, must not lose to an idol; otherwise how is he worthy of eonni

02Clean — in person, in habits, in paperwork

03Smart — but not the calculating kind

04No abacus beads in the eyes, ever

Item four was added, the committee confirmed, entirely because of one specific man.

Over the dryer’s noise Jessica heard her sister’s terms and felt, against every intention, another face surface in her mind.

Young.

Good-looking.

Clean?

No. She no longer dared use that word for him.

He had been… rough. He had made her cry. And last night, what he left inside her…

The thoughts tangled and went nowhere useful. And yet he was not oily at a glance the way Kwon Young-il was oily at a glance.

No. No.

Not now, and not attached to a phrase like future brother-in-law.

Krystal, missing her sister’s half-second absence entirely, went on with her enthusiasm.

“Eonni — should I take these two endorsements?”

Jessica came back. “Of course you should. And do the company’s activities seriously, too.”

Krystal nodded. “Don’t worry.”

She paused, then added, a little awkwardly: “Actually… I did want to go back on stage.”

Jessica switched the dryer off and looked at her.

Krystal turned her face away.

“Home is comfortable and all. But no work forever is awful. I don’t want to be forgotten like this.”

Jessica’s heart ached again.

She crossed the room and rubbed her sister’s hair, lightly.

“You won’t be. Our Soo-jung won’t be forgotten.”

Krystal pressed her lips, then: “What about you?”

Jessica’s hand stopped.

Krystal looked at her.

“Don’t let yourself be forgotten either. On stage, you were really beautiful.”

“I don’t say it, but a lot of people are still waiting for you.”

Jessica lowered her eyes. After a few seconds she said, quietly: “There will be a chance. There definitely will.”

Krystal smiled. “Mm.”

Only what this chance was traded for — that, she cannot say.

Then the phone screen lit once more.

Not a call. A message, from an unknown number.

Jessica picked it up. The message was very short. One line.

Message · unknown number

No contact card · no history · one line

Ms. Jung. The first token of good faith has been delivered.

No signature. She knew who.

Jessica’s face went cold in an instant.

Krystal noticed. “Who is it?”

Jessica killed the screen fast. “Spam.”

Krystal looked at her, foxily. “Really?”

Jessica laid the phone face-down on the table. “Really.”

Krystal found it odd, but did not push; her head was too full of activities and endorsements, and she went back to narrating the company’s call with fresh excitement.

Jessica sat beside her and listened quietly.

The happier her sister sounded, the more astringent her chest became.

The chaebol’s truest terror is not the threat. It is being shown, clearly, the price list: bow, and benefit arrives at once; resist, and cost arrives faster.

· · ·

In the evening, Krystal was summoned to the company to confirm her schedule.

After the door closed behind her, the smile went off Jessica’s face by degrees.

She sat with the photograph — herself and Bae Do-yoon, two years young and politely apart — and let the day’s whole ledger come up around her.

And under it, in the quiet, the list she had dictated over the phone arranged itself properly in her head. By noon tomorrow, the books. This is what they must contain:

The books, itemized — what she demanded by noon

Channel outlaysevery won spent opening a channel, and every won spent keeping one quiet

Investment lineswho holds what in the brand, on which terms, signed when

Inventory costswhat sits unsold, in whose warehouse, at whose expense

External contractsevery signature that binds the brand’s name without her own on it

If it is inconvenient to send, a lawyer will collect it. The sentence had felt, in her mouth, like a door finally unlocking from her side.

A note on ledgers

Two books are opened in this chapter: a brand’s, which Jessica demands in writing, and a sisterhood’s, which she settles with a hug and an apology. Only one of them can be audited by a lawyer. Guess which one she will still be reading at midnight.
