Chapter Twenty-Four

The Girls Next Door

Past midnight · his floor

The corridor’s sensor lights, waking one by one to his footsteps and dimming behind him

Two half-unpacked cartons by the entryway, tape torn halfway, forgotten in some moment

When Bae Do-yoon got back to the apartment, it was already past twelve.

In the corridor the sensor lights came on one by one for his footsteps, and behind him went quietly dark again.

He had not long moved into this apartment. Beside the entryway two cartons still stood unpacked — one full of books, one of miscellany — their sealing tape torn only halfway open, like things their owner had forgotten in some moment of himself.

In the living room only one floor lamp was lit. Its light was very pale, falling on the folder spread open across the desk.

The topmost document carried a sticky note: a task his senior prosecutor had handed down to him.

Bae Do-yoon stood before the desk for two seconds and sighed, lightly.

Some time into the job, he had discovered that television was all lies.

The prosecutors in dramas chase the truth every single day.

The rookie prosecutor in reality must first chase why the case number does not match the case.

It was very late, and yet he did not rest at once.

He had trained half a day at the martial arts hall in the morning and gone to see Irene in the evening — and he was not tired at all; if anything, he was a little strung up and bright.

He might as well work a while overtime.

He drew the chair out, sat, took up his pen — and had just turned to the second page when a muffled sound came through from next door.

Thud.

The pen in Bae Do-yoon’s hand paused a moment.

He raised his head to look at the wall.

A few seconds later, another sound.

Thud.

This time accompanied by Park Ji-yeon’s cry.

“Ahh — no no, redo!”

Boram’s voice was very calm.

“You nearly hit the table just now.”

“This time it will definitely work!”

Bae Do-yoon: …

He looked down at the evidence inventory on his desk, then at the wall clock.

In the middle of the night — what were they doing?

Catching mice?

Thud.

“Ahh!”

“You stepped on the edge of the mat again.”

“I know! But this game’s judging is really weird, okay!”

Bae Do-yoon was silent a few seconds. So they were playing a game.

Only days ago the three of them had eaten fried chicken together.

Park Ji-yeon had then declared, in all earnestness, that she was “a person who understands neighborly consideration very well.”

Bae Do-yoon now felt the credibility of that sentence declining at speed.

Thud.

“Stop stop stop — I think I twisted my ankle.”

Boram said: “You only stepped on your slipper.”

Park Ji-yeon would not concede.

“That counts as interference too!”

Bae Do-yoon put the pen down, stood, and went to the door.

The instant he opened it, the corridor light came on again.

Under the neighboring door a thread of light showed.

Inside there was a moment of quiet; then, close behind it, came Park Ji-yeon’s voice.

But wasn’t he not home?

Two knocks answered the question: knock, knock. Inside, instant silence; then, carefully: Bae Do-yoon-ssi? Then, from the corridor: mm, it’s me. Hurried footsteps; a door opened one crack.

The intelligence services of the neighboring apartment, currently restructuring after the forecast “not home tonight” failed in the field.

Boram said:

“It should be Bae Do-yoon-ssi.”

“He’s back?”

“It sounds like it.”

“But wasn’t he not home?”

Bae Do-yoon stood at his doorway, one brow-tip lifting, faintly.

He raised his hand and knocked twice on the door.

Knock, knock.

Inside, the sound died instantly.

After a few seconds, Park Ji-yeon’s careful voice came out.

“Bae Do-yoon-ssi?”

Bae Do-yoon spoke.

“Mm. It’s me.”

Inside there came a scramble of hurried footsteps.

The door opened a crack.

Doorway inventory · the neighbor caught mid-jump

Hair long, somewhat disordered; a jump-mode casualty

Forehead carrying a little sweat, evidence the “little activity” had been athletic after all

Loungewear loose, home-grade, one hand at the doorframe and the other pressed, without noticing, to the waist

Expression first guilt, then a somewhat stiff smile, deployed at speed: you… you’re back

Assembly rating from the corridor: nothing like a person who had merely “moved around a little”; everything like a person caught between jump mode and apology.

Park Ji-yeon poked her head out — long hair somewhat messy, a little sweat still on her forehead, dressed in loose loungewear, one hand holding the doorframe, the other pressed to her waist without her noticing it.

Seeing Bae Do-yoon, her expression went first guiltily slack, then immediately produced a somewhat stiff smile.

“You… you’re back.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her.

“Mm, I’m back. Much later and I might have started thinking the neighbors were doing renovation work.”

The smile on Park Ji-yeon’s face stiffened a little.

“No no, no renovation. It’s just… we moved around a little.”

From inside came Boram’s very light sigh.

“I said earlier we would disturb them…”

Park Ji-yeon turned her head at once.

“Unnie, you said just now we’d play one last round!”

Boram sat on the sofa with a water glass in hand, tone calm.

“I said one last round played sitting down.” She glanced at Bae Do-yoon and added one more line: “She turned on jump mode afterwards.”

Park Ji-yeon grew a little urgent.

“But playing it sitting down doesn’t count as exercise at all!”

Boram looked toward the low table.

“When we were eating jokbal, you weren’t this concerned about exercise.”

Park Ji-yeon: …

Bae Do-yoon let her gaze lead him and looked once into the room.

The scene in the living room was very persuasive.

The television screen still rested on the motion-game’s results page; a yoga mat lay askew on the floor; the low table had been shoved aside; two cushions had fallen; one slipper lay forlornly by the door.

On the low table stood takeout boxes not yet cleared away.

Verdict of the doorway: however viewed, not a little activity. The game’s results page, still on the screen, graded the chief athlete B.

“This game of yours makes rather a lot of noise,” Bae Do-yoon said, lightly.

Park Ji-yeon pressed her palms together and apologized at once:

“I’m sorry, really sorry. I thought you weren’t home, so I didn’t pay too much attention.”

Bae Do-yoon had only meant to remind them to keep it down; hearing this, he paused instead.

“How did you know I wasn’t home?”

The words out, he felt his own tone sounded a little like questioning a case; so he relaxed it and changed the phrasing.

“No — I mean, how were you so certain? I never told you I wouldn’t be back tonight.”

Park Ji-yeon visibly jammed; she glanced back at Boram without meaning to.

Boram had already lowered her head to drink water, her expression that of a person entirely uninvolved.

Bae Do-yoon watched the two of them react and could not help a laugh.

“What — do you need to align your testimonies first?”

Park Ji-yeon waved both hands at once.

“No no, not testimonies, really not. It’s just that this matter, well, speaking of it, is a little… complicated.”

Boram set the water glass down, tone calm. “Not complicated.”

Park Ji-yeon looked back at her.

“Unnie.”

Boram said: “We knocked on your door. No one answered, so she concluded you wouldn’t be back tonight.”

Park Ji-yeon hurried to supplement:

“It wasn’t my random conclusion! We really did knock — twice. The first time no one opened, and I thought maybe you were showering; then after a while we knocked again, and still no one opened.”

The more she spoke, the more right she felt, and her tone recovered a little.

“And your room had no sound the whole time, and the light wasn’t on either. That’s why I concluded you must have gone out.”

Reconstruction · how the neighboring intelligence service concluded “not home tonight”

02Interval observed; knock two: no answer. Shower hypothesis strained but retained

03Acoustic surveillance: no sound from within all evening. Visual surveillance: light off

05Field failure: subject returns past midnight, knocks twice, requests quiet. Review pending; chief analyst currently apologizing with palms together

Clarification on the record, offered rapidly: we were not spying on your schedule on purpose, and we were not studying when you come home. We only bought too much and meant to call you over to eat.

Bae Do-yoon only now understood, and nodded.

“Oh. So you didn’t see me go out — you came looking for me.”

Park Ji-yeon nodded rapidly.

“Yes yes yes, exactly that. We weren’t watching your movements on purpose, and we weren’t researching when you come home either.”

Saying this, she felt the words themselves a bit strange, and hurried to add one more:

“It’s only that today we bought a little too much. We meant to call you over to eat together, but you weren’t here, so we ate it ourselves.”

Boram said, lightly: “Mostly, she bought too much.”

Park Ji-yeon turned around.

“Unnie, this is not the moment to emphasize primary responsibility.”

Boram looked at her.

“But it was indeed you who bought it.”

“I was thinking of three people eating it!”

“Later it became two people eating it, and you didn’t eat less either.”

Park Ji-yeon was throttled.

Bae Do-yoon had been a little sleepy; their one-bout-and-another woke him somewhat.

He leaned against the doorframe, tone much lighter than before.

“So: you meant to call me to eat jokbal together; I wasn’t here; then you two ate too much and started playing a game to digest; and then you decided I wouldn’t be back tonight, so you felt safe turning on jump mode?”

Park Ji-yeon heard this chain summarized, and her expression grew embarrassed by degrees.

“Said that way, it feels like every step I took was absurd.”

Bae Do-yoon smiled. “Not every step.”

Park Ji-yeon’s eyes had just brightened a little.

Bae Do-yoon went on: “The first step — calling me to eat — was quite normal.”

Park Ji-yeon: …

Boram could not hold it; she lowered her head and laughed a little.

Park Ji-yeon saw it and was immediately displeased.

“Unnie, you’re laughing too.”

Boram slowly reined the laugh in.

“Sorry.” She paused, then said: “But what he says is quite right.”

Park Ji-yeon covered her face.

“I know, I know, I was wrong. It’s mainly that the game is too infuriating — it kept giving me B. I really only wanted to try once more.”

Bae Do-yoon glanced at the yoga mat in the room.

“Once more — tried until the neighbors heard it.”

Park Ji-yeon rebutted in a small voice: “It wasn’t as exaggerated as all that…”

Boram looked at her once.

Park Ji-yeon amended at once:

“All right. It was a little exaggerated.”

Bae Do-yoon did not pursue it further.

He had been to the martial arts hall by day and met Irene by night; his documents were not yet sorted, and he had only come to knock because the noise left him no choice. Knowing now that they had not meant it, he had no anger left.

He rubbed between his brows and said:

“All right — no more jumping tonight. I have work tomorrow morning.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at him guiltily.

“You still have to work?”

“Mm. Otherwise what?”

Bae Do-yoon sighed.

“I thought I’d come home to sort things quietly for a while. Turns out next door is livelier than my evidence inventory.”

Park Ji-yeon blinked.

“Evidence inventory?”

Bae Do-yoon waved a hand.

“Don’t ask. If you ask, it’s very boring.”

Boram then looked at him once. “Then — have you not eaten yet?”

Bae Do-yoon paused.

He had indeed eaten little in the evening; later, talking long with Irene, he had not gotten around to eating anything.

Park Ji-yeon, hearing this, seemed suddenly to remember something.

“Wait!”

She turned and ran back into the room.

Before Bae Do-yoon could ask, Park Ji-yeon had already run back hugging an unopened takeout box.

“This is for you.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down.

The box carried the shop’s label.

Korean-style jokbal, small portion, original flavor.

He raised his eyes to Park Ji-yeon.

“You two overate this and then started jumping, didn’t you?”

Park Ji-yeon nodded halfway, felt it not quite right, and hurried to explain:

“This isn’t leftovers. It’s new, unopened. We meant to share it with you from the start — you just weren’t here earlier.”

Boram added from the side: “Also something she ordered in excess.”

Park Ji-yeon turned to look at her.

“Unnie, are you truly not going to cover for me even one sentence today?”

Boram thought about it. “I’m afraid the more I cover, the more obvious it becomes. Bae Do-yoon-ssi is a clever person.”

Bae Do-yoon looked down at the box of jokbal, then once more at Park Ji-yeon.

Her face still carried the flush of exercise; her expression was somewhat sheepish, but her eyes were sincere.

He reached out and took it. “Then I’ll accept it.”

Park Ji-yeon visibly loosened with relief.

“Accepting means you’re not angry, right?”

Bae Do-yoon laughed a little.

“I wasn’t very angry to begin with. It’s only that for one instant just now, I truly suspected a construction crew had moved in next door.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed twice, embarrassed.

“It won’t happen again, I promise. No jump mode after midnight.”

Boram added, slowly:

“Preferably not after eleven at night, either.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at her.

“Unnie, why are you adding conditions too?”

Boram said: “For the harmony of neighborly relations.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at Bae Do-yoon. “Then… eleven?”

Bae Do-yoon thought about it. “Eleven-thirty, shall we say.”

Park Ji-yeon’s eyes went wide.

“Why are you adding some too?”

Bae Do-yoon, holding the jokbal, tone very natural:

“Because I live next door. I have speaking rights.”

Boram nodded.

“Mm. Extremely reasonable.”

Park Ji-yeon looked at the two of them, and at last gave up struggling.

“Fine. Eleven-thirty. No jumping after eleven-thirty — is that all right?”

Bae Do-yoon smiled.

“It’s all right.”

Park Ji-yeon said again, in a small voice:

“But next time, if we buy too much again — we can still knock on your door, right?”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

“You can. As long as you don’t notify me by dancing.”

Park Ji-yeon laughed at once.

“That definitely won’t happen. We’ll knock normally. Very normally.”

Bae Do-yoon could not help laughing once more.

The corridor treaty · jump-mode curfew and knock protocol

I. No jump mode after midnight (first offer, debtor’s own). Amended upward by the calm party to eleven; amended again by the adjacent holder of speaking rights to eleven-thirty. Ratified at eleven-thirty; further amendment barred tonight.

II. Knock protocol: knocking on the neighbor’s door remains lawful when too much food is bought; notification by dancing is expressly prohibited. The knocker pledges normal knocking — very normal.

III. Reparations: one sealed small box of Korean-style jokbal, original flavor, delivered at the doorway; acceptance deemed to extinguish the noise claim.

Witnessed in a pale corridor light: one guilty forehead, one calm water glass, one prosecutor holding pig’s trotters like a gavel.

Goodnights exchanged three ways. The calm party’s closing submission, overheard later: at the very least, it proves he was hungry.

The corridor light was very pale; the night was deep already. And yet these few exchanges of no consequence let the small irritation of being woken slowly scatter.

He stepped back one pace toward his own door.

“All right — you two rest early as well.”

Boram, seated on the sofa, said lightly as well:

“Good night.”

Bae Do-yoon went back into his own room and closed the door.

The documents still lay spread on the desk; the words on the sticky note reminded him that a pile of things waited before morning.

Boram held the cushion to her chest and straightened up.

Park Ji-yeon finally turned around, lowering her voice.

“Unnie, I just now truly discovered a secret. And it isn’t my wild imagining. Don’t look at me with that child-who-caused-trouble look first — hear me out to the end.”

The fried-chicken day · a declaration and its decline

Only days ago, over fried chicken, she had declared in all earnestness that she was a person who understands neighborly consideration very well. The declaration survived one meal.

Tonight it was impeached by three thuds, one twisted-ankle alarm that was a slipper, one jump mode, and a low table whose spread could not be reconciled with the phrase moved around a little. The declarant now apologizes with palms together and amends her exaggeration on one look from the calm one.

At the very least, it proves he was hungry.

On neighborly relations and speaking rights

A curfew negotiated at a doorway is not a curfew; it is three people learning each other’s hours, appetites and volumes, one amendment at a time — midnight offered, eleven countered, eleven-thirty ratified by a man holding pig’s trotters like a gavel because he lives next door and therefore has speaking rights. The calm one covers for no one and explains everything; the loud one confesses everything and is forgiven for one sealed box. And when the door closes, the loud one stands at the lock a moment too long, re-latches it very lightly, and consults the peephole — which is not neighborly consideration at all, but the first symptom of something the next chapter will have to name.
