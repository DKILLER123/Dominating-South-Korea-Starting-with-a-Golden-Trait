Chapter Thirty-Six

Where Am I, Bae Suzy, Inferior to Irene?

The same afternoon · JYP, the meeting room

One cleared patch of desk, one unlocked phone, and two pairs of eyes that have already decided to watch

The account, before the account

The plan was set, and none of the three wasted the moment. Step one began on the spot.

Farming the account. To be precise: taking the social platform account Bae Do-yoon already had, and growing it into a Bae Suzy fan account that was real enough, natural enough, to survive a second look.

The meeting table was cleared of its clutter quickly, one flat square of it given over to the work. Bae Do-yoon took out his own phone, unlocked it, and logged straight onto the platform.

Bae Suzy had been sitting across from him. Seeing that the real operation was about to start, she simply got up, crossed over, and pulled out the chair beside his, sitting down.

Kim Mi-ju took the position behind Bae Do-yoon, arms crossed, plainly in full spectator mode.

Two pairs of eyes settled on the phone screen. The curiosity was practically written on their faces.

Bae Do-yoon could see exactly what they were thinking, so he gave them the warning shot in advance.

“You don’t need to expect too much.”

Opening the profile page as he spoke, his tone stayed flat. “I’m a blank account. I’ve never posted anything.”

Bae Suzy had been carrying a thread of quiet expectation. The moment she heard that, she pursed her lips at once.

“Who was expecting.”

The denial came fast out of her mouth — and her tone still leaked, through the fastness, a faint, subtle disappointment. After all, people were only human; a little curiosity was natural. A man like Bae Do-yoon — what account he would use in private, whether there were traces on it that fit his image not at all: that was the kind of thing that made people want to take two extra looks.

Then, the next second.

When the page had actually loaded, that thread of disappointment on Bae Suzy’s face turned, all at once, into another, far brighter thing. She stared at the screen, her eyes widening a shade, her face full of not-believing.

The account was, as he had said, clean as could be.

Bae Do-yoon · the profile, as it loaded

The main page: blank like it had been registered yesterday

The posts — none. No photos. No retweets. No personal update of any kind.

The nickname — the only thing on the entire page that was not empty, hung in the most conspicuous spot, loud as a nameplate: The Peninsula’s First Heartthrob

But —!

A blank page is not the same as a page without impact. Because in the most conspicuous position of all, that nickname hung there — the one that could not be more eye-catching.

Bae Suzy stared at the characters, as if checking twice, and only then confirming she had not misread.

Then she finally stopped holding it in, entirely.

“Pfft—”

The laugh slipped out of her lips in one go. She turned her head almost by reflex, her shoulders shaking with it; this handle had clearly hit her harder than she had expected.

“What is this?”

Laughing as she looked at Bae Do-yoon again, glitter of mockery already in her eyes.

“The Peninsula’s First Heartthrob?”

Kim Mi-ju was a little more restrained than she — but not by much. She stood at the back, hand already up over her mouth, her eyes full of a smile she could not hold, her shoulders giving two small shakes of their own. If it had not been for the fact that Bae Do-yoon himself was still sitting there, she would have laughed out loud just as fast.

For his part, Bae Do-yoon’s expression stayed calm, as if the name were nothing worth noticing at all.

He looked down at his own page once, then lifted his eyes to Bae Suzy, who was close to laughing herself under the table, and kept his tone steady.

“What’s wrong?”

Seeing that perfectly straight face, Bae Suzy laughed even harder.

“You’re asking what’s wrong?”

She pointed at the nickname on the screen, her voice full of it.

“This name — it doesn’t fit you at all, does it?”

Kim Mi-ju, at last, added her line.

“Prosecutor Bae, this handle of yours… it certainly has impact.”

Bae Do-yoon was silent for two seconds, as if seriously weighing whether their reaction had any business being this large.

Then, with total calm, he explained: “Taken at random.”

“Taken at random, and it still came out like this.” Bae Suzy was still laughing. “The Peninsula’s First Heartthrob… in what state of mind did you pick this name?”

Bae Do-yoon’s brow did not move a single millimeter.

“Probably at registration the system flagged the nickname as duplicated too many times, and I was too lazy to think.”

The explanation did sound very much like his style. Casual, perfunctory, and without a single romantic component in it.

And precisely because of that, the nickname became even funnier.

Bae Suzy had finally pressed the last of the laughter down a little — and still could not stop herself from taking two more looks at the characters; the corner of her mouth would not stay down no matter what she did with it.

Kim Mi-ju’s self-control was clearly stronger than Bae Suzy’s. The first wave of laughter past, she reset her face quickly and put her attention back on the account. If this account was going to be rebuilt into a fan account real enough to pass, then every detail on the page needed checking.

The account audit · what a real fan account cannot skip

01The nickname

02The avatar

03The profile line

04The post history, such as it is

05The like record

06The follow list

None of them may be waved past — and it is the last one on the list that is about to turn the page.

Mid-thought, Kim Mi-ju’s gaze stopped, for a moment, on the screen.

“Prosecutor Bae.”

She leaned forward a little, toward one particular spot on the page.

“You’re following two people.”

Bae Do-yoon gave a short, “Mm.”

A little curiosity in her voice this time.

“Both of whom?”

Bae Suzy had been holding her laughter in; hearing this, she pulled it back at once and leaned in a step closer. The The Peninsula’s First Heartthrob had already given her one large shock. Now she was suddenly curious, too — a man who barely used social platforms at all: who on earth was in the follow list.

Bae Do-yoon did not hide anything. He opened the list directly.

The page jumped, and two accounts came up on the screen.

Bae Suzy’s gaze landed on them. First, a small stop. Then her face grew, very slightly, complicated. Because one of the two names was her own.

And the other was —

Irene.

The follow list · two names, no third

Name oneBae Suzy herself — followed, in his words, “just before I came today”; the line delivered plainly, without a thread of ambiguity in it

Name twoIrene — followed, his answer, “a very long time ago”; the reply fast, and clean

The explanation, when she tried to follow up“I am her fan.” — and the logic behind it, unimpeachable: the fandom is the whole reason for the follow; normal, reasonable, no problem at all

Bae Do-yoon had plainly noticed the shift in her gaze, and explained, quickly, before it grew.

“Don’t misunderstand — I followed you just before I came today.”

The line was given flatly, without a trace of romance in it.

And the flatter it was, the less Bae Suzy knew what face to make for a moment.

She shot Bae Do-yoon a look of odd color, then asked, faintly: “Then what about her?”

Eyes on the other name in the list, her tone made to sound like a passing question.

“Did you follow Irene today too?”

Bae Do-yoon shook his head.

“No.”

The answer was fast, and clean. “I’ve followed her a very long time.”

“…”

Bae Suzy’s look went a shade more complicated still. She opened her mouth, as if about to ask one more thing.

“Then…”

The question had barely begun when Bae Do-yoon took it over, calmly. “I am her fan.”

“…”

Bae Suzy went silent, all at once.

The answer was too direct. Every word she had been saving for later jammed, on the spot, in her throat.

And the reason behind it was, in fact, unimpeachable. Because you are a fan, you follow her. Normal. Reasonable. There was no problem with it at all.

Bae Suzy looked down at the name on the screen, her expression slowly turning complicated. Of course she knew Irene. Same industry, and the same surname — there was no way that name was a stranger to her. What she had not expected was that a man who looked like he had no connection to the entertainment world, to K-pop, to idol culture at all, would admit, so calmly, that he was a fan of a certain female idol.

And, even less expected — that that person was not her.

Bae Suzy lifted her eyes to Bae Do-yoon, her tone light, and light, and light.

“Didn’t expect there to be another side under Prosecutor Bae’s serious exterior.”

“I thought you wouldn’t like something so… off-limits as K-pop.”

Out of her mouth it stayed level, like a passing tease.

But inside those few characters there ran, faint, a twist that was not easy to catch.

Bae Do-yoon looked at her once.

“Music and the stage have no high and low.”

“To like is to like.”

Bae Suzy started, a beat.

What had been a casual line carrying a little feeling was taken back by him in this calm, serious way — and for a moment she could not find a single word to put on top of it.

Kim Mi-ju, from the side, had her face reset to work-calm, or nearly expressionless. But her eyes made one quiet sweep between Bae Suzy and Bae Do-yoon, and a thought popped up in her head that she could not stop.

Weird.

There was nothing in this meeting room. Nothing at all.

So why could she smell, faintly, a trace of jealousy?

The air in the room turned subtle, all at once. Nobody had said anything straight — and yet the air held a little more of something that could not be named.

Bae Do-yoon, for his part, appeared completely unaware. Or if he had noticed, he had clearly decided not to spend energy on anything unrelated to moving the case forward.

He looked down at the phone page, tapped the screen a few times, and drew the topic back onto the plan itself, naturally.

Step one · the farming, as laid down

The photo pipeline, the rhythm, and the first signal

The feedBae Suzy’s photos, sent to him — not many, but the types kept natural: daily life, events, endorsement promotions — sorted in batches and posted as the account’s early content

The rhythmnot too dense, and not a one-time homework dump; it should look like someone who has always been following, only a little more active lately

The first public movein two days, once the content is thick enough: a single smiling-face emoji, left under one of his posts

The gain of doing it this way: not too deliberate, but visible enough. The harasser, if he has been watching her updates, will, in all likelihood, not miss the signal.

“You don’t need to follow me right now.”

His tone was as usual — business, and business only.

“Next — send me some of your photos. Not too many; keep the types natural. Daily shots, event shots, endorsement-related promotional images. I’ll sort them in batches and post them, as the account’s early content.”

Bae Suzy said nothing. She just looked at him.

Bae Do-yoon went on.

“Posting can’t be too dense, and it can’t look like making up homework in one night. Control the rhythm. It should look like someone who has been following all along — just more active lately.”

“In two days, once the account has a little substance — leave a smiling-face emoji under one of my posts.”

He said it in fine detail. From the order of the posts, to the frequency of the interaction, to the timing of the first public exchange — almost all of it had been thought through in advance.

By all rights, this was the moment Bae Suzy should have listened to the analysis seriously, or at least followed his line and filled in details.

But her attention, at this moment, was not there at all.

Her damnable competitive spirit had surfaced again.

And it came in with force.

She was Bae Suzy. The Bae Suzy held up by countless people and called Nation’s First Love. In popularity, in national recognition, in looks — when had she ever been short of praise?

Let alone this man, in front of her. One of the rare types she found pleasant to look at, pleasant to look at longer, worth a second look.

The self-audit she will not file · run in the space of a few seconds

The titleheld up by countless people; the Nation’s First Love, by quoted label, never by fact

The recordpopularity, national recognition, looks — not one line on the audit without a full bar

The man in questionof the rare kind that is pleasant, and pleasant longer, and worth a second look

The fact on the tablehe has just, calmly, in front of her, named another woman as the object of his fandom

The audit finds no weakness anywhere — which is exactly the problem. A man with a perfect file has just handed the comparison to someone else.

And now this man had just, in front of her, said — calmly, flatly — that he was Irene’s fan.

The more Bae Suzy thought about it, the more something about it sat wrong.

How?

Is my charm less than Irene’s?

The moment the thought surfaced, she thought it was childish herself.

But the childish thoughts are exactly the ones that lodge, and will not come out.

It brought her back, suddenly, to those endless, pointless online rankings of old.

The ranking she remembers · the one with the gap

The ones the internet never runs out of: the female idol you most want to date, the most heart-fluttering first-love face, the most popular goddess board…

One of them, she remembers clearly. First place: Yoona — and a lead so wide the gap had its own name.

To that she had no objection; she lost, and lost with conviction. The face, the features, the bearing, the camera — every item on that girl stable to the point of absurdity. She admitted it: not her opponent.

But second place — second place, of all places — was not her either. It was Irene.

At the time: a net’s idle joke, glanced at once, and let pass. Rankings, after all, had never meant anything.

But the problem —!

Now the situation was different.

Bae Do-yoon was not a netizen. He was the living man standing in front of her — and one of the rare few she could bear to look at. And this person, in front of her, had named himself Irene’s fan.

That feeling, for no reason she could state, sat badly.

Bae Suzy’s head was down, her fingertips rubbing the rim of the paper cup; nothing obvious on her face. But the twist inside her had grown clearer and clearer.

There was nothing between them. Nothing, from the beginning to the end, but the needs of the case.

And yet she could not stop feeling…

How is this feeling so strange?

As if the “real deal” was sitting right here — and what filled Bae Do-yoon’s head, instead, was how to package himself into a loyal fan of another woman.

And that “another woman” happened, of all people, to be Irene.

The more Bae Suzy thought, the more her chest felt stuffed.

There was even an absurdity in it she could not put into words.

What is this?

The comparison made no sense, at all — and still her brain pushed out one outlandish line of its own…

What’s the difference from being cheated on right in front of me?

“Miss Bae?”

Bae Do-yoon had been talking for a while, and seeing no answer from across the table, he finally lifted his head to look at her.

“Are you listening?”

Bae Suzy snapped back, all at once.

“…Yes.”

She sat up straighter, quickly, her expression reset to normal, as if nothing had happened.

“Go on.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her for two seconds. He had probably noticed the lapse — but he did not press, and simply continued with the details that followed.

And Bae Suzy, on the surface, nodded along. Inside, only one thought grew, steadily, more solid.

No.

She has to find a way to turn this back.

At the very least — she will not let Bae Do-yoon take it for granted that she is less attractive than Irene.

Where am I, Bae Suzy, inferior to Irene?

On the nickname, and the smell

The book’s protagonist holds exactly one romantic artifact in his possession: a nickname the system flagged as duplicated, taken at random because he was too lazy to think. The room reads it as a declaration; he reads it as an administrative accident. Both readings are correct, and that is the whole joke of the page — the most conspicuous thing on a completely blank profile is the one thing that was never meant to be there, and the two women watching it load spend the next ten minutes on nothing else.

What the chapter keeps, though, is the smell. Kim Mi-ju smells it and says nothing — the clerk’s line has always been that: observe, file, do not name. Bae Do-yoon does not smell it at all, or has decided the case does not pay for it. And Bae Suzy, who is the smell, names it to no one, not even to herself — until the final line, where she addresses herself in the third person, because the question is too large to fit in the first. First place she has conceded; second place she has not. Between “a net’s idle joke” and “the living man standing in front of me” lies the whole chapter — and the rank that will not be conceded is the rank that will not let her sit still for the rest of it.
