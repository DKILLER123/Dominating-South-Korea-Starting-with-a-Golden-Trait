Chapter Thirty-Five

You Are That Lucky Fan

After the plan formed · his office

One notebook, a blank page, and a hand that writes plans the way it writes indictments

The lucky fan, before the face is found

Once the idea was there, Bae Do-yoon did not linger any further. He took the notebook on the desk, opened it to a blank page, and quickly wrote down the plan that had just taken shape. His writing was not slow, and his hand, as always, was crisp and calm — as if he were taking the thoughts in his head that were still scattered, and assembling them, bit by bit, into a scheme that was clear and executable.

The core of the plan was not complex. To put it bluntly: to manufacture a “lucky fan” — real enough, eye-catching enough — and provoke, out of the shadows, the harasser who had been hiding there all along.

The notebook · the plan, as written

But when it comes to real execution, the details matter more than the framework.

I. Male. The most critical point. From the surveillance information and the account’s behavior characteristics grasped so far, the person who has been harassing Bae Suzy for so long is, with high probability, also male. If so, then a male fan obtaining Bae Suzy’s attention will, clearly, be the stronger provocation. Jealousy, hostility, losing control — for a person who already carries a pathological possessiveness, a man who is suddenly “given a different look” by Bae Suzy hits the nerve far more easily than an ordinary female fan would.

II. Location: Seoul. The “lucky fan’s” range of activity must clearly point to Seoul — that is, the account’s various revealed details must let people naturally judge that this person lives in Seoul, at least is active in Seoul long-term. If the other party is out of town, even far from Bae Suzy’s life circle, then even if the harasser gets emotional over it, he may not act right away. But if the one she followed is right there in Seoul — in the same city, within what seems like reach — then the probability of action after the provocation rises, clearly. For an obsessive, distance itself is a trigger. The closer, the more he feels he can do something.

III. The account must be real enough to look like a truly existing devoted fan. Since the identity is set as Bae Suzy’s hardcore supporter, the account’s content naturally cannot be too casual. It must look real, natural, and stable over the long term — in other words, what this account has posted in the past must be, basically, all related to Bae Suzy: screenshots of her stages, impressions of TV drama clips, reposts of endorsement promotions, timely interaction after every one of her updates, and, mixed in, some “fan-perspective” daily records — collecting her posters, buying her endorsed products, checking in at the places where her events took place; even the tone of speech has to be like an ordinary fan who is truly obsessed with her. Only this way will the moment this “lucky fan” is noticed by Bae Suzy not look abrupt. And only this way will the harasser, seeing all of it, more easily believe that Bae Suzy has really given her attention to someone else — not by accident, and not for show.

Marginal notes, added after the pen tip paused: the account’s registration time must not be too new. The interaction traces must be complete enough. Follow list, like habits, posting frequency — all of it must fit the behavior logic of a normal fan. If necessary — even farm the account, in advance.

He leaned back in his chair, looked down at what he had just written, and his gaze swept slowly over those few lines. The prototype of the plan was already there. And the more he wrote downward, the more he felt this road was executable.

After that, Bae Do-yoon closed the notebook, raised a hand, and pressed the internal telephone. Before long, Kim Mi-ju came back in.

“Prosecutor Bae — were you looking for me?”

Bae Do-yoon pushed the notebook to the center of the desk and gestured for her to come look. Kim Mi-ju stepped closer, looked down, and skimmed the content above. At first, her expression was still calm — but the more she looked down, the more obvious the interest in her eyes became. After Bae Do-yoon had briefly and completely explained his idea to her, Kim Mi-ju’s eyes, too, lit up a little.

“This idea is pretty good.”

She raised her head, looked at Bae Do-yoon, her tone carrying a measure of agreement.

“By manufacturing a fan who gets Bae Suzy’s special attention, provoke that harasser’s jealous psychology — and make him proactively expose his action traces.”

She paused a beat, then asked: “But — will that harasser really take the hook?”

Bae Do-yoon’s expression was calm.

“Can’t guarantee it.”

He picked up the pen and tapped the notebook, lightly. “But we can try first. The loss, for us, isn’t big.”

Kim Mi-ju thought a moment, and nodded.

“Fair enough.”

If this method could not draw the other party out, at most it would waste a little time and a little energy. But if it were effective, that harasser who had been hiding behind the network the whole time would very likely be forced, out of the shadows, to walk. At least, it would be better than the current state of having no clue at all.

Bae Do-yoon closed the notebook and said, lightly: “Since you also think the plan is feasible — then let’s go find the principal.”

“After all, the decision is still in her hands.”

No matter how the plan was designed, in the end Bae Suzy’s cooperation was needed. Having her follow a specific account, even show enthusiasm — this was not a matter without risk for her. So Bae Do-yoon had no intention of deciding on his own.

Afterwards · JYP, the meeting room

A meeting room with three chairs for the length of the chapter; the approach took the quiet passage, the dress took civilian clothes

The plan on one side of the table, the decision on the other

The visit · logistics, as run

Everything before the door

Source: one advance phone contact, one car, and the building’s staff who had been told

The contactin advance, by phone; the reason given: case-related matters, face to face; the reply, quick

The locationas before — JYP

The approachthe relatively hidden passage, led by staff who had received notice; the dress — both changed into civilian clothes, to avoid drawing unnecessary attention

What the logistics protect is not the plan. It is the plan’s first condition: that as few people as possible know the plan exists.

The two of them briefly tidied up the materials, and on the phone had already contacted Bae Suzy in advance, explaining that there were some case-related matters needing face-to-face discussion. On Bae Suzy’s side there was a quick reply; the meeting location, as before, was at JYP. To avoid drawing unnecessary attention, both Bae Do-yoon and Kim Mi-ju changed into civilian clothes. After leaving the Prosecutors’ Office, the two of them drove straight to JYP. When they arrived at the building, the staff had already received advance notice, and led them up the floor through a relatively hidden passage. They had arranged to meet in the meeting room.

Bae Suzy came quickly. When she pushed the door open, her face still carried a faint smile, and her state looked a little more relaxed than the previous few times.

“Prosecutor Bae.”

She first greeted Bae Do-yoon, then looked at Kim Mi-ju and nodded lightly. After that, she sat down across from the two of them, her tone carrying a measure of anticipation.

“Why did you suddenly contact me? Is there a breakthrough in the case?”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Count it as a new direction.”

Bae Suzy’s expression immediately became serious. Bae Do-yoon did not beat around the bush; he directly explained, to her, the plan he had just organized.

The plan, as explained · three moves, no handout

Move one — the motive. What the harasser wants is not harm on paper. It is her noticing him; every account, every message, every uncomfortable appearance is one delivery of the same request, made in a voice the request is afraid to use.

Move two — the setup. A “lucky fan”: real enough to be believed, favored visibly — replied to, liked, followed on her own motion. The attention he has been demanding, given, openly, to someone else.

Move three — the provocation. The obsessive mind, watching that attention transfer, does not accept it. It moves. Approaches, attacks, and leaves traces a case can hold.

The objection anticipated before it is raised: this is not how the Prosecutors’ Office is imagined to work. The answer, given plainly: the method is not important — within the rules, the thing that finds the person in the dark is the valuable one.

The consent, not assumed: the decision is hers; the refusal clause is offered before the acceptance is asked.

He spoke clearly. Kim Mi-ju, from the side, occasionally added two sentences, making some of the execution-level details more concrete.

Bae Suzy, at first, listened very seriously. But by the time she heard the latter part, her expression had gradually become a little subtle. Bae Do-yoon noticed the change in her color, stopped talking, and asked: “What’s wrong?”

Bae Suzy blinked, and a moment later, she smiled.

“Nothing.”

She looked at Bae Do-yoon, her tone very sincere.

“This plan is actually pretty good — it’s just…”

Bae Do-yoon’s eyebrow rose a beat.

“Just what?”

Bae Suzy thought, and the smile on her face became a little more obvious.

“Ah — I don’t know how to describe it.”

She lightly pressed her lips, as if afraid her words would sound impolite, and slowed her tone a little.

“It’s just that I feel this plan is quite interesting.”

“Interesting?”

“Mm.”

Bae Suzy nodded. “Not quite the same as the Prosecutors’ Office case-handling style I imagined.”

She looked at Bae Do-yoon, her eyes carrying a little curiosity.

“I originally thought, a plan proposed by a prosecutor should be more… how to put it — more dignified, more formal.”

Having said this, she herself couldn’t help smiling a little.

“But this plan sounds, on the contrary, a little like fan operations.”

Kim Mi-ju, standing to the side, had the corner of her mouth rising a little.

Bae Do-yoon, on the other hand, was silent for two seconds. Of course he understood what Bae Suzy meant. Indeed. This plan was not, in the traditional sense, that kind of rigorous, solemn, step-by-step investigation plan. As a prosecutor, he represented the law and the dignity of the peninsula. And the current plan was to advance by borrowing social accounts, fan psychology, idol interaction — things that did not look all that “serious.” It did sound, indeed, unlike something the Prosecutors’ Office would do. But Bae Do-yoon did not particularly care about that.

He raised his eyes and looked at Bae Suzy, his tone still calm.

“The method isn’t important.”

“As long as it’s within the rules, as long as it can push the case forward, as long as it can find the person hiding in the dark — then it’s a valuable method.”

He paused a beat, then added: “Of course — if you don’t want to cooperate, we won’t force you either.”

Bae Suzy seemed to think seriously for a while. After that, she raised her head; the smile on her face had converged a little, but her expression had become resolute.

“No — I’m willing.”

She said lightly: “If this really can draw that person out, I’m willing to cooperate.”

Now that Bae Suzy had already agreed to this plan, the following advance naturally became much smoother. The most critical step had already been stepped out.

Bae Do-yoon looked down and opened the notebook, and on top of the original plan continued to fill in the execution details; Kim Mi-ju, standing to the side, followed his train of thought and thought through the subsequent arrangements. But after writing a few lines, Bae Do-yoon’s pen tip suddenly stopped.

“However — there’s one more problem.”

Bae Suzy was holding a paper cup of water and drinking; hearing this sentence, she raised her head, looking at him with some curiosity.

“What problem?”

Bae Do-yoon closed the notebook.

“The candidate for this ‘lucky fan’?”

This question was, indeed, very realistic, and very critical. Currently, the case of Bae Suzy being harassed was itself in a confidential state. Let alone the outside — even inside the company, not everyone knew. The fewer who knew this matter, the lower the leak risk; this was, originally, a necessary protective measure. And precisely because of that, the identity of this “lucky fan” absolutely could not be casually handed to anyone to play.

The candidate · the requirements, as the problem was put

01The case is confidential — the account, once in the plan, touches case details, interacts with Bae Suzy per the set scheme, and may become the harasser’s attack target

02The operator must be trustworthy enough to be trusted — the account touches what the case holds, and the plan cannot survive a second pair of eyes

03The operator must be calm enough to see the whole plan through — when the plan calls, and without a second script

The choice, delivered without owning it: “This candidate — you decide it yourself.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at Bae Suzy, and directly handed the choice over to her.

“This candidate — you decide it yourself.”

Bae Suzy nodded lightly, and did not answer right away. She lowered her head and thought seriously, as if sifting, one by one, through all the possible candidates around her, in her heart. But as she was thinking, her gaze, of its own accord, fell on Bae Do-yoon.

This young prosecutor in front of her was slightly lowering his head, his expression focused as he organized his notes. The lines of his profile were clean and crisp; his temperament, calm and restrained; the way he spoke and acted, all steady, without extra restlessness or showiness. From the first meeting, Bae Suzy had already felt that this person was not quite the same as the prosecutor she had imagined. Not just the work ability. Even the looks, the personality — even that if-present-if-absent sense of distance — all of it, very subtly, stepped right onto her aesthetic point.

She looked at him, and for a moment was actually entranced.

The read she will not file · assembled in the space of one glance

The profileclean, crisp lines, turned down; the notes in his hands steadier than the case

The temperamentcalm, restrained; speaking and acting all steady — no extra restlessness, no showiness

The distanceif present, if absent; sitting, precisely, on the aesthetic point

Since the first meeting: not quite the prosecutor she had imagined — and the correction has been running, quietly, in one direction only.

Kim Mi-ju, standing to the side, happened to take in this whole scene with her own eyes. She first stilled a little, and then, as if seeing what it was, a smile surfaced in her eyes, without her meaning to. Bae Suzy, originally still looking very candidly — but the next second, without her noticing, her eyes met Kim Mi-ju’s somewhat strange gaze. As if her thoughts had been poked through on the spot, she instantly froze, and immediately turned her head away. The tips of her ears also quietly flushed a little. She lightly pressed her lips, and used the motion of drinking water to slightly cover her own awkwardness; only when her emotions had mostly settled did she look at Bae Do-yoon, again.

“Prosecutor Bae.”

“I already have a candidate.”

Bae Do-yoon, hearing this, nodded, his expression not changing at all.

“Good — then contact him, and have him come over, and we’ll discuss the specific details again.”

Bae Suzy looked at him, and suddenly smiled, lightly.

“No need.”

“He’s already here.”

“Hm?”

Bae Do-yoon clearly paused a beat, and instinctively looked around the meeting room. But in this not-very-large meeting room, besides the three of them, there was no fourth person. He looked at Bae Suzy, again.

Bae Suzy met his gaze, the corner of her mouth slightly raised, and said, slowly and deliberately:

“Prosecutor Bae — stop looking.”

She extended a hand, and pointed at the direction in front of herself.

“He’s right in front of me.”

He’s right in front of me.

Bae Do-yoon first looked at her, then, as if finally reacting to something, his gaze paused a beat.

“Me?”

Bae Suzy nodded, a faint smile on her face, her tone natural as if stating the most reasonable fact in the world.

“Is there a more suitable candidate than you?”

As she spoke, she counted the conditions off for him, finger by finger: “Male, trustworthy, knows the case details, can cooperate with the plan’s advance at any time, and also doesn’t need to explain anything extra to outsiders.”

Having said this, she paused a little, and the smile in her eyes became more obvious.

“Most importantly — you are originally the proposer of this plan, and you know the whole plan best.”

The candidate, as she counted him · terms, finger by finger

I. Male.

II. Trustworthy — the case is confidential, and the account touches what the case holds.

III. Knows the case details — the account must be run per the scheme, and the scheme is known.

IV. Able to cooperate with the plan’s advance at any time — on schedule, on demand, without a second script.

V. No explanation owed to outsiders — the fewer who know, the lower the leak risk; the clause protects the case, not the secret.

Most importantly — the proposer, who knows the whole plan best. Counted, and left standing.

This remark was, actually, not exaggerated. From the objective conditions, Bae Do-yoon was indeed the most suitable candidate. And, almost — the only one.

Kim Mi-ju, from the side, had the curve of her mouth almost at the breaking point — but she was still tactful enough not to butt in, only quietly watching this scene.

Bae Do-yoon, on the contrary, did not show too much surprise. He was just silent for a few seconds, as if truly, seriously evaluating the feasibility of this matter. A moment later, he gave a light nod.

“Indeed.”

“I am pretty suitable.”

On the lucky fan

The plan asked for a face, and the room supplied one — which is how the chapter stands at its end, and the reason it closes on the man’s nod rather than on the woman’s smile. “Indeed. I am pretty suitable,” said, lightly, by a man who is not joking, in a room where two of the people are. The bait, in this book, has never been chosen for what it can do to the case. It is chosen for what the case can do to no one at all — and for what the room, quietly, can do to both of them. The lucky fan is the only role in the plan with no script: the plan knows what he must appear to be, and it does not know what he is, which is exactly why he is the right one for it. What the plan does not know, the two people in the room already do — and that gap, between the plan’s knowledge and the room’s knowledge, is where the next payment comes from.
