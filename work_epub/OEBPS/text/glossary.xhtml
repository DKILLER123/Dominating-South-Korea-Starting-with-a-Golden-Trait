Language & Address

Glossary

Korean address is not decoration. Who calls whom what, in which verb ending, is how this book says who holds the power in the room. Terms are romanized in Revised Romanization, given names hyphenated, and Hangul appears only in this page and in the glosses on the character cards.

· · ·

Extended through Chapter 5. A card is added the cycle a chapter first uses the term, and never before.

How to read a card

The term is what the translation prints. The gloss is what it literally means. The use line says who may say it to whom; the example line shows it inside a sentence the book would actually write.

Where the raw has a Chinese-character idiom or a Korean honorific with no English equivalent, the translation keeps the Korean word and lets the context carry it. Nothing is smoothed into “sir” or “ma’am” just because it is easier to set.

01Address suffixes

-nim님

Honorific suffix, the safe default. Strangers, superiors, clients, judges, anyone outranking you in the room.

“Prosecutor Bae-nim, the file is on your desk.”

-ssi씨

Neutral title for an equal or a subordinate. Office peers, witnesses, a suspect being handled politely.

“Ms. Jung, we are not recording tonight’s conversation.”

-yang양

Young or unmarried woman. Students, junior trainees, a girl in a police report.

“The witness is a Miss of the same year as my sister.”

-gun군

Young or unmarried man. Students, first-job kids, a boy who has done something stupid.

“Bae Do-yoon was a promising kid three years ago.”

02Seniority

eonni언니

Older sister, said by a woman. Idol rooms, sorority, a senior who has earned the softness.

“Eonni, put the phone down before the picture is taken.”

noona누나

Older sister, said by a man. Family, neighbours, and any man choosing to be disarmed in public.

“Noona, I am a prosecutor today, not your brother’s friend.”

oppa오빠

Older brother, said by a woman. Intimacy or performance, sometimes both in one sentence.

“Oppa, that is not a thing you say to a witness.”

hyung형

Older brother, said by a man. Prosecutors’ office brothers, loan-shark brothers, army brothers.

“Hyung, let me carry this one before it carries you.”

sunbae선배

Senior by year, at school or at work. The hierarchy that follows you from the lecture hall into the office.

“Sunbae-nim signed off on this case before I was born.”

hubae후배

Junior. Addressed by name and rank, rarely to the face with affection.

“Teach your hubae to knock before opening a drawer.”

03Office & rank

geomsa검사

Prosecutor. The book’s currency: the office, the badge, and the fear that comes with both.

“A geomsa who needs explaining has already lost the room.”

hoejang-nim회장님

Chairman. How a chaebol house is addressed when it is being feared.

“Hoejang-nim will hear about this from me, not from you.”

sa-jang-nim사장님

Company president. An agency head, a subsidiary chief, a man one rank below real power.

“Sa-jang-nim, the idol is not the problem. The contract is.”

bu-jang-nim부장님

Department head or senior manager. The rank that decides whether a report is read or buried.

“Bu-jang-nim wants the file closed by Friday, and by Friday he means today.”

daeri대리

Assistant manager, the junior office rank. Useful for showing who in a room is not yet anyone.

“He was a daeri when the house first noticed him.”

yeonseupsaeng연습생

Trainee. The years a company owns before it lets a person be photographed. Anyone who has signed one knows the tense: permanent, and revocable.

“He planned to walk into SM as a trainee straight out of high school, which is the sentence a man only says while he still believes doors open.”

yeong-eop영업

Working the room; the scheduled warmth of a promotion period. What a smile is called when it belongs to a roster. The serial’s idols are always either in it or coming out of it.

“One entry, a photograph with a stranger, five minutes of yeong-eop, and then the corridor goes cold again.”

04Family

appa아빠

Dad, warm form. Childhood memory and a household that has not yet learned money.

“Appa never asked me what I wanted to be.”

eomma엄마

Mom, warm form. Worry expressed as food, and anger expressed as silence.

“Eomma left the light on in the hallway, again.”

harabeoji할아버지

Grandfather. In this book, sometimes the only man in the family with a vote.

“Harabeoji says no, and the meeting is over.”

halmeoni할머니

Grandmother. The relative who feeds you and then tells the truth.

“Halmeoni told the neighbours I was studying law, and then winked.”

05Social register

jondaemal존댓말

Polite, elevated speech. The register a room puts on when it is negotiating or hiding.

“He switched to jondaemal, so the offer had become serious.”

banmal반말

Plain, informal speech. Intimacy granted, or status asserted by dropping it in public.

“Do you want to speak banmal with me, or do you want to keep your job?”

nunchi눈치

Reading the room. The skill this book’s men are rated on, silently, at every dinner.

“Zero nunchi, and he is still the most dangerous man at the table.”

chaebol재벌

A great family-run conglomerate. Not a company; a house with a company attached and a marriage policy.

“A chaebol does not hire a prosecutor. It collects one.”

controversial artist

A demotion nobody has to announce, and nobody has to sign. Said by the industry about a woman whose name has become inconvenient. No document exists; every booking sheet reflects it.

“SM has not said it out loud, but in this industry you have already been flagged a controversial artist.”

white gloves · black gloves

The hands a house uses for what can be photographed and for what cannot. A chaebol’s own vocabulary, spoken aloud in a chapter by a man listing what is arrayed against him.

“Commercial agents. White gloves, for what can be photographed. Black gloves, for what cannot.”

06Expressions

aigo아이고

Exhaustion, dismay, or delight at someone’s audacity. Older relatives and tired men in elevators.

“Aigo. You brought the press into the lobby.”

jeongmal정말

Really, truly. Doubt and wonder with the same two syllables.

“Jeongmal? You took the case yourself.”

geurae그래

Alright, then, so. Agreement between equals, in banmal.

“Geurae. If they want a wolf, be a wolf.”

mwo뭐

What. Also: nothing, whatever, an insult. Flat, unimpressed, and frequently the beginning of a fight.

“Mwo. Sit down before someone photographs you.”

the entry

A line of annotation, floating in the depth of the mind. Not a voice, not a menu, not a friend. It grants a talent and prints a cost beneath it, in the same paragraph.

“No chime, no flash. One purple sentence, floating there as though it had always been in the room.”

golden finger

The serial’s own slang for a cheat carried out of a previous life. Used by the narrator, dryly, when a man with an unfair advantage is still losing.

“Even with both the halo of a rebirth and a golden finger handed to him at birth, a man can still be standing at the bottom of a hole.”

air force

Forum slang for a man with no romantic history at all. A joke the book makes once, about the previous life, and then pays for seriously.

“Not the service with jets and a flag — the other one. The word for a man no woman had ever been in the way of.”

reverse scale

The scale on a dragon’s throat that must not be touched, or touched and survived. What a sister is called when she is the only lever that works on a woman who has no weaknesses left.

“Her sister is the one place on her that no one is allowed to touch.”

-ah / -ya

Vocative suffixes welded onto a given name when calling a friend, a junior, or an intimate: -ah after a closing consonant, -ya after a vowel. Kept in place where the raw speaks it; this book lets it surface once, in a sister mimicking a manager’s mouth.

“Soo-jung-ah, the company has been considering your direction for a while.”

sasaeng

Obsessive fans who chase non-public schedules, obtained legally or otherwise. The network a schedule-leak chain can reach into, named once where the leak question is opened.

“A schedule leak can reach into agency staff, outsourced security, event organizers — even the sasaeng networks that have followed entertainers for years.”

ganjang gejang

Raw crab marinated in soy sauce; a late-night dish worth changing a departure over. Kept in Korean where the crabs themselves are the point; glossed as soy-sauce marinated crab elsewhere.

“The lid opens and its particular sweet-savory smell crosses the whole room at once.”

Nation’s First Love

The national nickname the media attached to Bae Suzy. Used in this book only as a quoted label the country uses — never as a fact about anybody’s heart.

“…crowned with the nickname the Nation’s First Love; hot in the way only a few names in the country are hot.”

hanja

Chinese characters, as used on Korean signboards, names and calligraphy. Kept as the word hanja in prose wherever a sign is the point; the sign itself is translated on the spot.

“On the second-floor signboard, several very eye-catching hanja characters stood written: Inmudang Chinese Martial Arts Hall.”

wing chun

A southern Chinese fist system built on close-range economy and borrowed force. First on a gym’s listing line, then in a master’s four-beat throw that reads like arithmetic.

“wing chun, qinna joint-locks, sanshou, self-defense, children’s martial arts, adult practical combat class.”

Red Velvet

The SM group Irene leads. Named in prose only where the leader’s other life is the point; the friend in this book predates the name.

“When he first met her, she was not yet Irene, and not yet the leader of Red Velvet.”

street casting

The industry practice of scouts approaching strangers in public to offer auditions. What happens to a sweating prosecutor over an iced americano, to the café’s great excitement.

“I’m with SM Entertainment’s New Development Team. My main work is discovering new talent.”

visual · face of the group

The member a group fronts as its beauty standard; the face on the poster, the first name in a headline. Deployed in a basement rest room as the perfect un-refusable introduction candidate — and refused anyway, three times, in a whisper.

“She is SM Entertainment’s face. Who doesn’t like her?”

slow-burn type

A person who warms to feeling slowly; in this book, a self-declaration made very solemnly at a first meeting. Usually a line girls keep for refusing blind dates; inexplicably reasonable on one prosecutor, who can say it’s cold outside, go back in after an evening like that — and then close the last few centimeters at spiderweb-removal speed.

“Slow-burn type. No. Super slow-burn type.”

CCET · Pink Encounter

A woman’s perfume, the luxury among perfumes, much worn by artists; fruit at the front, soft floral and musk behind, not sweet-cloying, very clean. Identified through a doorway, through jokbal and garlic and sesame oil, by one neighbor with a cushion; co-analyzed by a second who swore she would not.

“CCET. Pink Encounter. The luxury among perfumes. Many artists use it.”

jokbal

Korean-style braised pig’s trotters, ordered in portions that assume three people and fed to two. The headliner of the neighbors’ low table; the cause of jump mode; and, sealed and unopened, the reparations that proved a prosecutor was hungry rather than angry.

“At the very least, it proves he was hungry.”

tteokbokki

Spicy stir-fried rice cakes; the supporting cast of any late-night delivery spread that stops being supporting. Found on a low table shoved aside, with buckwheat mixed noodles and rice balls, impeaching the phrase moved around a little.

“Rice balls · tteokbokki — the supporting cast that stopped being supporting somewhere around the second round.”

paparazzi · the tabloids

The photographers and papers that wait at alley mouths for idols; the authors of headlines nobody confirmed. The reason for hats, masks, safe distances and one black business van; also the credited authors of tonight’s recited headline, whose imagination was ruled not bad.

“Who made me a popular artist right now? I have to watch out for the tabloids at all times.”

seon선

A matchmaking introduction: the formal meeting arranged by families or go-betweens for people presumed ready to marry. The fate the office quietly tries to arrange for its single prosecutors; Officer Choi’s inquiry, routed through Kim Mi-ju, was one knocking at the door.

“At least this way, when I turn away matchmaking introductions for you in future, I’ll have a little more confidence in my heart.”

standard answers모범 답변

The safe, prepared replies every industry keeps ranked by deployment: I’m fine; I’ll work hard to adjust, so please don’t worry; and a third no one admits to using. Recited on demand under company observation; retired by order of one closed door, where the real state was admitted instead.

“I’ll work hard to adjust, so please don’t worry.”

stage control · kongchang

The skill of holding a room’s temperature: who speaks, who calms, who is shielded; the artist’s name for the prosecutor’s professional capability of not saying the dangerous thing. Graded in both directions across one cleared meeting room — her timing accurate, his information-content verdict: on the low side.

“Artists need that capability too. Only, we call it controlling the room.”

dongsaeng동생

Younger sibling; as an address from an older speaker, a claim of family-ish closeness that may or may not be sincere. Lee Boo-jin’s framing for Bae Do-yoon (I’ve always seen you as a younger brother), priced by him on sight: every declaration of this kind comes with a fee; also the word for the sister Jung Soo-yeon will not gamble with.

“You’d better not really treat me as a younger brother. Your way of raising younger brothers — ordinary people can’t bear it.”

sunbae선배

Senior: anyone who entered an organization before you and therefore outranks you in experience, procedure and precedence. The prosecutors a newcomer follows while running procedure; also the variable in Lee Boo-jin’s rearrangement model (who follows which sunbae is never random).

“Just entered — odd jobs, organizing materials, following sunbaes running procedure, writing things nobody wants to write.”

little-puppy type

Dating-type slang: a young man kept close, devoted and adoring, who is never expected to push back. Jessica’s first reading of what Bae Do-yoon might be in Lee Boo-jin’s design — ruled out on sight: a role that light would not cost Lee that much effort, and the composure does not fit the type.

“First, the little-puppy option could be ruled out directly.”

account farming

Raising a social account before it is put to work: registration age, interaction history, follow list, posting rhythm — pre-built so the account reads, the moment it enters play, as long-established and real. Third condition of the lucky-fan plan, written as a marginal note on the notebook page: if necessary — even farm the account, in advance.

“The account’s registration time must not be too new … if necessary — even farm the account, in advance.”

peninsula’s first heartthrob

Bae Do-yoon’s social account nickname — the only thing on a completely blank profile that is not empty. The room reads it as a declaration; he reads it as an administrative accident. His stated origin: the system flagged the nickname as duplicated too many times, and he was too lazy to think.

“Probably at registration the system flagged the nickname as duplicated too many times, and I was too lazy to think.”

Glossary updated through Chapter 38 — fifty-seven cards, each one earned by a line of prose that used the term.

House romanization: Revised Romanization, given names hyphenated, honorific suffixes joined with a hyphen where the raw writes them as one word with a name. Hangul in this book is confined to this page and to card glosses; chapter prose stays English with Korean address words left in place.
