Chapter Twenty-Six

Prosecutor Bae Is in a Good Mood

The next morning · the Prosecutors’ Office

Corridor already busy: printers, phone rings, footsteps, low talk

Two soft knocks on an office door, and a clerk carrying files and a diagnosis

Early the next morning, Bae Do-yoon returned to the Prosecutors’ Office.

The corridor already held its busy sounds: printers working, a phone ringing now and then, footsteps and low conversation woven between.

He draped his coat over the chair back, and had not long sat down when the office door was knocked, lightly, twice.

“Prosecutor Bae.”

Bae Do-yoon raised his head.

At the door stood his assigned clerk, Kim Mi-ju.

She held a stack of just-sorted documents, dressed sharply, her hair tied back neat at the nape.

Bae Do-yoon nodded once.

“Morning.”

Kim Mi-ju came in, set the documents on his desk, glanced at him in passing — and suddenly smiled.

“Prosecutor Bae, you look like you’re in a good mood today?”

Bae Do-yoon stilled a moment, and raised a hand to touch his own face without meaning to. “Uh… do I?”

Kim Mi-ju nodded, her face full of certainty. “You do.”

Bae Do-yoon was a little taken aback. “Is it showing that much?”

“Not especially much, either.” Kim Mi-ju drew out the chair opposite him and sat, her tone carrying a thread of tease. “Your expression is about the same as always — that same very reliable, very hard-to-get-close-to face.”

Bae Do-yoon heard the first half with nothing in him; hearing the second half, his brow-tip moved, lightly.

“Mm? I’m hard to get close to?”

Kim Mi-ju, without a change of expression: “Yes. That’s the feeling after these few days of working together. Although, well — cold outside, warm in…”

Bae Do-yoon let out a laugh.

“So from where exactly do you see that I’m in a good mood?”

Kim Mi-ju lifted a finger and pointed at him, as if presenting evidence in earnest.

The good-mood evidence · as presented, finger raised, in earnest

01On entering: steps a touch lighter than usual

02On sitting: no immediate frown-and-open-the-files; instead, two seconds of staring at nothing

03On being greeted: the look lifted at that moment had light in it

04Decisive exhibit: today, he said morning first

Method note: the examiner studied psychology at university; theory largely forgotten among documents and cases, but reading a person’s mood — some of it remained. The examined party’s rebuttal: on the basis of these, you can tell? Ruling: of course.

“First, when you came in, your steps were a little lighter than usual,” she said. “After sitting, you didn’t frown and go straight for the files — you stared into space for two seconds first. And just now, when I came in and greeted you, that look you lifted — there was light in it.”

She paused, then added one more stroke of the knife.

“Most importantly — today, you actually said ‘morning’ first.”

Bae Do-yoon: …

Kim Mi-ju, seeing him not speak, smiled deeper.

“Prosecutor Bae, although on the surface you’re about the same as always, some small expressions and little movements still show. You, today, are quite happy.”

Bae Do-yoon blinked, still not quite believing it.

“On the basis of these, you can tell?”

“Of course.”

Kim Mi-ju’s tone was very natural.

“I studied psychology at university. These days I face documents and cases all day, and I’ve forgotten no little of the theory — but for reading a person’s mood, a little of it remained.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“So that’s how it is.”

Having said it, he leaned back against the chair, and suddenly fell a little into abstraction.

Was he happy?

When the question surfaced, last night’s scenes flashed through his mind, quite naturally.

Three scenes from last night, crowding the office morning

First: hiding from Irene’s manager with Irene, pressed close together behind a row of vending machines, her two fingers on his sleeve for safety.

Then: Boram on the sofa, unhurriedly taking Park Ji-yeon’s own stage apart plank by plank, water glass in hand, entirely uninvolved. And last: Park Ji-yeon at the door with the box of jokbal, face perfectly serious, making him take it.

Three scenes jumbled together in no order — and yet far more vivid than the stack of files on the desk. The corner of his mouth bent, a little, without his permission.

First, hiding from the manager with Irene, pressed tight beside her.

Then Boram on the sofa, unhurriedly dismantling Park Ji-yeon’s positions one by one; and finally Park Ji-yeon standing at the door hugging that box of jokbal, face utterly serious, making him accept it.

Three scenes crowded together in a jumble — and yet far more vivid than the pile of files on his desk.

Thinking thus, the corner of Bae Do-yoon’s mouth bent, a little, without his noticing.

Kim Mi-ju saw that small expression.

She lifted a brow; her expression turned instantly, profoundly meaningful.

“Ah.”

Bae Do-yoon came back to himself; seeing that look of hers — as if she had suddenly understood something — a distinctly bad premonition rose in him.

“…What is that reaction?”

Kim Mi-ju laid her hands folded on her lap, sitting perfectly properly, her tone not at all proper.

“Nothing. I only suddenly felt that psychology is, occasionally, still very useful.”

Bae Do-yoon looked at her. “Speak in human words.”

Kim Mi-ju held her laugh in.

“I mean — Prosecutor Bae’s present state does not look like simple good mood.”

“It looks more like,” she paused, deliberately, “it has to do with a woman.”

Bae Do-yoon very nearly choked on his own saliva.

“What?”

Kim Mi-ju, perfectly straight-faced:

“Ordinarily, when a man whose mood rarely fluctuates suddenly comes to work in a good mood, and even shows this kind of smile when remembering — the high probability is not work relations, nor other life reasons.”

Bae Do-yoon was silent for two seconds.

“Do all of you who studied psychology conclude things this readily?”

Kim Mi-ju corrected him.

“Not concluding. Reasonable inference.”

Bae Do-yoon raised a hand and pressed his temple, suddenly regretting that moment of staring into space.

And Kim Mi-ju, precisely, did not intend to let him off there.

“But I am a little curious,” she said, looking at Bae Do-yoon, her eyes full of spectator’s delight. “Which one is it?”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his eyes. “Which one?”

“The one who makes you sit here first thing in the morning and smile to yourself.”

Bae Do-yoon opened his mouth — and found, remarkably, that he could not answer at once.

He had meant to brush it off with a casual you’re overthinking it; but at his lips, his mind inexplicably threw up three women’s faces again.

Irene. Boram. Park Ji-yeon.

…

He himself stilled a moment.

Why three at once?

Kim Mi-ju had only guessed on impulse; but seeing that instant’s subtle pause in Bae Do-yoon, her very eyes changed.

“No way.”

Bae Do-yoon came back to himself at once.

“Don’t think wildly.”

“I haven’t said anything yet,” Kim Mi-ju said, eyes narrowed. “Prosecutor Bae, this reaction of yours is even more suspicious than the last one.”

Bae Do-yoon gave a light cough, trying to drag the topic back.

“Are the documents delivered?”

Kim Mi-ju looked at him, her smile very restrained, and plainly unbelieving.

“Delivered. Left side is what you should read today; right side is what the bu-jang-nim’s side just passed down. The topmost one — remember to give it to me before ten.”

Bae Do-yoon nodded.

“Understood.”

Kim Mi-ju did not rise at once, however; she looked at him two more times instead.

“But seriously, Prosecutor Bae.”

“Mm?”

“You, occasionally looking like a normal young man — it’s actually rather good.”

Bae Do-yoon stilled, and lifted his eyes to her.

Kim Mi-ju shrugged.

“Usually you wear that I-can-still-read-thirty-more-files face so constantly, people will assume you have no private life at all.”

Bae Do-yoon was a little helpless.

“I don’t not have one.”

“Then good,” Kim Mi-ju said, standing and gathering the emptied folder; and at the door she added one more line: “At least this way, when I turn away matchmaking introductions for you in future, I’ll have a little more confidence in my heart.”

Bae Do-yoon failed to react for a moment.

“What matchmaking introductions?”

Kim Mi-ju had already reached the door; hearing him, she turned her head back.

“Didn’t you know? Last week Officer Choi from the next section asked me whether Prosecutor Bae is single.”

Bae Do-yoon: …

Kim Mi-ju appreciated his rare frozen expression for a moment, and smiled, satisfied.

“But looking at things now, I shouldn’t need to worry about it.”

“Wait,” Bae Do-yoon called her. “You haven’t already said something on my behalf, have you?”

“Rest assured. I am very professional.”

“That sentence makes me less reassured, not more.”

Kim Mi-ju stood at the door, holding her laugh in with visible difficulty.

“I only said: Prosecutor Bae’s work is very busy; he is not suitable for having these arranged casually.”

Only then did Bae Do-yoon relax.

“That’s acceptable.”

Kim Mi-ju nodded.

“But if someone asks again next time, I may have to reconsider my wording.”

Bae Do-yoon lifted his head to look at her, his eyes carrying a helpless warning.

“Officer Kim.”

“Yes.”

“Work properly.”

“Yes, Prosecutor Bae.”

She answered very crisply; and when she turned and went out, the corners of her mouth were still carrying a smile.

The office door closed again; the surroundings went quiet for a while.

Bae Do-yoon looked down at the documents on his desk, pen turning in his hand, a long while without opening the first page.

After a few seconds he could not help one low laugh, even at himself.

His good mood had to do with women.

One: an old acquaintance of many years, impossible not to care about — Irene.

One: always able to make a scene more delicate in the calmest possible tone — Boram.

And one: who, having plainly caused the trouble, could still hug a box of jokbal and apologize in earnest — Park Ji-yeon.

Thinking thus, Bae Do-yoon finally realized a more troublesome problem.

His recent life had, indeed, become far livelier than before.

Before, he had always been alone, utterly alone…

And now this liveliness actually put him in a good mood.

Bae Do-yoon stared at the files on the desk for two seconds, and in the end opened the first page, resigned.

“Forget it.”

He muttered it to himself, low.

“Work first.”

Nearly ten · the desk corner

One phone, one soft vibration, one screen lighting up

Where a good morning ends and an old case returns

Near ten o’clock.

Bae Do-yoon lowered his head and made his mark at the last place in the document, and had just meant to close it when the phone at the desk’s corner vibrated, lightly, once.

The screen lit.

He had only meant to glance at it at random; but seeing the sender’s name, his motion stopped.

Bae Suzy

The screen went dark; the office sounds came back, clear again. The files did not.

The message was from Bae Suzy — one line, no preamble.

Close behind it, several screenshots arrived.

Bae Do-yoon took up the phone and opened them.

The screenshots came from Bae Suzy’s social platform.

The other side was a new account; avatar and name both ordinary, like a blank sub-account registered in passing.

There were many messages.

At first only the usual tone of an ordinary fan; but the further on, the more uncomfortable the content became.

Bae Do-yoon scrolled down, his gaze stopping on a few of the lines.

In the last screenshot, the other side had sent only one sentence.

Bae Do-yoon looked at that sentence for several seconds.

The calm he had been keeping for the work faded quickly from his face; he typed:

After the message sent, Bae Suzy’s side soon showed typing.

After a while she replied: all right, sorry to trouble you.

Having sent it, Bae Do-yoon put the phone back on the desk.

The screen went dark; the office sounds grew clear again.

But he could no longer go on reading files with that earlier air of nothing-wrong.

Just then, the office door was knocked.

Kim Mi-ju pushed the door and came in.

“Prosecutor Bae, the bu-jang-nim’s side says that material from just now…”

Halfway through, seeing Bae Do-yoon’s expression, her voice lowered, naturally.

“What is it?”

Bae Do-yoon set the phone aside and reached into the cabinet for a file.

“The JYP case.”

Kim Mi-ju reacted at once. “Bae Suzy?”

“Mm,” Bae Do-yoon said, opening the file. “The harasser has appeared again.”

Kim Mi-ju looked once at his face, and did not joke as she had that morning.

“Is it serious?”

Bae Do-yoon did not hand her the screenshots to read one by one; Kim Mi-ju’s expression grew heavy all the same.

“We go to JYP this afternoon.”

Kim Mi-ju nodded. “Mm.”

Bae Do-yoon took up his pen and wrote a few simple items on a sticky note.

At three o’clock in the afternoon exactly, Bae Do-yoon and Kim Mi-ju arrived at JYP.

They did not enter by the main door; a person arranged by JYP’s legal team led them from the underground parking lot straight up to the fifth floor.

When the door opened, the people inside looked over at once.

JYP’s head of legal sat nearest the door; Bae Suzy’s manager sat to one side; on the table lay several bundles of material prepared in advance.

Bae Suzy sat by the window.

She wore a hat today, and a light-colored coat; at her hand stood a cup of iced americano, its ice already half melted.

Meeting-room assembly · the idol as witness, not as poster

Hat on today, brim neither staged nor styled; a working hat

Coat light-colored, unremarkable on purpose; the white coat of yesterday’s photographs expressly not worn

Coffee iced americano at the hand, ice half melted — the meeting’s true clock

Manner rises on the prosecutor’s entry, nods, tone perfectly normal: Prosecutor Bae

Assembly unchanged in lineage from the earlier JYP meeting room; the calm, this time, is her own and not the company’s.

Seeing Bae Do-yoon come in, she stood and nodded to him.

“Prosecutor Bae.” Her tone perfectly normal.

Bae Do-yoon returned the nod.

“Ms. Bae.”

He sat opposite her; Kim Mi-ju sat to one side and opened her notebook.

The head of legal pushed the materials over.

“Prosecutor Bae, these are this morning’s screenshots, together with the related content from the previous several occasions. We have sorted it all.”

Bae Do-yoon opened the materials.

The discussion that followed lasted about twenty minutes.

There was nothing complicated in it, in truth.

Bae Suzy answered very cooperatively.

She did not perform excessive fear; at times her tone even carried a little helplessness.

Asked what her reaction had been on seeing those words this morning, she thought about it, and said:

“My first reaction was — here we go again.”

The manager sat beside her, his face heavier than hers.

Bae Suzy glanced at him and added one more line:

“I’m not saying it isn’t serious. I’m only saying it’s really a bit annoying.”

The manager looked as if to speak and stopped.

The head of legal gave a light cough, as if to draw the atmosphere back toward severity.

Bae Do-yoon said nothing; he only lowered his head and made one note.

The whole questioning process ended quickly.

The head of legal was just about to ask about the follow-up arrangements when Bae Do-yoon closed the materials and lifted his head to look at Bae Suzy.

“I have roughly understood the situation.”

The head of legal straightened at once. “Then how do we need to cooperate from here?”

Bae Do-yoon did not answer immediately; he looked, instead, at the people in the meeting room.

Then he said: “I would like a few words with Ms. Bae alone.”

“That…” The manager looked at Bae Suzy without thinking.

Bae Suzy showed no particular reaction; she only lifted a brow, as if a little surprised.

The head of legal hesitated. “Alone?”

“Mm,” Bae Do-yoon said, tone level. “Not long. What needed confirming has been confirmed just now; a few sentences remain, and they do not need so many people present.”

The manager was plainly not at ease.

“Suzy…”

Bae Suzy glanced at him. “It’s fine. Prosecutor Bae isn’t a suspicious person, is he?”

The head of legal looked at Bae Do-yoon, then at Bae Suzy, and finally nodded.

“Then we’ll step out first.”

The manager still left one instruction behind: “Call if anything happens.”

Bae Suzy waved a hand.

“Understood. You’ll be right outside the door — if I call, you’ll certainly hear.”

The manager looked at her helplessly, and only then rose and went out.

Kim Mi-ju, standing beside them, heard Bae Do-yoon say:

“Officer Kim — you step out first as well.”

Kim Mi-ju’s motion paused; she looked at Bae Do-yoon, a little surprise in her eyes.

Bae Do-yoon did not explain.

Kim Mi-ju understood quickly, and nodded.

“All right. I’ll wait outside.”

She took up her notebook; passing Bae Suzy, Bae Suzy even smiled at her, a little sheepishly.

Kim Mi-ju returned a very light smile, and closed the door for them.

Remaining on the table: two people, one half-melted iced americano, and the version of the evening that is not the press version.

It has to do with a woman.

On readable faces and unread messages

A clerk who studied psychology once can read a good mood off two seconds of staring and one unsolicited morning; the same face that leaked a corner-of-mouth smile at three remembered women goes perfectly blank an hour later over four screenshots, because some messages cannot be smiled at. The office morning and the fifth-floor afternoon are the same man: the one who was told his good mood has to do with a woman, and the one who now sits across from a woman whose stalker knows which side of the street she looked toward. He asked everyone to leave the room not to be kind, but because press versions have no evidence in them — and the truth, he has learned this week, only walks in when the door is closed and nobody is performing.
